## 3. Findings by slice

### 3.1 Ancient Civilizations — 11 records — HOLD

**Positive evidence**
- OpenStax, *World History Volume 1*, §3.1 directly describes early civilizations as settled populations supported by agriculture; food surpluses enabled specialization; exchange and accumulated wealth contributed to social stratification. Its discussion is explicit that developments varied across regions and that older mobile lifeways persisted. The relevant text is in the page's opening discussion and “Attributes of Early Civilizations” section: https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/3-1-early-civilizations
- Smithsonian Profiles identifies Melinda A. Zeder's 2011 review, “The Origins of Agriculture in the Near East,” and its abstract emphasizes multiple regional domestication trajectories, long periods of management, and considerable regional variation: https://profiles.si.edu/display/sro_109973 ; DOI https://doi.org/10.1086/659307

**Claim-level assessment**
- Claim A (settlement, labour specialization, social stratification): materially supported by OpenStax §3.1. The phrasing “among other things” is appropriately non-exhaustive.
- Claim B (agriculture, settlement and demography vary by time/place): supported in broad terms by OpenStax and the regional variation described by Zeder. Smithsonian Evidence Use is linked to Claim B and accurately narrows the interpretation.
- Claim C (archaeological evidence and written materials where preserved): plausible but the current OpenStax source needs a precise pinpoint for the archaeological/written-evidence distinction. The source page also notes that prehistoric peoples left no writings and that artifacts must be interpreted cautiously; this nuance should not be flattened into a universal claim.

**Blockers**
- The three standard Evidence Use records cite a broad chapter URI and generic material (“source used within declared material”), without section/page/paragraph locator or a proposition-specific explanation.
- The additional Smithsonian Evidence Use has a more useful explanation, but still lacks a pinpoint locator and stable page/DOI locator details in the canonical Source/Evidence Use records.
- OpenStax chapter attribution states CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Non-commercial / share-alike conditions must be assessed against the eventual project distribution and licensing model; no compatibility or permission is assumed.
- Smithsonian Profiles is a metadata/abstract record for a journal article. The visible abstract is not equivalent to clearance to reproduce the full article; rights and permitted reuse remain unresolved.

**Disposition:** retain all 11 records on HOLD. Evidence is promising for A and B; complete exact locators and rights review before any inclusion consideration.

### 3.2 Anthropology — 9 records — HOLD

The AAA page “What is Anthropology?” describes anthropology as a broad study of human experience and identifies human biology, archaeology, cultural anthropology and linguistics as major disciplinary pillars in the US professional context: https://americananthro.org/practice-teach/what-is-anthropology/

- Claim A (“the slice introduces basic concepts and relations”) is an editorial description of the slice, not a substantive anthropological proposition.
- Claims B and C are generic epistemic/professional caveats, not anthropology-specific knowledge claims.
- Evidence Use A/B/C all point to the same broad AAA page and use generic support language; no pinpoint or specific proposition mapping is supplied.
- The stored Source URI uses an older `/learn-teach/` path, whereas the currently indexed AAA page is under `/practice-teach/`. Revalidate and normalize the canonical locator only under an explicit bounded correction.
- The AAA material can support a substantive introductory claim about the scope and subfields of anthropology, but the present Claims do not actually state that content.

**Disposition:** retain all 9 records on HOLD. Replace placeholder Claims with actual, carefully scoped anthropological propositions and map Evidence Use to exact sections before consideration. No replacement wording is authorized by this screen.

### 3.3 Linguistics — 9 records — HOLD

Independent authoritative corroboration is available from UCLA's linguistics department, which defines linguistics as the scientific study of language and describes phonetics, phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics and cross-cutting subfields: https://linguistics.ucla.edu/undergraduate/what-is-linguistics/

- The UCLA material confirms that a useful substantive introductory slice can be grounded in well-defined disciplinary content; it does not retroactively validate the current generic Claims or authorize changing the canonical Source.

**Disposition:** retain all 9 records on HOLD. Rebuild the content around explicit linguistics concepts and proposition-specific Evidence Use; preserve the existing record versions until bounded owner authorization exists.

### 3.5 Ethics — 9 records — HOLD

The current Source points to the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry “The Definition of Morality”: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/morality-definition/

- The SEP entry explicitly distinguishes descriptive and normative senses of morality and cautions that a single definition may not cover all moral discussions. It is a strong source for that narrowly defined topic.
- Claim A merely says that the slice describes basic concepts and their relationships; it does not state what ethics or morality is.
- Claims B and C are generic epistemic/professional caveats, not substantive ethics propositions.
- The SEP entry is about the definition of morality, not a complete introductory account of ethics. Treating it as blanket support for all three Claims would overstate its scope.
- The three Evidence Use records have no pinpoint locators or proposition-specific material.

**Disposition:** retain all 9 records on HOLD. Select a coherent introductory ethics scope (for example, normative/descriptive ethics, moral reasoning, values, and ethical theories) and attach topic-specific sources and locators. No such scope or Claims are approved by this screen.
