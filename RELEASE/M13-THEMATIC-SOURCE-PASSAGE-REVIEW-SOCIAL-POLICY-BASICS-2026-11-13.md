## Claim-level findings

### CLM-SOCIAL_POLICY_BASICS-A@1
**Claim:** Social policy covers measures intended to protect people and families from social risks and support well-being.

**Primary passage located — OECD, “Social policy”, opening summary and “Key messages”:**
- The opening summary states that social policy protects individuals and families and helps them lead a fulfilling life.
- “Key messages” describes social spending as redistribution supporting people in challenging circumstances, including poverty, unemployment and old age, with financial assistance and services for healthcare, families and housing.
- URL: https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/policy-issues/social-policy.html

**Corroborating passage — ILO, “Social protection”, definition paragraph:**
- Defines social protection/social security as benefits for risks across the life cycle, including unemployment, disability and maternity, and for people experiencing poverty and social exclusion.
- URL: https://www.ilo.org/topics-and-sectors/social-protection

**Fit:** Direct support for the core idea, with a terminology caveat: the sources speak most precisely about social policy/social protection, and “social risks” is a broad umbrella rather than a single standardized list. The Claim is suitable as a high-level educational statement, but its evidence records need exact passages rather than generic descriptions.

### CLM-SOCIAL_POLICY_BASICS-B@1
**Claim:** Social expenditure can include cash assistance and services in poverty, unemployment, health, family and housing domains.

**Primary passage located — OECD, “Social policy”, “Key messages”:**
- Explicitly lists financial assistance and services for people facing poverty or unemployment, healthcare benefits, family assistance programmes and housing support.
- URL: https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/policy-issues/social-policy.html

**More precise measurement passage — OECD, “Social spending”, Definition:**
- Social expenditure includes cash benefits, in-kind goods and services, and tax breaks with a social purpose; the definition also identifies groups and programme criteria.
- URL: https://www.oecd.org/en/data/indicators/social-spending.html

**Fit:** Direct support for the examples. The wording “social expenses” should not be read as an exhaustive classification: OECD measurement distinguishes cash, in-kind provision and tax breaks and applies programme criteria. The existing Claim is broadly correct but should ideally make the non-exhaustive nature explicit if later editorial revision is authorized.

### CLM-SOCIAL_POLICY_BASICS-C@1
**Claim:** Social protection differs across countries in coverage, financing, accessibility and eligibility conditions; parameters depend on country, period and current programmes.

**Primary passage located — OECD, “Social policy”, Context / Public social spending:**
- OECD reports marked differences in welfare-state size and public social spending across OECD countries, with country comparisons and historical change over time.
- URL: https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/policy-issues/social-policy.html

**Measurement support — OECD, “Social spending”, Definition:**
- Distinguishes public expenditure managed by general government from private expenditure and describes differences in programme design/participation and redistribution criteria.
- URL: https://www.oecd.org/en/data/indicators/social-spending.html

**Fit:** Direct support for cross-country and temporal variation in spending and system design. The exact phrase “accessibility and eligibility conditions” is not fully established by the cited OECD overview passage alone; it requires a more targeted comparative source/passages before the full sentence can be treated as fully supported. Do not infer programme-level eligibility from aggregate expenditure data.

## Evidence-record and source-identity findings
1. The three primary Evidence Use records point to an OECD topic landing page, but their material descriptions are generic or paraphrase-level. None identifies the specific “Key messages” or “Context / Public social spending” passage. These are reproducibility defects, not proof that the claims are false.
2. The purported independent ILO Source currently points to an ILO project page about partnership work concerning forcibly displaced people. That page is narrower than the general claims in this slice. The current ILO topical page at https://www.ilo.org/topics-and-sectors/social-protection provides a much closer general definition and lifecycle-risk framing.
3. The three “INDEPENDENT” Evidence Use records share one ILO Source record. They are not three independent sources. ILO is institutionally distinct from OECD, but independence of evidence must be assessed at the source/document level and cannot be inferred from the label.
4. For Claim B, OECD's dedicated Social Spending indicator provides a precise operational definition of social expenditure. For Claim C, country-comparison data should be paired with a targeted source on access/eligibility before retaining all dimensions in the current wording.

## Recommended bounded follow-up
- For the OECD Evidence Uses, record the exact page section/paragraph: “Key messages” for A and B; “Context — Public social spending” for C; and the dedicated “Social spending — Definition” for the measurement scope of B.
- Review whether the ILO Source identity/URI should be versioned to the general ILO “Social protection” topic page. Do not replace the existing Source or update Evidence Uses without explicit bounded editorial authorization.
- For C, locate and cite a comparative source that directly discusses access and eligibility rules, not merely spending levels.
- Keep all three Claims on HOLD for publication until exact pinpoints, source identity, rights review, and independent human review are complete.
