# M13 high-consequence Evidence Use locator review — environmental health, occupational safety and disaster response

Date: 2026-11-12
Scope: 12 Claims from the full-inventory high-consequence generic-Evidence-Use worklist. This report identifies exact source passages and Evidence Use remediation needs. It does not change D/E/B scores or canonical records.

## 1. Environmental health

### CLM-ENVIRONMENTAL_HEALTH_BASICS-B

Claim: environmental-health risk assessment uses exposure information and relationships between exposure and health outcomes.

Canonical source: WHO Environmental health topic page.

Finding: WHO's overview identifies environmental exposures and health impacts, but the page does not provide a method for exposure assessment or exposure-response analysis. The current Source is relevant to the domain but does not substantiate the methodological part of the Claim.

Required remediation:
- Add a primary risk-assessment source that defines exposure assessment and exposure-response/dose-response assessment (e.g. WHO human-health risk assessment guidance or an authoritative environmental epidemiology source).
- Add exact chapter/section/page locators for exposure assessment and exposure-response evaluation.
- Keep HOLD; do not treat a general environmental-health overview as support for the risk-assessment method.

### CLM-ENVIRONMENTAL_HEALTH_BASICS-C

Claim: environmental health requires intersectoral consideration because risks are connected with transport, energy, agriculture, cities and other systems.

WHO Environmental health, Overview, paragraphs 1–3 (page lines 86–91) describes health dependencies on clean air, stable climate, water/sanitation, chemical management, food production and working conditions. The page's broader environmental-health framework includes sectors and determinants beyond clinical services.

Finding: relevant and broadly supportive, but the listed sectors transport, energy, agriculture and urban systems should each be tied to explicit WHO text or a complementary WHO/UNEP source. Add a pinpoint for the relevant cross-sector paragraphs; do not infer a sector-specific causal effect from the general overview alone.

## 2. Wastewater

### CLM-WASTEWATER_BASICS-A

Claim: wastewater is collected and treated before water returns to the hydrological cycle.

US EPA Wastewater Basics 101:
- slides 2–3 describe wastewater collection and the purpose/scope of wastewater treatment systems;
- slides 10–11 describe wastewater composition and contaminants;
- slides 19–20 and 23–30 describe liquid/solid separation, secondary treatment and further treatment/disinfection before discharge.

Finding: the source supports collection and treatment prior to discharge/return to the environment. The phrase “hydrological cycle” is broader than the EPA presentation's explicit process description; add a hydrology/water-cycle source or narrow the statement to treated effluent being discharged/returned to the environment.

Proposed locator: “US EPA, Wastewater Basics 101, slides 2–3, 10–11, 19–20 and 23–30.”

## 3. Occupational safety

Canonical source: EU-OSHA OSHwiki, “OSH in general” (latest update 15 June 2025).

### CLM-OCCUPATIONAL_SAFETY_BASICS-A

Verified passage: Introduction and “What is occupational safety and health?”, lines 31–40. OSH is defined as a multidisciplinary field concerned with preventing work-related injuries, diseases and deaths and promoting safe and healthy working environments; the ILO definition emphasizes prevention and protection/promotion of workers' health.

Finding: DIRECT support.  
Proposed locator: “EU-OSHA OSHwiki, OSH in general, Introduction and Definition, lines 31–40.”

### CLM-OCCUPATIONAL_SAFETY_BASICS-B

Verified passages:
- Basic OSH concepts, lines 44–57: hazards, risk, risk assessment and hierarchy of controls.
- Managing OSH in the workplace, lines 65–71: risk assessment, controls, priorities/responsibilities, worker participation and review when workplace changes affect risks.
- Worker participation, lines 98–109: consultation and worker involvement.

Finding: DIRECT support. The claim appropriately combines risk identification/control, organizational processes and worker participation.  
Proposed locator: “EU-OSHA OSHwiki, OSH in general, Basic OSH concepts, lines 44–57; Managing OSH in the workplace, lines 65–71; Worker participation, lines 98–109.”

### CLM-OCCUPATIONAL_SAFETY_BASICS-C

Verified passages: EU OSH policy, lines 58–64, states the EU Framework Directive sets common minimum standards and Member States may adopt more rigorous provisions; workplace risk assessment and controls are contextual. Lines 69–71 explain review when work processes, equipment, premises or organization change.

Finding: DIRECT support for jurisdictional and workplace-specific dependence, within the EU framework. The Claim should not imply that EU-OSHA rules apply globally; national and sectoral requirements differ.  
Proposed locator: “EU-OSHA OSHwiki, OSH in general, EU OSH policy, lines 58–64; Risk assessment, lines 69–71.”

## 4. Workplace inspection

Canonical source: OSHA, “Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) Inspections” fact sheet (DEP FS-3783, August 2016).

### CLM-INSPECTION_BASICS-A

Verified passage: “On-Site Inspections,” lines 23–31 describes preparation, opening conference, walkaround and closing conference, including review of processes, standards, worksite conditions and records.

Finding: DIRECT support for a systematic inspection process with defined scope and stages.  
Proposed locator: “OSHA FactSheet, Occupational Safety and Health Administration Inspections, On-Site Inspections, lines 23–31.”

### CLM-INSPECTION_BASICS-B

Verified passages: lines 25–31 describe preparation, standards selection, testing instruments, scope, interviews, walkaround observations, records and discussion of findings. Lines 32–38 describe citations, correction deadlines and appeal/contest processes.

Finding: DIRECT support for dependence on scope, methods, criteria and recorded data. The source is a U.S. workplace-enforcement example; it should not be presented as a universal inspection procedure for all technical domains.  
Proposed locator: “OSHA FactSheet, Occupational Safety and Health Administration Inspections, lines 25–38.”

### CLM-INSPECTION_BASICS-C

Verified passage: lines 31–38 describes findings, possible citations and contest/appeal mechanisms.

Finding: PARTIAL. The fact sheet supports that an inspection produces findings and may trigger further regulatory action, but it does not directly establish that inspection is not a substitute for a specialized technical conclusion. Add an authoritative inspection/engineering standard that distinguishes visual/field inspection from testing, diagnosis, certification or engineering assessment. Keep HOLD.

## 5. Disaster behavioral health

Canonical source: SAMHSA Disaster Behavioral Health Planners Resource Portal and Disaster Response Needs Assessment Toolkit.

### CLM-DISASTER_BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH_BASICS-A

Verified passages: SAMHSA Disaster Behavioral Health Planners Resource Portal states that plans address mental and behavioural health capacity for communities and individuals affected by hazards. The Needs Assessment Toolkit states that assessment determines the need for crisis counseling, mental-health/substance-use treatment, public education and information dissemination.

Finding: DIRECT support for mental, emotional and substance-use needs in affected communities.  
Proposed locator: “SAMHSA Disaster Behavioral Health Planners Resource Portal, ‘Developing Comprehensive Disaster Behavioral Health Plans’; Disaster Response Needs Assessment Toolkit, ‘What is a Needs Assessment?’”

### CLM-DISASTER_BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH_BASICS-B

Verified passages: SAMHSA disaster-planning materials identify preparedness, response, recovery and phase-down of operations; the Disaster Behavioral Health planning portal covers planning for multiple hazard types and response/recovery.

Finding: PARTIAL. The accessed SAMHSA material explicitly identifies preparedness, response, recovery and phase-down, but does not clearly identify mitigation in the same framework. Add FEMA/WHO disaster-risk-reduction guidance that explicitly includes mitigation as a phase or activity. Do not silently equate phase-down with mitigation.

### CLM-DISASTER_BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH_BASICS-C

Verified passage: SAMHSA Needs Assessment Toolkit says needs assessment is continuous, identifies at-risk populations and service needs, uses local damage/casualty/exposure information and should be updated throughout disaster response to adapt services to changing community needs.

Finding: DIRECT support for phase- and community-specific planning.  
Proposed locator: “SAMHSA Disaster Response Needs Assessment Toolkit, ‘What is a Needs Assessment?’ and ‘A needs assessment’.”
