## 1. accounting-basics — 9 records

Claims:
- A: accounting produces information about economic transactions and financial position.
- B: financial statements present information in a structured form for statement users.
- C: accounting estimates and classifications depend on the applicable standards and conditions.

Current Source: IFRS Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting  
https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/conceptual-framework/

Findings:
- The IFRS Conceptual Framework directly covers the objective of general-purpose financial reporting, useful financial information, definitions of financial statement elements, recognition/derecognition, measurement bases, presentation and disclosure. This provides a strong source fit for Claims B/C.
- Claim A is broadly compatible with financial reporting's purpose, but the current Source should be pinned to the framework's objective and reporting-entity/financial-position passages; the statement also uses the broader term “accounting” rather than only financial reporting.
- All three EUs are generic and provide no framework paragraph/section locators.
- Context and Scope target Claim A only.
- IFRS standards and framework text are copyrighted; factual paraphrase is distinct from reproducing the framework. The intended edition must not reproduce IFRS text/tables without permission and must keep attribution/rights review separate.

Decision: **HOLD — strong authoritative source fit, incomplete pinpoint mapping and rights review.** Use exact IFRS Conceptual Framework sections and keep the distinction between bookkeeping/accounting processes and general-purpose financial reporting clear.

## 2. macroeconomics-basics — 9 records

Claims:
- A: macroeconomics considers aggregate indicators of production, income, employment and prices.
- B: GDP measures the market value of final goods and services produced in an economy over a period.
- C: inflation describes a sustained change in the general price level measured by a selected indicator.

Current Source: IMF Finance & Development, “Back to Basics” collection  
https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/fandd/issues/Series/Back-to-Basics

Additional direct IMF material checked:
- “Gross Domestic Product: An Economy’s All”: https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/fandd/issues/Series/Back-to-Basics/gross-domestic-product-gdp

Findings:
- Claim A is consistent with macroeconomic analysis, but the current Source is a series landing page rather than a specific definition or section.
- Claim B is directly supported by the IMF GDP article: GDP measures the monetary value of final goods and services produced within a country's borders over a specified period. The Claim should retain the final-output, territorial and time-period boundaries.
- Claim C is conceptually correct, but no exact IMF inflation article/locator is represented in the current Source/EU chain. It should distinguish a sustained general price-level increase from a one-time price change and identify the selected index when used operationally.
- All three EUs are generic and do not name specific IMF articles or passages.
- Context and Scope target Claim A only.

Decision: **HOLD — good substantive fit, insufficient source pinpoints.** Add exact IMF GDP and inflation article references and claim-specific locators through authorized versioned evidence work. Do not treat the IMF collection landing page as direct evidence for every macroeconomic proposition.

## 3. microeconomics-basics — 9 records

Claims:
- A: supply and demand describe the relationship between price and quantity under specified conditions.
- B: markets coordinate the decisions of buyers and sellers through exchange and prices.
- C: market equilibrium depends on demand, supply and the selected definition of the market.

Source: OpenStax Principles of Economics 3e  
https://openstax.org/details/books/principles-economics-3e

Direct sections checked:
- Chapter 3, “Demand, Supply, and Equilibrium in Markets for Goods and Services”: https://openstax.org/books/principles-economics-3e/pages/3-1-demand-supply-and-equilibrium-in-markets-for-goods-and-services
- §3.5, “Demand, Supply, and Efficiency”: https://openstax.org/books/principles-economics-3e/pages/3-5-demand-supply-and-efficiency

Findings:
- Claim A is directly supported by Chapter 3's demand/supply curves and ceteris paribus conditions.
- Claim B is supported by §3.5, which describes demand and supply as a social adjustment mechanism and explains how market prices coordinate decisions about what is produced and for whom.
- Claim C is supported by the equilibrium treatment in §3.1 and the shifts/market-definition context in §§3.2–3.3. It should not imply that every market has a unique stable equilibrium independent of assumptions.
- All three EUs are generic and do not identify the relevant chapter/sections.
- Context and Scope target Claim A only.
- OpenStax Principles of Economics 3e is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; attribution, noncommercial, ShareAlike and LLM-ingestion restrictions require rights review for the planned edition.

Decision: **HOLD — strong direct source fit.** The three Claims are supportable, but the EUs need section-level pinpoints, the equilibrium assumptions must remain visible, and rights/reader review remain open.

## 4. money-and-banking-basics — 9 records

Claims:
- A: money is used as a means of payment and a store of value.
- B: monetary aggregates classify monetary and near-money assets according to liquidity and function.
- C: different forms of money and financial assets differ in liquidity and use.

Source: Federal Reserve, “New Forms of Money and the U.S. Monetary Aggregates” (4 September 2026)  
https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/new-forms-of-money-and-the-u-s-monetary-aggregates-20260904.html

Findings:
- The Federal Reserve defines money as a group of safe assets used for payments or short-term investment and explicitly discusses medium-of-exchange and store-of-value functions. This directly supports Claim A.
- The same note explains monetary aggregates as classifications by liquidity and function, including M1 and M2. This directly supports Claim B.
- It distinguishes highly liquid transaction assets from less liquid savings-type assets and discusses their uses, directly supporting Claim C.
- The existing Context appropriately states that definitions depend on jurisdiction, aggregate definition, asset type and period.
- All three EUs remain generic and do not cite the relevant Federal Reserve sections or the date/vintage.
- Scope is general educational information and explicitly not individual financial advice.

Decision: **HOLD — strong direct source fit with a good jurisdiction/time boundary.** Add exact Federal Reserve locators, preserve the U.S.-specific scope and date, and do not generalize U.S. M1/M2 definitions to other jurisdictions.

## 5. organizational-behavior-basics — 9 records

Claims:
- A: organizational behaviour studies interactions among people, groups and organizational conditions.
- B: motivation, communication and task structure can influence workplace behaviour.
- C: conclusions about organizational behaviour depend on the group studied and observation conditions.

Source: American Psychological Association, “Industrial and Organizational Psychology”  
https://www.apa.org/ed/graduate/specialize/industrial.aspx

Findings:
- The APA directly describes industrial-organizational psychology as the scientific study of individual, group and organizational behaviour in workplaces. This supports Claim A.
- The APA source lists workplace motivation, reward systems, structure of work and human factors, which supports part of Claim B; communication needs a more direct locator or separate evidence.
- Claim C is a methodological boundary and needs evidence on study populations, settings, sampling and external validity; the current source does not directly establish all of those conditions.
- All EUs are generic and lack claim-specific locators.
- Context and Scope target Claim A only.

Decision: **HOLD — good introductory source fit, incomplete claim-level evidence.** Use precise APA sections for individual/group/organizational behaviour and motivation/work structure; add direct support for communication and research-context generalizability.
