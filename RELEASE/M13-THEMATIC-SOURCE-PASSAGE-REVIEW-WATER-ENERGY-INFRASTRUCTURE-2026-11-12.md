# M13 thematic source-passage review — water, energy and infrastructure systems

Date: 2026-11-12
Branch: `m13-system-wide-depth-audit-2026-10-09`
Queue: 359 generic-Evidence-Use Claims remaining outside the 140-Claim machine high-consequence subset, after the first 20-Claim elevated review.
Scope: 20 Claims selected by topic coherence and practical relevance across energy security, hydropower, water resources, waste/materials management, infrastructure governance and building enclosures.

## 1. Purpose and method

This is a thematic source-passage review, not a numbered scoring Batch. It examines the exact Claim statements against the linked Source records and authoritative public passages. Generic Evidence Use descriptions still do not provide reproducible Claim-specific locators. The locators below are proposals for controlled remediation; they have not been written into canonical records.

A passage that supports a Claim at the stated scope does not by itself authorize publication, raise the current E score, or establish independent corroboration. Where the linked source is too broad or only partially supports the proposition, the gap is stated explicitly.

## 2. Energy security — 3 Claims

Canonical Source: International Energy Agency (IEA), “Energy Security”, https://www.iea.org/topics/energy-security

- `CLM-ENERGY_SECURITY_BASICS-A` — the IEA page heading “Reliable, affordable access to all fuels and energy sources” and its opening description of the Agency’s work to avoid, mitigate and manage energy disruptions support the core definition. Proposed locator: IEA, “Energy Security”, page heading and opening overview.
- `CLM-ENERGY_SECURITY_BASICS-B` — the opening overview identifies geopolitical risks, cyberattacks on energy infrastructure, supply-chain disruptions and extreme weather. Proposed locator: IEA, “Energy Security”, opening overview, paragraph on evolving threats.
- `CLM-ENERGY_SECURITY_BASICS-C` — the current IEA topic page is not sufficiently specific for the Claim’s assessment dimensions. IEA, “ASEAN Energy Security Review”, section “Energy security context, framework and overview”, explicitly frames assessment across fuels, technologies and systems and identifies a five-year assessment horizon and regional context. Proposed action: add this report as a distinct Source and use its assessment-framework section as an additional Evidence Use. Do not silently attribute it to the existing IEA topic-page Source.

Disposition: A/B DIRECT; C PARTIAL pending an additional versioned Source/Evidence Use and exact locator. Preserve the fact that energy-security assessment is fuel-, infrastructure-, time- and region-dependent.

## 3. Hydropower — 2 Claims

Canonical Source: U.S. Department of Energy (DOE), “Hydropower Basics”, https://www.energy.gov/cmei/water/hydropower-basics

The DOE page “Types of Hydropower Plants”, https://www.energy.gov/cmei/water/types-hydropower-plants, provides more precise passages than the general overview.

- `CLM-HYDROPOWER_BASICS-B` — DOE identifies impoundment, diversion and pumped-storage facilities; the “Diversion” subsection describes run-of-river arrangements that channel part of a stream through a powerhouse. Proposed locator: DOE, “Types of Hydropower Plants”, “Overview”, “Impoundment” and “Diversion”.
- `CLM-HYDROPOWER_BASICS-C` — the “Pumped Storage” subsection describes pumping water from a lower reservoir to an upper reservoir when demand is low and releasing it through a turbine when demand is high. Proposed locator: DOE, “Types of Hydropower Plants”, “Pumped Storage”.

Disposition: DIRECT at the stated descriptive scope. These passages do not establish the safety, environmental acceptability, site suitability or engineering design of any particular hydropower project.

## 4. Water resources — 2 Claims

Canonical Source: U.S. Geological Survey (USGS), “Water Resources Mission Area”, https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/water-resources

- `CLM_WATER_RESOURCES_BASICS_A` — the opening paragraph identifies streamflow, groundwater, water quality, water use and availability as monitored and assessed water-resource conditions. The present page does not explicitly establish the Claim’s full reference to wetlands and all ecosystem functions. Proposed locator: USGS, “Water Resources Mission Area”, opening paragraph and “Popular Water Data and Tools”. Add a distinct wetlands/ecosystem source if that part of the Claim is intended to remain.
- `CLM_WATER_RESOURCES_BASICS_C` — the page supports monitoring and assessment across multiple water-resource conditions and identifies data products collected at approximately 1.9 million sites. It does not, by itself, establish the full methodological statement about spatial and temporal interpretation. Proposed locator: USGS, “Water Resources Mission Area”, opening paragraph and “Water Data for the Nation”; add a source that directly explains spatial/temporal representativeness and observation limits.

Disposition: PARTIAL for both Claims pending augmentation. The same USGS URL is also used by the separate water-resources-management Source record; the two Source IDs must not be counted as independent corroboration merely because their identifiers differ.

## 5. Water-resources management — 3 Claims

Canonical Source: U.S. Geological Survey (USGS), “Water Resources Mission Area”, https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/water-resources

- `CLM-WATER_RESOURCES_MANAGEMENT_BASICS-A` — USGS identifies streamflow, groundwater, water quality, water use and availability as core water-resource conditions. This supports quantity/quality/availability but does not fully establish distribution and variability as a complete management framework. Proposed locator: USGS, “Water Resources Mission Area”, opening paragraph and “Popular Water Data and Tools”.
- `CLM-WATER_RESOURCES_MANAGEMENT_BASICS-B` — the USGS page explicitly describes monitoring and assessment and identifies streamflow and groundwater information. Proposed locator: USGS, “Water Resources Mission Area”, opening paragraph and “Water Data for the Nation”.
- `CLM-WATER_RESOURCES_MANAGEMENT_BASICS-C` — the linked USGS page does not directly establish the full decision framework involving user needs, infrastructure and applicable rules. Add jurisdiction- and basin-specific water-management policy/primary sources before treating the whole Claim as directly evidenced.

Disposition: B DIRECT; A/C PARTIAL. Keep the natural-resource data source distinct from sources establishing allocation rules, infrastructure constraints and user priorities.

## 6. Water security — 2 Claims

Canonical Source: UN-Water, “Water Security and the Global Water Agenda”, https://www.unwater.org/publications/water-security-and-global-water-agenda

- `CLM-WATER_SECURITY-A` — the brief’s section “A Working Definition of Water Security” defines water security in terms of sustainable access to adequate quantities of acceptable-quality water. Proposed locator: UN-Water Analytical Brief, §1, “A Working Definition of Water Security”, definition paragraph.
- `CLM-WATER_SECURITY-C` — the same definition and its explanatory paragraph connect water security to the water cycle, livelihoods, human well-being, socio-economic development, water-related pollution/disasters, ecosystems and political/social stability. Proposed locator: UN-Water Analytical Brief, §1, definition and explanatory paragraph.

Disposition: DIRECT as a working international policy definition. Preserve the source’s status as an analytical brief and working definition; do not present it as a binding legal standard or a technical water-safety specification.

## 7. Waste and materials infrastructure — 2 Claims

Canonical Source: U.S. EPA, Sustainable Materials Management, https://www.epa.gov/smm

More specific passages:
- EPA, “Sustainable Materials Management Basics”, https://www.epa.gov/smm/sustainable-materials-management-basics
- EPA, “Sustainable Materials Management Tools”, https://www.epa.gov/smm/sustainable-materials-management-tools
- EPA, “Sustainable Materials Management: Non-Hazardous Materials and Waste Management Hierarchy”, https://www.epa.gov/smm/sustainable-materials-management-non-hazardous-materials-and-waste-management-hierarchy

- `CLM-WASTE_INFRASTRUCTURE_BASICS-B` — EPA’s hierarchy page states that no single waste-management approach is suitable for all materials and waste streams in all circumstances. EPA’s SMM Tools page describes measuring production, use and entry into waste-management streams and evaluating environmental and economic effects. Proposed locator: EPA, “Non-Hazardous Materials and Waste Management Hierarchy”, opening paragraph; “Sustainable Materials Management Tools”, “Why is Measurement Important?”.
- `CLM-WASTE_INFRASTRUCTURE_BASICS-C` — EPA’s SMM tools support considering environmental impacts and economic benefits in a specific scenario, while the SMM Basics page describes life-cycle impacts and resource use. These sources do not fully establish every operational constraint (local capacity, technology, workforce and regulatory requirements). Proposed locator: EPA, “Sustainable Materials Management Basics”, “What is Sustainable Materials Management?” and “Sustainable Material Management’s Life-cycle Perspective”; supplement with local system/permit and technical feasibility sources where the Claim is applied.

Disposition: PARTIAL for both Claims until the relevant local and operational constraints are evidenced. The existing EPA landing page is too broad to serve as the sole pinpoint for the full propositions.

- `CLM-INFRASTRUCTURE_GOVERNANCE_BASICS-B` — OECD describes alignment of strategic goals, project selection, funding and implementation, with evidence-based analysis and coordination. Proposed locator: OECD, “Infrastructure Governance”, “Long-term strategic infrastructure planning” and “Project selection and prioritisation”.
- `CLM-INFRASTRUCTURE_GOVERNANCE_BASICS-C` — OECD Infrastructure Governance Indicators describes a life-cycle perspective spanning long-term strategic planning, project appraisal, capital budgeting, procurement, asset management, monitoring and evaluation. Proposed locator: OECD Infrastructure Governance Indicators, section on the life-cycle perspective and Figure 4.2.

Disposition: DIRECT at the stated governance-framework level. The OECD material describes policy and institutional practice; it is not a substitute for the applicable national legal framework or project-specific approval requirements.

## 9. Building envelope — 3 Claims

Canonical Source: National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), Building Science, https://www.nist.gov/building-science

The landing page is not sufficiently specific for the three technical propositions. More precise NIST material includes:
- NIST Technical Note 1869, section 3.1.1, “Building Enclosure Design”: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/TechnicalNotes/NIST.TN.1869.pdf
- NIST Technical Note 1847, section 2.2.5, “Establish water and moisture control for the building enclosure”: https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=916412
- NIST Building Science Series 173, “Introduction”: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/BSS/nistbuildingscience173.pdf
- WBDG, “Building Enclosure Design Principles and Strategies”: https://www.wbdg.org/resources/building-enclosure-design-principles-and-strategies

- `CLM-BUILDING_ENVELOPE_BASICS-A` — NIST TN 1869 §3.1.1 describes the enclosure as the physical separation between inside and outside and identifies water, airflow, vapor and thermal control systems. Proposed locator: NIST TN 1869, §3.1.1, “Building Enclosure Design”.
- `CLM-BUILDING_ENVELOPE_BASICS-B` — NIST TN 1869 §3.1.1 links water/vapor control to durability and indoor environmental quality and airflow/thermal control to heat transfer. Proposed locator: NIST TN 1869, §3.1.1; NIST TN 1847 §2.2.5.
- `CLM-BUILDING_ENVELOPE_BASICS-C` — NIST BSS 173 explains that moisture-control practices depend on geographic location, construction materials, field studies and local experience. WBDG provides additional treatment of performance requirements, materials/assemblies and continuity of enclosure control layers. The current evidence does not yet fully establish workmanship/installation quality as a separate factor. Proposed locator: NIST BSS 173, “Introduction”; WBDG, “Building Enclosure Design Principles and Strategies”, sections on performance requirements and continuity of control layers.

Disposition: A/B DIRECT at the general building-science level; C PARTIAL pending a direct workmanship/installation passage and explicit climate/material scope. These sources do not provide a universal construction detail or substitute for current local codes and qualified design.
