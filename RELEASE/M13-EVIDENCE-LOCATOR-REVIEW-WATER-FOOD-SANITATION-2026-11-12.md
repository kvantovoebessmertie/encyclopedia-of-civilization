### Environmental health

**CLM-ENVIRONMENTAL_HEALTH_BASICS-A**  
Claim: environmental health is affected by air, water and sanitation, chemical exposures and climate conditions.  
Canonical source: WHO Environmental health topic page.  
Verified passage: Overview, paragraphs 1–2. WHO states that good health depends on clean air, stable climate, preserved environment, adequate water/sanitation/hygiene and protection from harmful radiation, unsafe chemical management and unhealthy working conditions; it then describes indirect impacts through food production, migration, economic instability and social inequality.  
Proposed Evidence Use locator: “WHO, Environmental health, Overview, paragraphs 1–2 (page lines 86–89), accessed 2026-11-12.”  
Fit: DIRECT. The Claim's list is narrower than WHO's full list and is supported.

### Food preservation

The canonical Source record identifies USDA FSIS “Food Safety Basics” at the FSIS overview URL. Automated retrieval of that landing page returned HTTP 403 in this check. Official FSIS askFSIS Q&A pages and FoodSafety.gov provide accessible, precise support but are distinct URLs and should be recorded as additional Source records if used. Do not silently attribute them to the existing Source record.

**CLM-FOOD_PRESERVATION_BASICS-A**  
Claim: food safety requires control of factors that support growth or transfer of hazardous microorganisms.  
Verified passages: FSIS askFSIS, “What is the Danger Zone?” (updated 6 Sep 2024): 40°F–140°F (4.4°C–60°C) is the range in which bacteria can grow rapidly; keep cold foods at or below 40°F and hot foods at or above 140°F; limit time in the danger zone. FSIS “How do I keep my foods safe to eat?” (updated 1 Oct 2024) explains clean/separate/cook/chill and that cold temperatures slow bacterial growth.  
Proposed locator if these official Q&A pages are added as Sources: “USDA FSIS askFSIS, What is the Danger Zone?, answer body, updated 2024-09-06; How do I keep my foods safe to eat?, steps 1–4, updated 2024-10-01.”  
Fit: DIRECT for the general microbial-control principle. The current canonical source landing page alone does not preserve these exact locators.

**CLM-FOOD_PRESERVATION_BASICS-B**  
Claim: food safety depends on handling, storage conditions, time, temperature and product properties.  
Verified passages: FSIS “What is the Danger Zone?” and FoodSafety.gov “4 Steps to Food Safety,” section “Chill: Refrigerate and Freeze Food Properly,” explain time-temperature controls, the 2-hour/1-hour rule, refrigerator/freezer limits and product-specific storage charts. FSIS also warns that some spoilage/pathogenic bacteria can grow at refrigerator temperatures, including Listeria.  
Proposed locator: “FoodSafety.gov, 4 Steps to Food Safety, Cook and Chill sections; USDA FSIS askFSIS, What is the Danger Zone? and Can bacteria grow in the refrigerator?, answer bodies.”  
Fit: DIRECT, provided product-specific guidance is retained rather than generalizing one time/temperature value to all foods.

**CLM-FOOD_PRESERVATION_BASICS-C**  
Claim: general food-preservation information does not replace product- and method-specific safety requirements from authoritative sources.  
Verified passages: FDA Food Safety at Home, “4 basic steps for food safety,” lines 108–129, provides separate cooking temperatures for different food categories and time limits for chilling; FoodSafety.gov provides safe-storage charts and distinct product handling instructions.  
Proposed locator: “FDA, Food Safety at Home, 4 basic steps, lines 108–129; FoodSafety.gov, 4 Steps to Food Safety, Chill section and Safe Storage Times chart.”  
Fit: DIRECT as a boundary principle. It is especially important because product-specific temperatures, storage periods and preservation methods are not interchangeable.

### Food safety

Canonical source: FDA, Food Safety at Home.

**CLM-FOOD_SAFETY_BASICS-A**  
Claim: basic measures include cleaning, separation, adequate heat treatment and proper chilling.  
Verified passage: FDA Food Safety at Home, “4 basic steps for food safety,” lines 106–129. It lists Clean, Separate, Cook and Chill with detailed handling steps.  
Proposed locator: “FDA, Food Safety at Home, section ‘4 basic steps for food safety’, lines 106–129.”  
Fit: DIRECT.

**CLM-FOOD_SAFETY_BASICS-B**  
Claim: separating raw food and ready-to-eat food reduces cross-contamination risk.  
Verified passage: FDA Food Safety at Home, “Separate (keep apart),” lines 114–118. FDA says raw meat, poultry, seafood and eggs should be kept away from other foods because germs can spread from one food to another.  
Proposed locator: “FDA, Food Safety at Home, ‘Separate (keep apart)’, lines 114–118.”  
Fit: DIRECT.

**CLM-FOOD_SAFETY_BASICS-C**  
Claim: temperature, storage and food-risk controls depend on product, process and applicable norms.  
Verified passage: FDA Food Safety at Home, cooking temperatures and chill time rules, lines 120–134; it lists different internal temperatures by food category and the 2-hour/1-hour chilling rule. FoodSafety.gov additionally links safe storage times and special population guidance.  
Proposed locator: “FDA, Food Safety at Home, ‘Cook’ and ‘Chill’, lines 120–134; FoodSafety.gov, 4 Steps to Food Safety, Cook and Chill sections.”  
Fit: DIRECT for product/process dependence. Jurisdictional qualification is appropriate: FDA/USDA values are U.S. federal guidance, not universal legal requirements.

### Wastewater

Canonical source: US EPA, Wastewater 101: The Fundamentals, with the linked PDF presentation.

**CLM-WASTEWATER_BASICS-B**  
Claim: wastewater treatment transforms contaminated water to reduce associated risks and impacts.  
Verified passages: EPA Wastewater Basics 101 PDF, pp. 10–11 (PDF lines 51–57) identifies organic matter, microorganisms including pathogens and inorganic compounds as contaminants; pp. 19–20 (lines 114–120) introduces liquid/solid separation; pp. 23–30 (lines 138–166) describes secondary treatment and reduction of oxygen demand and microbial populations; pp. 26–27 (lines 162–172) describes clarification, disinfection and nutrient removal.  
Proposed locator: “US EPA, Wastewater Basics 101, slides 10–11, 19–20, 23–27 and 30 (PDF pages 10–11, 19–20, 23–27 and 30).”  
Fit: DIRECT for the broad purpose. The presentation is introductory and not a design standard.

**CLM-WASTEWATER_BASICS-C**  
Claim: treatment processes and requirements depend on wastewater composition, system, intended use and local regulation.  
Verified passages: EPA Wastewater Basics 101, pp. 2–3 (scope includes individual/small-community systems); pp. 23–25 (secondary treatment choices depend on land and energy); pp. 26–34 (nutrient removal and disinfection depend on the discharge context and nutrient requirements).  
Proposed locator: “US EPA, Wastewater Basics 101, slides 2–3, 23–25 and 26–34.”  
Fit: PARTIAL. The source supports system/process variation and technical constraints, but it does not establish the full local regulatory component. Add the applicable EPA/state/local permit or wastewater standard as an additional Source before treating the whole Claim as directly evidenced.

### Drinking-water distribution infrastructure

Canonical source: US EPA, Drinking Water Distribution System Tools and Resources.

**CLM-WATER_INFRASTRUCTURE_BASICS-A**  
Claim: distribution systems connect sources/treatment plants to consumers through pipes, storage, valves and pumps.  
Verified passage: EPA page, introductory paragraph, lines 3–5. It defines distribution systems and lists pipes, storage facilities, valves and pumps.  
Proposed locator: “US EPA, Drinking Water Distribution System Tools and Resources, introductory definition, lines 3–5.”  
Fit: DIRECT.

**CLM-WATER_INFRASTRUCTURE_BASICS-B**  
Claim: distribution networks are a major physical part of water systems and must be operated to reduce contamination risk.  
Verified passage: EPA page, lines 5–7. It calls distribution systems the vast majority of physical infrastructure and the final barrier against contamination, and says they must be operated/maintained to reduce contamination from external/internal sources including microbial growth and corrosion.  
Proposed locator: “US EPA, Drinking Water Distribution System Tools and Resources, lines 5–7.”  
Fit: DIRECT.

**CLM-WATER_INFRASTRUCTURE_BASICS-C**  
Claim: pressure, water age, storage, corrosion, backflow and other conditions affect distribution quality; requirements depend on system/jurisdiction.  
Verified passages: EPA page, lines 7–17 lists water age, storage, pressure management, corrosion/leaching and cross-connections/backflow; lines 51–64 discusses biofilms, microbial growth, disinfectant residual and disinfection byproducts.  
Proposed locator: “US EPA, Drinking Water Distribution System Tools and Resources, lines 7–17 and 51–64.”  
Fit: DIRECT for the listed system factors. Jurisdiction-specific requirements still need local regulatory sources.
