## Executive result

The three Claims are substantively supported by ILO material. The current Source is only a broad ILO topic landing page and the Evidence Uses repeat a generic description without identifying the actual definition, programme characteristics or measurement framework. The key correction is to use the ILO World Social Protection Report 2024–26 glossary and measurement annex, rather than treating the topic page as a reproducible pinpoint.

**All 3 Claims remain HOLD** until the source identity and exact locators are recorded and independently reviewed. No canonical records, scores, publication states, manifests or release artifacts were changed.

## Record-by-record findings

### Claim A — definition and purpose

Claim: Social protection includes systems and measures intended to protect people against social and economic risks.

Candidate direct support:
- ILO, *World Social Protection Report 2024–26*, Annex 1 Glossary: defines social protection/social security as policies and programmes designed to reduce and prevent poverty, vulnerability and social exclusion throughout the life cycle.
- The same glossary lists nine main areas, including child/family, maternity, unemployment, employment injury, sickness, health, old age, disability and survivors' benefits.
- ILO topic overview explains that benefits respond to risks across the life cycle and that systems can be financed through wage contributions/social insurance or general taxation/social assistance.

Finding: **DIRECT SUPPORT FOUND**. The current claim is a valid high-level summary, but “social and economic risks” is less precise than the ILO's life-cycle poverty, vulnerability and exclusion framing. Consider wording that mirrors the ILO definition without implying that every system has identical design.

### Claim B — country variation in programmes, eligibility and financing

Claim: Specific programmes vary by country, access conditions and financing sources.

Candidate direct support:
- ILO, *World Social Protection Report 2024–26*, Annex 1 Glossary: a programme/scheme's rules specify geographical and personal scope/target group, entitlement conditions, benefit type and amount, periodicity, financing, governance and administration.
- The glossary distinguishes contributory social insurance from non-contributory, tax-financed benefits and explains that schemes are nationally defined.
- ILO Convention No. 102 FAQ outlines minimum standards and recognizes different national arrangements for financing, governance and administration.

Finding: **DIRECT SUPPORT FOUND**. The claim is well framed. The EU should cite the programme/scheme definition and the financing paragraph, not the generic ILO topic page.

### Claim C — evaluating coverage, adequacy and accessibility

Claim: Evaluation requires considering coverage, adequacy and accessibility.

Candidate direct support:
- ILO, *World Social Protection Report 2024–26*, Annex 2 “Measuring social protection coverage and expenditure”: coverage is multidimensional, including scope, extent and level/adequacy.
- The same report's Chapter 3 distinguishes effective access, comprehensive/adequate protection and sustainable/equitable financing; sections 3.2 and 3.3 cover access, coverage and adequacy.
- OECD Social Protection System Review describes coverage, adequacy/effectiveness, equity, efficiency and sustainability as distinct analytical dimensions.

Finding: **DIRECT SUPPORT FOUND, WITH TERMINOLOGY REFINEMENT**. “Accessibility” is supported as effective access; “coverage” and “adequacy” are distinct dimensions. ILO Annex 2 uses scope, extent and level as measurement dimensions. The EU should avoid collapsing these into a single coverage percentage.
