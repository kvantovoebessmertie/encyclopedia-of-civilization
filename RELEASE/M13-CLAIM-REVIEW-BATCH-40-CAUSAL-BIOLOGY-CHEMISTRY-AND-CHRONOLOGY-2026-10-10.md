## Method
Reviewed 30 Claim statements across ten slices: causal inference, cell basics, cell biology basics, central banking, ceramics, chemical engineering, chemical reactions, chemical-water advisories, chronology methods, and civil engineering. D = explanatory depth, E = claim-specific evidence fit, B = boundary discipline; each scored independently from 0–3. Source identity and canonical locator were checked. Scores assess the claim and evidence traceability as represented in this corpus, not professional validation or a live safety assessment. Machine triage is not treated as a semantic score.

## Consolidated findings

1. **Evidence traceability remains uneven.** The chemistry reaction-stoichiometry claims and ceramic safety claims have stronger topical/claim fit. Multiple other claims use broad institutional landing pages without a precise passage/section. The source identity alone is not enough for reproducible claim-level verification.
2. **Editorial claims are mixed into substantive domain scoring.** All three cell-biology basics claims and all three civil-engineering basics claims are generic statements about the slice's educational role, context or limits. They should not inflate counts of substantive biology/engineering knowledge. This is a corpus taxonomy/coverage issue, not an automatic authorization to rewrite the records.
3. **High-consequence ceramic safety:** respirable crystalline silica, glaze constituents, kiln heat/emissions and food-contact safety have relevant institutional source identities. Keep the boundaries visible and do not imply generic educational claims are product safety certification or installation/operating instructions.
4. **Chemical-water advisory:** the CDC claim that boiling does not remove harmful chemical contaminants is a critical distinction from microbial disinfection. “Do not drink” notices are use-specific; follow the local advisory exactly rather than inferring rules for every exposure route.
5. **Causal inference:** association is not causation; diagrams and counterfactuals make assumptions explicit but do not prove them. Keep this as a prominent cross-domain epistemic boundary.
6. **Central banking:** the claims correctly acknowledge variation in mandates and jurisdictional context, but broad BIS source locators need claim-specific sections. Avoid universalizing one central bank's mandate or assuming policy effects from a generic description.
7. No content Records were changed. This report records audit findings only.
