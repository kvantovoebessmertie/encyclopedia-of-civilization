## 4. Cross-record and source-quality findings

1. `SRC-RELIABILITY_ENGINEERING_BASICS` is a relevant NASA systems-engineering appendix and directly defines reliability, but it only lists FMEA in its glossary. The failure-analysis method in Claim C needs a specific FMEA/FMECA source such as NASA SWEHB section 8.5 or active GSFC-HDBK-8004.
2. `SRC-RELIGIOUS_STUDIES` is the Harvard Pluralism Project's “About” page. It supports research on religious diversity and changing religious landscapes in the United States, but it is not a disciplinary methodology source for the confessional/non-confessional distinction. The AAR Religious Literacy Guidelines are a better candidate for Claims A and C.
3. `SRC-REMOTE_SENSING_BASICS` is a Landsat mission page. It supports satellite acquisition and points to remote-sensing resources, but it is not a complete technical source for reflectance, target properties, spectral/spatial resolution and observation conditions.
4. `SRC-RESEARCH_DATA_MANAGEMENT_BASICS` is a USGS lifecycle hub. It directly supports the lifecycle structure, but its Metadata Creation, Data Standards, Manage Quality and Data Release links should be represented as separate exact sources for Claims B and C.
5. `SRC-RESEARCH_METHODS_BASICS` is a strong report for reproducibility/replicability and research validity, but it is not a general definition of the entire scientific method. Keep Claim A narrower or add a dedicated research-methods source.
6. `SRC-RESOURCE_ALLOCATION_BASICS` is a broad World Bank Public Sector Management overview. It does not establish a general or formal resource-allocation decision framework by itself.
7. `SRC-ROBOTICS_ENGINEERING_BASICS` is a NIST topic hub. It lists relevant robotics domains but does not provide the system architecture, implementation or validation criteria asserted by the Claims.
8. All Evidence Use descriptions are generic and lack exact passage locators. A valid Claim → Evidence Use → Source chain does not by itself establish complete substantive support.
