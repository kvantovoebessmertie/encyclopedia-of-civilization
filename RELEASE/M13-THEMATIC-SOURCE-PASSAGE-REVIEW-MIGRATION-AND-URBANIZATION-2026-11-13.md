# M13 thematic source-passage review — Migration and Urbanization

Date: 2026-11-13  
Branch: `m13-system-wide-depth-audit-2026-10-09`  
Review type: source-passage desk review; noncanonical audit artifact  
Scope: Migration Basics (3 Claims), Migration and Mobility Basics (3 Claims), Urbanization (3 Claims), associated Evidence Uses and Sources

## Executive result

Nine Claims were checked against the source records and official institutional material. The two migration slices contain useful conceptual statements but have weak or generic Evidence Use descriptions. The urbanization slice relies on a generic UN-Habitat urban-planning landing page and its three Evidence Uses do not identify any passage. Official sources directly support the central concepts, but the current canonical evidence links are not sufficiently reproducible.

**All 9 Claims remain HOLD.** This is a source-passage desk review, not independent review and not a D/E/B scoring decision. No canonical records, scores, publication states, manifests or release artifacts were changed.

## 1. Migration Basics

Source in corpus: `SRC-MIGRATION_BASICS` — IOM, *World Migration Report*, generic portal URL. Three EUs repeat a generic statement that the source is used without going beyond the material.

### Claim A — definition and scope of migration
Claim: Migration includes movement from one place of residence to another, including across international borders.

Finding: **SUBSTANTIVE SUPPORT FOUND**. IOM's *World Migration Report 2024 Education Toolkit*, Module 1, defines migration as movement away from usual residence either across an international border or within a State, to a new residence. The resource also distinguishes internal and international migration. Exact passage appears in Module 1, “What is migration?” and “Internal and international migration”. Candidate source: https://publications.iom.int/system/files/pdf/pub2024-039-l-world-migration-education-toolkit_en.pdf

### Claim B — diverse reasons and forms
Claim: Reasons and forms may include economic, family, education, conflict and other circumstances.

Finding: **SUBSTANTIVE SUPPORT FOUND WITH QUALIFICATION**. IOM's same Module 1 describes varied motives and notes that not all international migration is connected to or caused by human insecurity; examples include opportunity, cultural experience and adventure, while ability to migrate is unequally distributed. The current wording is broad and acceptable as an illustrative list, but the EU must not imply that this is an exhaustive taxonomy or that every listed reason is equally represented.

### Claim C — migrant/refugee/displaced-person categories
Claim: Status is determined by applicable legal and factual circumstances; terms are not interchangeable.

Finding: **DIRECT SUPPORT FOUND**. UNHCR's refugee definition under the 1951 Convention identifies the legal criteria for refugee status. UNHCR explicitly distinguishes the legal term “refugee” from the broad term “migrant” and cautions against calling people who may be refugees or asylum-seekers migrants. Candidate sources:
- UNHCR, “What is a refugee?” https://www.unhcr.org/what-is-a-refugee
- UNHCR, “The 1951 Refugee Convention” https://www.unhcr.org/about-unhcr/overview/1951-refugee-convention

Required correction:
- Replace the generic IOM portal-only Source identity with exact publication-level source metadata if that toolkit is the evidence actually used.
- Add a separate UNHCR Source for Claim C.
- Rewrite each EU with the exact module heading/page or Convention article and a precise explanation of what is supported.
- Preserve the caveat that “migrant” does not have one universally agreed international legal definition.

## 2. Migration and Mobility Basics

Sources in corpus:
- `SRC-MIGRATION_AND_MOBILITY_BASICS`: “Migration and mobility — public reference”, pointing to an IOM educator toolkit resources page.
- `SRC-MIGRATION_AND_MOBILITY_BASICS-INDEPENDENT`: World Bank “Migration and Labor Mobility”.
- Three primary EUs and three purported independent EUs.

### Claim A — migration as movement from usual residence
Finding: **DIRECT SUPPORT FOUND**. IOM Module 1 gives a close definition: movement away from the place of usual residence, across an international border or within a State, to a new residence. Candidate passage: IOM Education Toolkit, Module 1. The source record currently points to a resources page rather than the exact module publication. The independent World Bank EU description does not identify a passage, so independence and substantive corroboration are not established by the record alone.

### Claim B — internal vs international migration
Finding: **DIRECT SUPPORT FOUND**. IOM Module 1 explicitly distinguishes the two types and describes movement within a country versus crossing state boundaries. The EU's conceptual limitation that this does not determine individual immigration/work rights is appropriate. The World Bank “independent” EU remains generic and needs an exact passage before it can count as independent corroboration.

### Claim C — comparability requires definitions, period, territory and flow/stock measurement
Finding: **DIRECT SUPPORT FOUND, WITH IMPORTANT PRECISION**. UN Statistics Division's revised *Recommendations on Statistics of International Migration and Temporary Mobility* distinguish migration from temporary mobility and require statistical alignment between migration and population measurement, including consistent duration criteria. UN DESA also explains that international migration flows refer to entries/exits during a specified period, while migrant stocks count migrants present at a point in time. ONS provides an accessible explanation that flows and stocks measure different things and cannot be directly compared. Candidate sources:
- UN Statistics Division, revised recommendations: https://unstats.un.org/unsd/demographic-social/sconcerns/migration/docs/Recommendations-on-Statistics-of-International-Migration-and-Temporary-Mobility_rev2.pdf
- UN DESA, *International Migration and Sustainable Development*: https://www.un.org/development/desa/pd/sites/www.un.org.development.desa.pd/files/undesa_pd_2024_megatrends3-international_migration.pdf
- ONS, migration flows and stocks: https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/internationalmigration/methodologies/longterminternationalmigrationfrequentlyaskedquestionsandbackgroundnotes

Required correction:
- The primary Source identity should name the IOM toolkit/publication, not “Migration and mobility — public reference”.
- Add a separate UN Statistics Division Source for Claim C.
- Add exact module and recommendations locators to all EUs.
- Treat the World Bank source as an independent source only after the exact relevant passage is located and shown to support the specific Claim. A different institution alone does not establish independent evidence.

## 3. Urbanization

Source in corpus: `SRC-URBANIZATION` — “UN-Habitat”, URI is a general urban-planning topic page. All three EUs repeat a generic description and provide no passage locator.

### Claim A — urbanization and urban settlement growth/transformation
Finding: **SUBSTANTIVE SUPPORT FOUND, BUT CLAIM NEEDS TERMINOLOGICAL CARE**. UN DESA's *World Urbanization Prospects 2025* provides estimates and projections of city, town and rural populations and documents global urban transformation from 1950 to 2050. Its Degree of Urbanization framework supports international comparison, but urbanization should not be reduced to “growth of cities” alone: the indicator depends on the classification and definition of urban areas. Candidate source: https://www.un.org/development/desa/pd/world-urbanization-prospects-2025

### Claim B — opportunities and challenges in housing, transport, services, infrastructure and spatial inequality
Finding: **DIRECT SUPPORT FOUND**. UN-Habitat's *World Cities Report 2026* documents housing affordability and supply gaps, inequality, infrastructure, and the need to coordinate housing with transport, basic services and spatial planning. UN SDG Goal 11 reporting identifies service access, public transport, slums/informal settlements and urban land-use pressures. Candidate sources:
- UN-Habitat, *World Cities Report 2026*: https://unhabitat.org/sites/default/files/2026/05/wcr_2026.pdf
- UN Statistics Division, SDG Goal 11 report: https://unstats.un.org/sdgs/report/2026/Goal-11

### Claim C — integrated spatial, social, economic and environmental urban planning
Finding: **DIRECT SUPPORT FOUND**. The UN-Habitat International Guidelines on Urban and Territorial Planning require attention to environmental and social resilience, and combine spatial, institutional and financial dimensions across time horizons and scales. UN-Habitat's strategic plan also describes integrated planning and infrastructure as central to sustainable development across social, economic and ecological dimensions. Candidate sources:
- UN-Habitat, *International Guidelines on Urban and Territorial Planning*: https://unhabitat.org/books/international-guidelines-on-urban-and-territorial-planning/
- UN-Habitat Strategic Plan 2026–2029: https://unhabitat.org/sites/default/files/2026/04/strategic_plan_2026-2029_en.pdf

Required correction:
- Replace the generic organization-only Source identity with publication-specific records for World Urbanization Prospects, World Cities Report and the planning guidelines.
- Split the evidence across distinct Sources rather than attributing every claim to a single UN-Habitat landing page.
- Pinpoint the exact report chapter/section/table or guideline principles supporting each claim.
- Clarify that urbanization metrics vary by national definition and by harmonized spatial definitions; do not conflate urban population growth, urban land expansion and rural-to-urban migration.

## Cross-slice structural and evidence findings

1. Nine of nine reviewed EUs have generic, incomplete or insufficiently reproducible passage descriptions.
2. Migration Basics has one broad IOM Source for three claims, including a legal-status claim that requires UNHCR legal material.
3. Migration and Mobility Basics has an IOM source with a generic identity and a World Bank “independent” source whose current EUs do not identify exact passages. Independence cannot be accepted solely because the institution differs.
4. Urbanization has a generic UN-Habitat Source used for claims that require distinct statistical, urban-development and planning sources.
5. The claims are broadly plausible, but source identity and evidence granularity are not adequate for release.

## Required correction sequence

1. Create publication-level Source records with exact title, issuing institution, year/edition, stable URL and access date.
2. Split Evidence Uses when a Claim depends on more than one distinct publication or legal instrument.
3. Add precise locators: module and page for IOM; Convention article and UNHCR explanatory section for refugee status; recommendations/paragraphs for migration statistics; report chapter/table or guideline principle for urbanization.
4. Recheck claim wording against the exact passages, especially “migrant” terminology, flow/stock distinctions and urbanization definitions.
5. Run schema, reference, duplicate-source, source-identity and release-conformance checks after authorized canonical edits.
6. Obtain independent reviewer assessments. This desk review does not create independent reviewer scores or establish evidence independence.
