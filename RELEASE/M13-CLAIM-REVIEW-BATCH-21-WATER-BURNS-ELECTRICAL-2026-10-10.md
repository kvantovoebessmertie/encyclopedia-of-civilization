## Method
Reviewed the listed Claim statements and their linked Evidence Use records from the frozen M13 worksheet. Checked EPA emergency water guidance, UN-Water’s working definition, American Red Cross first-aid guidance, and OSHA electrical-safety standards. D (explanatory depth), E (claim-specific evidence fit) and B (boundary discipline) are independently scored 0–3. These are desk-review findings; they do not constitute clinical, engineering, legal or operational certification.

## Findings
1. **Strong critical boundary:** the emergency-water claim clearly says that boiling/disinfection does not remove many chemical contaminants. This must remain visible beside any emergency water-disinfection instructions.
2. **Water-treatment claims are properly conditional:** they resist universal-method claims. The next practical-depth improvement is to connect treatment selection to contaminant identification and testing without giving unsupported one-size-fits-all recipes.
3. **Burn first aid needs careful operational boundaries:** the small-burn cooling claim is correct but under-specified for a reader trying to act. A future correction must preserve escalation criteria and avoid applying a small-burn procedure to severe, chemical or electrical injuries.
4. **Electrical safety is bounded but jurisdiction-sensitive:** OSHA-specific statements must remain explicitly tied to OSHA’s scope; general advice should not be misrepresented as a substitute for local electrical codes or qualified work practices.
5. No content records were changed. These 15 Claims remain in the fixed 1,494-Claim denominator. This batch does not close water, burn/first aid, electrical safety, high-consequence recall, Human View, Relations, independent review or M13.
