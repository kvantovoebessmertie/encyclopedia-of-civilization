## Findings
1. **Evidence-use specificity debt confirmed for three nuclear-foundation Claims.** The repeated generic sentence does not show what source material supports each proposition. This is an evidence-description issue; it does not, by itself, prove that the Claims are false.
2. **The water-limit Claim has direct support in current CDC guidance.** The linked Evidence Use is comparatively specific, but the content should be checked for integration with the broader emergency-water and radiological-emergency guidance.
3. **Risk-screen interpretation:** only four Claims were flagged in the nuclear/radiological domain in this worksheet. This count must not be interpreted as evidence that only four Claims are relevant to radiation emergencies; keyword screening can miss implicit or differently worded risk-bearing Claims.
4. **No content record was edited in this batch.** Any correction to Evidence Use must be separately authorized, narrowly scoped, and regression-tested.

## Source checks
- CDC, “How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency”: https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/ — states that boiling or disinfecting water containing fuel, toxic chemicals, or radioactive materials will not make it safe.
- IAEA, “Nuclear science”: https://www.iaea.org/topics/nuclear-science — broad overview; insufficient alone to substantiate every detailed proposition without identifying the relevant section.
- CDC, “Food, Drinking Water, and Medicine Safety in a Radiation Emergency”: https://www.cdc.gov/radiation-emergencies/safety/food-drinking-water-medicine-safety.html — additional practical radiation-emergency guidance relevant to the cross-domain integration review.
