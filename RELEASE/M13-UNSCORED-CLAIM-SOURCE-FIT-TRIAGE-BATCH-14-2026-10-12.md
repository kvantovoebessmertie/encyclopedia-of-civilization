## Cross-cutting findings and dispositions

1. **Confirmed metadata-quality defects requiring repair or verification:** HCI Source identity repeats a definition rather than identifying the linked NIST page; hydrology Source identity repeats a Claim-like definition rather than the USGS page title. These are Source metadata issues, not automatic proof that the linked content is wrong.
2. **Source identity/locator mismatch candidate:** OpenStax immunology Source title does not match the cited section title. Verify the actual intended section before assigning evidence-fit credit.
3. **High-consequence queues:** home fire/smoke safety, history of medicine, hydropower, process/industrial safety, and other flagged Claims require exact passage checks and the applicable specialist review.
4. **Generic Evidence Use descriptions dominate:** 82/100 Claims have at least one generic/near-generic description; 75/100 have only generic/near-generic descriptions. This is a locator/support-traceability debt signal, not a blanket failure verdict.
5. **Potential cross-slice duplicates/overlap:** industrial process safety vs. industrial safety; infrastructure basics vs. infrastructure resilience; human settlement systems vs. housing/infrastructure. Compare Claim semantics, scopes, and evidence before deciding to merge, narrow, or retain.
6. **Evidence independence is not established by publisher count.** ACRL/UNESCO and FEMA/NIST need claim-level comparison; different organizations and URLs are not enough to establish independent corroboration.
7. **Rights review remains separate from source fit.** In particular, OpenStax material must not be considered publishable until its applicable reuse terms and any AI-ingestion restrictions are explicitly resolved.
