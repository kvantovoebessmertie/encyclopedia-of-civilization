## 1. Developmental biology — 2 Claims

Canonical Source: OpenStax Biology 2e, Chapter 43, “Animal Reproduction and Development”.

### CLM-DEVELOPMENTAL_BIOLOGY_BASICS-A
Claim: “Development includes sequential changes in cells, tissues and the organism.”

Exact support:
- OpenStax Biology 2e §43.6 states that an organism develops from a single-celled zygote into a multicellular organism and describes cleavage, blastula formation and gastrulation.
- §43.7, “Organogenesis and Vertebrate Axis Formation”, explains that organs form from germ layers and that each germ layer gives rise to specific tissue types.
- OpenStax Biology 2e §1.2 explains that cells form tissues, tissues form organs and organs form organ systems.

Assessment: DIRECT support for the broad sequence, provided the Evidence Use links both the organismal development chapter and the cellular/tissue hierarchy. Chapter 43 alone is animal-development focused and should not be generalized to every organism without qualification.

Proposed locators: OpenStax Biology 2e §§1.2, 43.6 and 43.7.

### CLM-DEVELOPMENTAL_BIOLOGY_BASICS-B
Claim: “Cell differentiation leads to cells with different structures and functions.”

Exact support:
- OpenStax Biology 2e §43.7 describes the three germ layers and the specialized tissue types that develop from them, including nervous tissue, muscle/connective tissues and internal organs.
- OpenStax Biology 2e §27.1 describes specialized cells grouped into tissues with specialized functions.

Assessment: DIRECT support. Proposed locator: OpenStax Biology 2e §43.7, “Organogenesis and Vertebrate Axis Formation”, together with §27.1, “Complex Tissue Structure”.

## 2. Virology — 1 Claim

Canonical Source: OpenStax Biology 2e §21.1, “Viral Evolution, Morphology, and Classification”.

### CLM-VIROLOGY_BASICS-A
Claim: “Viruses do not have all characteristics of cellular organisms and depend on host cells for replication.”

Exact support:
- OpenStax Biology 2e §21.1 defines viruses as noncellular entities lacking cellular structure and most cellular components, including organelles, ribosomes and a plasma membrane.
- OpenStax Biology 2e §21.2, “Virus Infections and Hosts”, explicitly describes viruses as obligate intracellular parasites and explains that a virus must attach to and enter a living cell, copy its genome and produce proteins before progeny virions can escape.

Assessment: DIRECT support when both §21.1 and §21.2 are linked. The current canonical Source URL points only to §21.1, which does not fully support the host-cell replication component. Add §21.2 as a distinct Source/Evidence Use or a separately identified section locator. Do not infer that every virus infects animal cells; hosts include bacteria, archaea, fungi, plants and animals.

## 3. Historical method — 2 Claims

Canonical Source: American Historical Association, “History Discipline Core” / “AP Histories: Historical Thinking Skills”.

### CLM-HISTORY_METHODS_BASICS-A
Claim: “Historical research uses sources to reconstruct and interpret the past.”

Exact support:
- AHA History Discipline Core states that history is an interpretive account of the human past created in the present from surviving evidence.
- It identifies collecting, sifting, organizing, questioning, synthesizing and interpreting complex material as historical methods.
- AHA historical-thinking guidance distinguishes primary and secondary materials and calls for evaluation of credibility, position, perspective and relevance.

Assessment: DIRECT support. Proposed locator: AHA, “History Discipline Core”, “Develop historical methods” and “Understand the complex nature of the historical record”.

### CLM-HISTORY_METHODS_BASICS-C
Claim: “The temporal sequence of events alone does not prove a causal relationship.”

Exact support:
- AHA “AP Histories: Historical Thinking Skills” lists chronological reasoning and causation as distinct historical-thinking categories and requires historians to evaluate causal claims using evidence.
- AHA History Discipline Core says historical arguments should explain and justify multiple causes of complex events using conflicting sources and perspectives.
- The AHA framework supports the methodological boundary: chronology is relevant evidence for causal analysis but is not itself a complete causal argument.

Assessment: DIRECT support for the methodological distinction. Proposed locator: AHA, “AP Histories: Historical Thinking Skills”, “Chronological Reasoning” and “Causation”; AHA History Discipline Core, “Develop historical methods”.

## 4. Human rights — 1 Claim

Canonical Source: United Nations, Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR).

### CLM-HUMAN_RIGHTS_BASICS-B
Claim: “The Declaration formulates a general set of fundamental rights and freedoms which, according to its text, should belong to all people.”

Exact support:
- UDHR Article 1 states that all human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights.
- Article 2 states that everyone is entitled to the rights and freedoms set forth in the Declaration without distinction.
- Articles 3–30 enumerate specific rights and freedoms.

Assessment: DIRECT support as a description of what the Declaration says. Preserve the words “according to its text”: the Claim describes the Declaration's normative formulation, not a claim that every right is uniformly realized or enforceable in every jurisdiction. Proposed locator: UDHR, Preamble and Articles 1–2, with Articles 3–30 as the rights catalogue.

## 5. International law — 1 Claim

Canonical Source: United Nations Charter.

### CLM-INTERNATIONAL_LAW_BASICS-A
Claim: “International law regulates relations between States and other recognized subjects of international law in relevant fields.”

Exact support checked:
- The UN Charter is an international treaty and codifies principles of international relations, including sovereign equality of States and the prohibition on the use of force.
- The Charter binds UN Member States and establishes the International Court of Justice as the principal judicial organ of the UN.

Assessment: PARTIAL. The current Charter source directly supports the State-to-State and institutional framework, but it does not by itself define the full range of subjects of international law or support the phrase “other recognized subjects” across all fields. The category and legal capacity of international organizations, individuals and other entities vary by legal context and instrument. Add an authoritative international-law source on international legal personality and narrow the statement to the specific subjects and fields actually evidenced. Keep HOLD; do not treat the Charter landing page as a complete doctrinal source.

Proposed additional source: International Court of Justice, Reparation for Injuries Suffered in the Service of the United Nations, Advisory Opinion (1949), for the international legal personality of the United Nations; further doctrine is required before generalizing to all other subjects.

## 6. Ecophysiology — 3 Claims

Canonical Source: OpenStax Biology 2e. The currently linked URI, https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/44-animal-sensory-systems-and-movement, could not be retrieved as a valid OpenStax page in this pass. Do not rely on this URI until the canonical source identity and path are corrected.

### CLM-ECOPHYSIOLOGY_BASICS-A
Claim: “An organism's physiological processes depend on external conditions.”

Exact support:
- OpenStax Biology 2e §33.3, “Homeostasis”, states that animal organ systems adjust to internal and external changes, including external temperature.
- §§33.3–33.4 describe environmental temperature and other stimuli affecting physiological regulation.
- §44.2, “Biogeography”, explains that temperature affects organism physiology, enzyme activity and the conditions in which organisms can survive.

Assessment: DIRECT at the general level for animal physiology and temperature. Do not universalize the animal-specific homeostasis discussion to all organisms without additional sources.

Proposed locator: OpenStax Biology 2e §33.3, “Homeostatic Process” and “Homeostasis: Thermoregulation”; §44.2, “Temperature”.

### CLM-ECOPHYSIOLOGY_BASICS-B
Claim: “Organisms have ranges of conditions within which their physiological functions are maintained.”

Exact support:
- OpenStax Biology 2e §33.3 states that homeostasis maintains body functions within specific ranges and describes responses to internal and external stimuli.
- §44.2 explains that enzymes function efficiently within specific temperature ranges and that organisms must maintain or inhabit conditions that keep metabolism within supporting ranges.

Assessment: DIRECT for the physiological-range concept, but the present source support is strongest for animal homeostasis and temperature. Add a broader ecology/physiology source if the Claim is intended to apply to all organisms and multiple environmental variables.

Proposed locator: OpenStax Biology 2e §33.3, opening paragraphs and “Homeostasis: Thermoregulation”; §44.2, “Temperature”.

### CLM-ECOPHYSIOLOGY_BASICS-C
Claim: “Comparison of physiological responses requires comparable measurement conditions.”

Exact support checked:
- The current canonical Source page could not be retrieved.
- The OpenStax homeostasis material explains regulation in response to environmental stimuli, but does not directly establish a general measurement-design rule requiring comparable conditions for cross-organism or cross-study comparisons.

Assessment: NOT DIRECTLY SUPPORTED by the current canonical source. The proposition is methodologically plausible but requires an experimental-design or comparative-physiology source that explicitly addresses standardized measurement conditions, controlled variables, acclimation, temperature, hydration, activity state and measurement protocol. Keep HOLD and do not substitute a general homeostasis page for a methods source.
