## Slice findings

### 1. historical-geography-basics — HOLD

- Claims A–C are substantive and sensible: historical maps are time/place-bound spatial sources; maps may depict physical and cultural features; interpretation depends on date, scale, provenance and purpose.
- The Source is the Library of Congress Geography and Map collections landing page, a useful access point but not a specific map, collection item or methodological guide.
- All three Evidence Use records are generic and do not identify a particular map, map series, catalog record, date, scale or Rights Advisory.
- Context and Scope are generic and do not require a reader to distinguish the map's production purpose from the historical reality being inferred.

Required work: pin a concrete LoC map item or collection with exact date, title, scale, creator and catalog locator for each Claim. Add an explicit method boundary: a map is a constructed representation, not a transparent or neutral copy of territory. Check item-level Rights and Access before any image reproduction.

### 2. history-of-science-basics — HOLD

- Claims A–C are substantive at a high level: the field studies scientific domains, sources, ideas and practices over time; uses books and archival materials; and requires evidence appropriate to the period and causal question.
- The Smithsonian History of Science and Technology Research Guide is a strong research-navigation source. It lists catalogs, archival collections, instruments, primary sources and specialist projects.
- The guide is not itself a historiographical-methodology source and does not directly establish the broad definition in Claim A or the caution about causal/priority claims in Claim C.
- Evidence Use A–C all contain generic descriptions without a specific section, collection or linked primary-source locator.
- Context and Scope are boilerplate.

Required work: keep the Smithsonian guide as a discovery index, but cite the specific collection, archive, primary source or scholarly methodology text used for each proposition. Add an exact locator for the research-guide categories supporting Claim B; add historiography/methodology support for Claim C. Avoid treating a research guide as direct evidence for a particular historical claim.

### 3. history-of-technology-basics — HOLD

- Claims A–C are substantive: technological objects/practices/knowledge change over time; sources include technical, engineering and archival collections; and artifacts alone do not prove a single cause of invention or diffusion.
- The Source is the same Smithsonian guide used by the history-of-science slice. It contains a useful “History of Technology and Engineering” section and references trade literature, technical manuals, company catalogs, archives and engineering-history collections.
- This directly helps with Claim B, but does not independently establish a full definition of the field or the methodological proposition in Claim C.
- Evidence Use A–C are generic, with no section or linked collection locator. Context and Scope are boilerplate.

Required work: pin the Smithsonian guide's “History of Technology and Engineering” section and the exact linked collections relevant to Claim B. Add a specialist history-of-technology methodology source for A/C and distinguish invention, adoption, diffusion and social impact rather than treating them as one process.

### 4. religious-studies-basics — HOLD

- Claims A–C are substantive and maintain a useful descriptive/non-confessional boundary.
- The Harvard Pluralism Project explicitly studies and interprets religious diversity and interfaith relations in the United States and describes a changing U.S. religious landscape. This supports a U.S.-bounded example of religious diversity and historical/social study.
- The Source does not establish the full scope or method of religious studies as an academic field, and the page is explicitly U.S.-focused. Claims A and C are methodological propositions about not adjudicating truth claims; they need a dedicated academic-methodology source.
- Evidence Use A–C are generic and lack page/section locators.
- Context and Scope are boilerplate and do not make the U.S. case boundary visible.

Required work: either explicitly narrow the slice to a U.S. pluralism case study or add broader, non-confessional religious-studies methodology sources. Pin the Pluralism Project's “About” and “What is Pluralism?” sections for the relevant descriptive claims. Keep descriptive analysis separate from theological evaluation and avoid implying that all religious communities or traditions are internally uniform.

### 5. literature-basics — HOLD

- Claims A/B are substantive and well matched to the Library of Congress Literary Research Resource Guide: the guide covers fiction, poetry, drama, historical/theoretical/critical material, books, periodicals, manuscripts, digital resources, audio and film.
- Claim C distinguishes literary interpretation from external historical fact. This is a useful epistemic boundary but is not directly established by the LoC research guide.
- Evidence Use A–C are generic and do not identify the Introduction or the exact collection/search-guide sections.
- The Source is a research guide rather than a literary theory or historical-methodology source. Context and Scope are boilerplate.

Required work: pin the LoC guide Introduction for genres and collection formats; add a literary-studies or historiography methodology source for Claim C. Keep primary text interpretation, author biography and historical claims distinct and use item-level evidence for claims about a particular work.

### 6. cultural-anthropology-basics — HOLD

- Claims A/B are broadly supported by the American Anthropological Association's “What is Anthropology?” page: cultural anthropology is one of four fields; anthropology examines human experience across biological, historical, linguistic and social dimensions.
- Claim C is only partly supported. The AAA page says anthropologists observe communities from an insider's perspective, but it does not establish the additional methodological requirement to describe context and research limitations.
- Evidence Use A–C are generic and have no exact section locator. The Source identity is precise enough to identify the AAA page, but a single introductory page is not a full ethnographic-methods authority.
- Context and Scope are boilerplate and omit fieldwork ethics, positionality, consent and limitations.

Required work: pin the AAA description of the four fields and insider-perspective observation. Add authoritative ethnographic-methods and research-ethics sources for Claim C, including positionality, context, consent and limits of inference. Avoid treating one researcher's account as representative of an entire culture.

## Cross-slice findings

1. **Record shape:** all six slices contain the expected nine record roles, with current versioned references in the expected Source → Claim → Evidence Use structure and Context/Scope targeting Claim A.
2. **Evidence Use:** all 18 records lack precise page, section, item or archive locators. Generic references to an institution's guide do not establish support for a specific proposition.
3. **Source fit:** LoC and Smithsonian guides are useful discovery/index sources, but need exact catalog items or linked collections for direct support. Harvard Pluralism is explicitly U.S.-focused. AAA is a broad disciplinary introduction, not a complete ethnographic-methods standard.
4. **Methodological boundaries:** historical maps require provenance and purpose; science/technology histories require period-appropriate primary evidence and cautious causal claims; religious studies must remain non-confessional and context-sensitive; literary interpretation must be distinguished from external historical fact; cultural anthropology needs fieldwork ethics and positionality.
5. **Context/Scope:** all six pairs are generic and should be made subject-specific, with explicit temporal, geographic, disciplinary and methodological boundaries.
6. **Rights:** LoC map rights are item-specific; Smithsonian website content is CC0 only when specifically marked and otherwise subject to usage conditions; Harvard and AAA pages require item-level rights checks. No rights clearance is asserted.
7. **Selection:** all six slices remain HOLD. Existing `publication_status=published` values do not constitute first-edition approval.
