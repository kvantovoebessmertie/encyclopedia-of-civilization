## 2. Agriculture basics — 3 Claims

Existing Source: `SRC-AGRICULTURE_BASICS`, “FAO — Agriculture”, https://www.fao.org/agriculture/

### `CLM-AGRICULTURE_BASICS-A`

Claim: “Сельское хозяйство включает деятельность по производству растительной и животной продукции и управлению связанными ресурсами.”

Evidence inspected:
- FAO, Frequently asked questions on agrifood systems transformation, “Why use the term ‘agrifood system’ instead of ‘food system’?”, states that in the FAO Constitution “agriculture” and its derivatives include crop and livestock products, fisheries, marine products, aquaculture, forests and primary forestry products: https://www.fao.org/food-systems/faq/en?page=15
- FAO Strategic Framework, paragraph 62, frames the supply of food and other products from crop, livestock, fisheries and forestry sectors: https://www.fao.org/4/W9414E/W9414e00.htm

Disposition: DIRECT for the core crop/livestock production scope, with a scope note. The Claim wording does not explicitly enumerate fisheries, aquaculture and forestry; the FAO definition is broader than ordinary narrow crop-and-livestock usage. The phrase “management of associated resources” is not fully established by the existing Agriculture landing-page Source. If that phrase is intended as a substantive proposition, add a resource-management passage/source rather than treating the whole sentence as supported by the broad landing page.

The existing FAO Agriculture Source URL is unavailable (HTTP 404) and cannot presently support this Claim. Proposed replacement/augmentation Sources: FAO FAQ, the named definition question, paragraph beginning “Agrifood systems are broader than ‘food systems’…”; and FAO Strategic Framework, paragraph 69 on resource-use management in production systems. Capture exact canonical URLs, document versions/dates and locators at correction time. Do not silently overwrite the existing Source identity.

### `CLM-AGRICULTURE_BASICS-B`

Claim: “Производительность сельского хозяйства зависит от взаимодействия почвы, воды, биологических факторов, климата и способов управления.”

Evidence inspected:
- FAO Agro-Ecological Zoning and land evaluation describes crop suitability and production potential as dependent on crop ecophysiological traits and specific climatic and soil requirements, under defined input and management conditions: https://www.fao.org/geospatial/our-focus/agro-ecological-zoning---land-evaluation/
- FAO Agroecology Knowledge Hub, “Publications”, describes farms as ecosystems, biological interactions, and agroecological design/management beyond field scale: https://www.fao.org/agroecology/new-knowledge-module/publications/6/en
- FAO agrifood systems FAQ, “What are agrifood systems?”, identifies interaction with environmental systems including land and water and notes that connected components determine productivity and other outcomes: https://www.fao.org/food-systems/faq/en?page=15

Disposition: DIRECT at the general systems level, provided the three passages are represented as distinct sources and not collapsed into one generic FAO homepage citation. The existing FAO Agriculture Source URL returning 404 is not used as support. The AEZ passage directly covers soil, climate, crop ecophysiology, inputs and management; the agroecology/agrifood passages supply the biological and water-system context. This does not establish a universal quantitative model of agricultural productivity or the relative causal contribution of each factor.

Proposed locator: FAO AEZ page, paragraphs 2–3 (“identifies constraints and advantages…”; “specific climatic and soil requirements…”; “defined input and management conditions”); FAO Agroecology Knowledge Hub, paragraphs 1–2; FAO Agrifood Systems FAQ, “What are agrifood systems?”, paragraphs on interrelated systems and outcomes.

### `CLM-AGRICULTURE_BASICS-C`

Claim: “Сельскохозяйственная практика должна учитывать местные природные условия, поскольку результат одного способа ведения хозяйства не гарантируется в другом месте.”

Evidence inspected:
- FAO Agroecology Knowledge Hub states that management practices and technologies are location-specific and shaped by the social-ecological context: https://www.fao.org/agroecology/new-knowledge-module/publications/6/en
- FAO Family Farming Knowledge Platform, “Practices and techniques”, describes practices as local-specific methods and says that soil texture, water balance and plant health matter: https://www.fao.org/family-farming/practices-techniques/en
- FAO AEZ methodology identifies constraints and advantages by crop, soil and climate conditions and evaluates crop suitability under defined input and management conditions: https://www.fao.org/geospatial/our-focus/agro-ecological-zoning---land-evaluation/

Disposition: DIRECT. The source evidence supports context dependence and the need to adapt practices to local ecological conditions. It does not establish that every practice necessarily fails outside its original location; the Claim should retain its present cautious formulation (“result is not guaranteed”) and not be strengthened into an absolute rule.

Proposed locator: FAO Agroecology Knowledge Hub, “Publications”, paragraphs 3–4; FAO Family Farming Knowledge Platform, “Practices and techniques”, opening paragraphs; FAO AEZ page, paragraphs 2–3.

## 3. Agrifood systems basics — 3 Claims

Existing Source: `SRC-AGRIFOOD_SYSTEMS_BASICS`, “FAO — Agrifood Systems”, https://www.fao.org/food-systems/en

### `CLM-AGRIFOOD_SYSTEMS_BASICS-A`

Claim: “Агропродовольственная система включает людей, деятельность, инвестиции и решения, связанные с производством и доставкой продовольствия и сельскохозяйственных товаров.”

Evidence inspected: FAO’s Agrifood Systems homepage states that agrifood systems tie together “the people, activities, investments, and choices” involved in producing and delivering food and agricultural goods. It further includes non-food agricultural products such as forestry, feedstock, biomass for biofuels and fibres.

Disposition: DIRECT. The existing Source URL contains the matching definition in its page introduction. Proposed locator: FAO Agrifood Systems homepage, opening definition paragraph immediately below the page title.

### `CLM-AGRIFOOD_SYSTEMS_BASICS-B`

Claim: “Цепочка агропродовольственной системы может включать выращивание, сбор, переработку, упаковку, транспортировку, распределение, торговлю, приготовление, потребление и утилизацию.”

Evidence inspected: FAO’s Agrifood Systems homepage enumerates growing, harvesting, processing, packaging, transporting, distributing, trading, buying, preparing, eating and eventual disposal. The FAO FAQ gives a more explicit expanded sequence, including raised, cultured, fished, harvested, purchased, eaten, disposed of and reused.

Disposition: DIRECT. Proposed locator: FAO Agrifood Systems homepage, opening definition paragraph; supplementary locator: FAO FAQ, “What are agrifood systems?”, first paragraph. The existing homepage passage supports the full sequence in the Claim; the FAQ is a separate, more detailed Source candidate.

### `CLM-AGRIFOOD_SYSTEMS_BASICS-C`

Claim: “Анализ продовольствия как системы требует учитывать одновременно производственные, экономические, социальные и экологические связи.”

Evidence inspected: FAO FAQ states that agrifood systems interact with environmental (including land and water), economic, health and social systems; that their connected components and subsystems determine outcomes; and that these outcomes include productivity, diets and health, environmental sustainability, inclusion, economic growth, jobs and livelihoods. The same FAQ explains that a system is a set of interconnected components that interact dynamically and that changes in one part influence outcomes elsewhere.

Disposition: DIRECT, but not adequately pinpointed by the current homepage Source alone. Add the FAO FAQ as a separate Source record with its own Evidence Use; do not silently treat a different URL as already present in `SRC-AGRIFOOD_SYSTEMS_BASICS`.

Proposed locator: FAO FAQ, “What are agrifood systems?”, paragraphs 2–4; “Why should we think of food and agriculture as a system?”, opening paragraph.
