## Claim-level review

### `CLM-WATER-STORAGE-CONTAINER` — D2 / E3 / B2
**Statement:** CDC recommends food-grade containers for emergency water storage; such containers should not transfer toxic substances into the water.

**Linked records:** `EU-WATER-STORAGE-CONTAINER` → CDC; `EU-EPA-EMERGENCY-WATER-STORAGE` → EPA.

**External check:** CDC's [How to Create an Emergency Water Supply](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/how-to-create-and-store-an-emergency-water-supply.html) directly recommends FDA-approved food-grade storage containers and states that they will not transfer toxic substances into water. It separately warns not to reuse containers that held toxic chemicals such as bleach or pesticides. CDC's [Safe Water Storage](https://www.cdc.gov/global-water-sanitation-hygiene/about/about-safe-water-storage.html) gives similar guidance on container choice and avoiding toxic-chemical containers.

**Assessment:** The claim is accurately supported by the primary CDC source. The CDC-linked Evidence Use is claim-specific. The EPA Evidence Use is transparent that EPA is contextual corroboration about emergency water supply rather than direct support for the container-specific proposition; that is a positive practice, not a false claim of independent corroboration. The claim could be more operational if the user-facing flow also says not to reuse containers that held toxic chemicals and explains how to clean/sanitize an appropriate container before filling.

**Disposition:** No factual defect found in the claim. Human View should test whether a reader can choose a safe container and find cleaning/sanitizing steps without needing to infer them from this general proposition.

## Batch findings
1. This is a positive evidence-traceability example: one direct source and one explicitly contextual source with different evidentiary roles.
2. The proposition is supported by current CDC guidance; no source mismatch was identified.
3. A claim about container material alone does not provide the full procedure for cleaning, sanitizing, filling, labeling and storing water. The slice's operational completeness must be checked at the Human View level; do not infer missing coverage from this single claim.
4. This is one reviewed claim, not a full slice or corpus audit.

## Required follow-up
