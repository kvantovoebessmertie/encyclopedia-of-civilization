## 3. Education systems — A and B direct; C requires a bounded source/scope decision

### Claim A — right to education and lifelong learning

Current Claim: UNESCO describes education as a human right and lifelong learning as part of that right.

- Current Source page: https://www.unesco.org/en/right-education
- Direct locator: page heading “The right to education” and opening statement, “Every human being has the right to quality education and lifelong learning opportunities”; opening paragraph also states that education is a basic human right.
- Fit: direct. Preserve the distinction between the right to quality education and the provision of lifelong-learning opportunities; do not turn the wording into an unlimited legal entitlement to every form of learning.

### Claim B — the 4A framework

Current Claim: States have responsibilities concerning availability, accessibility, acceptability and adaptability of education.

- Exact UNESCO page: https://www.unesco.org/en/right-education/state-obligations-responsibilities
- Recommended locator: “Content of states’ obligations concerning the right to education”, including the paragraph defining the four essential features.
- Direct support: UNESCO expressly categorizes obligations related to the essential features of the right to education under availability, accessibility, acceptability and adaptability.
- Source handling: the existing root page is not the best locator. Either update the Source URI to the state-obligations page or create a distinct Source record for this exact page, according to the authorized source model.

### Claim C — cross-country differences in law, financing, governance, curriculum and delivery

Current Claim: education systems differ across countries in law, financing, governance, curriculum and delivery.

The current UNESCO “Right to education” landing page is **not sufficient** to support this broad comparative proposition. Three defensible paths are available:

**Option 1 — narrow to a single-source claim (recommended minimum change):**

Proposed statement for owner/editor review:

> UNESCO's education-system review methodology is tailored to each Member State's priorities and context and examines system performance, governance and financing, teacher policies, curriculum and pedagogy, and system coherence.

Source: UNESCO, “Education system reviews” — https://www.unesco.org/en/education-policies/reviews

Direct locator: page opening and “Areas commonly covered” list. The page says each review is tailored to Member State priorities/context and enumerates policy, governance, financing, teacher policy, curriculum/pedagogy and system coherence. This is a precise description of the UNESCO review framework, not a universal claim about how every national system differs.

**Option 2 — retain the broader comparative proposition but add multiple evidence chains:**

- Legal frameworks and national policies: UNESCO GEM Report, PEER country profiles — https://www.unesco.org/gem-report/en/peer. The profiles describe country-specific education laws, policies and programmes.
- Governance and delivery: OECD, *PISA 2022 Results, Volume II*, “Governing education systems” — https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-ii_a97db61c-en/full-report/component-13.html. The report compares responsibility for hiring, salaries, budgeting, admissions, discipline, learning materials and curriculum across education systems.
- Curriculum and law: OECD, *What Students Learn Matters: Towards a 21st Century Curriculum*, “How do countries compare?” — https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/what-students-learn-matters_d86d4d9a-en/full-report/how-do-countries-compare_910c7e9c.html. It documents country variation in curriculum reform and the laws/regulations specifying curricula.
- Financing: OECD, *Education at a Glance 2026*, “How are primary and lower secondary education financed?” — https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/education-at-a-glance-2026_b4968bbc-en/full-report/how-are-primary-and-lower-secondary-education-financed_f7fa7bec.html. It reports cross-country variation in public/private funding and spending.

This option requires additional Source and Evidence Use records or another explicitly authorized multi-source graph design. The current single UNESCO Source must not be represented as proving all five dimensions.

**Decision recommendation:** use Option 1 if the intent is to describe UNESCO's system-review methodology. Use Option 2 only if the editor wants to retain the general comparative claim and authorizes the extra source graph. No option is applied here.
