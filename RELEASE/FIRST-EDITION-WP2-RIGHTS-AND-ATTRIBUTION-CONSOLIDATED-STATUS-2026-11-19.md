## 2. Rights patterns found

### A. Explicitly restrictive or conditional source licences

- OpenStax textbooks: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; noncommercial and ShareAlike conditions apply to adapted expression. Current attribution notices also raise an AI-ingestion/training permission question that must be assessed against the actual use model.
- Cornell Legal Information Institute: CC BY-NC-SA 2.5 for its editorial content; commercial redistribution and compatibility with the still-unselected project licence remain unresolved.
- MacTutor History of Mathematics: CC BY-SA 4.0 for covered text; adaptation requires attribution and ShareAlike compatibility. Images are separately rights-screened.
- IOM publication materials: likely CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 IGO for relevant post-2021 publications; exact edition and adaptation/translation restrictions must be checked.
- OECD, World Bank and UN publication families: terms vary by exact publication, date, component, dataset and third-party material; a landing-page or institutional policy cannot substitute for the exact item licence.
- IEEE/SWEBOK, ASCE, IMF and several professional/institutional sources: no blanket open licence established for expressive text/figures; permissions or a documented legal determination may be required for reuse beyond independent factual paraphrase and citation.

### B. Government/public-domain sources — bounded, not blanket

NIST, NIH/NINDS, NLM, USGS, USACE and other government sources may have public-domain status for government-authored text in the relevant jurisdiction. The screens correctly preserve important boundaries:
- third-party images, maps, figures, contractor reports, datasets and collection items may have separate rights;
- U.S. public-domain status does not automatically settle every non-U.S. territorial question;
- agency names, seals and logos are not automatically cleared;
- attribution and non-endorsement statements remain necessary.

### C. Archive, heritage and community materials

Library of Congress, Smithsonian, UNESCO, IFLA, SAA, ICA and other archive/heritage sources require item-level rights checks. Community/ethnographic/heritage materials additionally require consent, privacy, confidentiality, donor/access restrictions, cultural protocols and review for sacred or restricted knowledge. Public access to an archive or portal is not permission to republish its contents.

### D. Current canonical content boundary

The screens report that the remediated records primarily use independently worded factual propositions and citations, not copied source paragraphs, figures, tables, images, scans or diagrams. That is a materially safer posture, but it is not a universal legal conclusion. Exact source terms, jurisdiction, intended distribution, database/compilation rights, and any future reuse of expressive content still require review.

## 5. Recommended first-edition operating baseline for owner consideration

These are recommendations, not adopted decisions:

- distribute only a narrowly allowlisted, version-pinned release candidate;
- include independently written factual summaries and citations only where source use has been screened;
- do not reproduce third-party text, images, tables, diagrams, maps or datasets without item-specific rights evidence;
- keep code licensing separate from content/data licensing;
- use local-only contribution export and collect no personal contact data in canonical records;
- require one named accountable editor/release owner and a separate release approval;
- retain HOLD for any item with unresolved rights, attribution, source specificity, high-consequence review or Human View.

The project owner must explicitly adopt or replace these recommendations.
