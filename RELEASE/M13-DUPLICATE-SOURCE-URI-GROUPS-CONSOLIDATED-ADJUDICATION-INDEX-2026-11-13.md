## 3. Cross-group conclusions

1. **A shared URL is not proof of independent evidence.** Multiple Source IDs that resolve to the same page/document count as one source identity for corroboration.
2. **A source page is not necessarily the underlying publication.** This is material for NIST glossaries, NCBI Bookshelf, Smithsonian research guides, FAO/UNESCO topic pages and OSHA/EPA landing pages.
3. **URL equality can mask wrong source metadata.** OpenStax behavioral biology and biodiversity records point to sections that do not match their Source identities; NASA/communication locators also need precise chapter/page identification.
4. **Some duplicate groups contain real independent sources elsewhere in the Claim chain.** Examples include NIST/FEMA infrastructure resilience, EPA/USGS water resources Claim A, and USGS/FEMA corroboration of Red Cross earthquake guidance. Count the distinct source publications, not the repeated IDs.
5. **Generic Evidence Use text is not a pinpoint.** Most reviewed EUs need exact section, page, paragraph, term entry, standard clause or publication locator.
6. **Some linked Claims are not subject-matter Claims.** Generic slice descriptions, evidence-method rules and professional-scope cautions should not be treated as ordinary factual Claims in the relevant scientific topic slices.
7. **Some current source URLs are unavailable or blocked.** NIST Building Science and USGS GIS returned 404; FEMA infrastructure returned 403; Smithsonian retrieval returned an error page; NCBI sequence-analysis pages triggered reCAPTCHA. These remain verification limitations, not proof that replacement content is identical.
8. **Rights and jurisdiction are part of evidence fitness.** OpenStax Biology 2e carries CC BY-NC-SA conditions and an LLM-training restriction; OSHA is US-jurisdictional; ILO global guidance does not replace national entitlement law.
