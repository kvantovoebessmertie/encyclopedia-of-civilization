## 2. Claim-by-Claim source-fit findings

### 2.1 Information Literacy D — authority, context, information creation and perspectives

**Claim:** “Оценка информации зависит не только от формального авторитета источника, но и от контекста информационной потребности, способа создания информации и множества перспектив.”

**Registered source:** ACRL Framework for Information Literacy for Higher Education — https://www.ala.org/acrl/standards/ilframework

**Finding: DIRECT CONCEPTUAL SUPPORT.** The ACRL Framework's “Authority Is Constructed and Contextual” frame says resources are evaluated in relation to the information need and context of use. “Information Creation as a Process” requires examining how information came to be and its capabilities/constraints. “Scholarship as Conversation” calls for seeking multiple perspectives. Together these directly support all elements of the Claim.

**Follow-up:** Add three exact EU locators to the corresponding ACRL frames. Do not present ACRL as a universal professional standard for every domain; its declared context is higher education, though the conceptual ideas can be applied more broadly with qualification.

### 2.2 Information Retrieval A — document collections, queries and relevance

**Claim:** “Информационный поиск занимается представлением коллекций документов, обработкой запросов и нахождением релевантной информации.”

**Registered source:** Stanford *Introduction to Information Retrieval* — https://nlp.stanford.edu/IR-book/

**Finding: DIRECT SUPPORT.** The book's opening chapters define information retrieval around document collections, query processing and retrieval of relevant documents. Chapter 1 introduces Boolean retrieval and the basic IR problem; Chapters 2–4 cover term vocabularies, postings, dictionaries and index construction.

**Follow-up:** Add Chapter 1 and the relevant table-of-contents/chapter references. Keep the Claim at the conceptual level; do not imply that all retrieval systems use the same architecture.

### 2.3 Information Retrieval B — indexing and efficient query processing

**Claim:** “Индексирование позволяет организовать документы и термины так, чтобы запросы можно было обрабатывать эффективнее, чем последовательным просмотром всей коллекции.”

**Finding: DIRECT SUPPORT.** Stanford Chapters 2–4 explain term vocabularies, postings lists, dictionaries and index construction. These structures permit retrieval without scanning every document in full for every query. The book's index-construction chapter is the direct technical support.

**Follow-up:** Add Chapter 4, “Index construction,” and the Chapter 2 postings-list discussion as pinpoints. The comparison with a sequential scan is a conceptual performance explanation; avoid claiming a fixed speedup independent of data and implementation.

### 2.4 Information Retrieval C — relevance must be evaluated against the user's information need

**Claim:** “Оценка системы информационного поиска должна учитывать качество результатов относительно задачи пользователя; наличие индекса само по себе не доказывает релевантность найденных документов.”

**Finding: DIRECT SUPPORT.** Stanford Chapter 8 states that relevance is assessed relative to an information need, not merely the query terms, and describes evaluation using relevance judgments, precision and recall. This directly supports the Claim's distinction between an index existing and the results actually satisfying the user's need.

**Follow-up:** Add Chapter 8.1–8.3 pinpoints. Preserve the distinction between retrieval efficiency and retrieval effectiveness.

### 2.5 Information Security Operations A — preparation, detection, response and recovery

**Claim:** “Операционная безопасность включает подготовку, обнаружение, реагирование и восстановление.”

**Registered source:** NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 — https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/61/r3/final

**Finding: PARTIAL / TERMINOLOGY SCOPE.** NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 (2025) supports preparation for incident response and improving detection, response and recovery activities. NIST explicitly explains that Govern, Identify and Protect are broader preparation/risk-management activities, while Detect, Respond and Recover form the incident-response lifecycle. The Claim's phrase “операционная безопасность” is broader and less precise than cybersecurity incident response.

**Follow-up:** Narrow the Claim to cybersecurity incident preparedness/response, or provide a separate operational-security framework if the wider term is intentional. Cite NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 and its incident-response lifecycle explanation. Do not silently reuse the older Rev. 2 lifecycle as if it were the current model.
