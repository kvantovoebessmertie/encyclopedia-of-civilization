# M13 high-consequence evidence-use review — water, food and sanitation (12 Claims)

Date: 2026-11-12
Source audit revision: 2db3995b50c3b26179a341612c3aa1f3438551d1
Branch: m13-system-wide-depth-audit-2026-10-09
Scope: 12 high-consequence Claims flagged because at least one linked Evidence Use record has a generic/template-like material description. This is a source-passage review and locator proposal, not a new D/E/B scoring batch, specialist approval or release clearance.

## 1. Method and rule

For each Claim, the audit worksheet was used to identify every linked Evidence Use and Source record. Public official source pages, reports and transcripts were checked for exact supporting passages. The generic-EU flag applies to the stored Evidence Use description, not automatically to every Evidence Use linked to the Claim.

The canonical Evidence Use records do not preserve reproducible page/section/table/figure locators for the generic items. Exact locators are therefore proposed below for controlled editorial remediation. E must not be raised merely because a relevant source passage has now been found in this desk review. No canonical record was changed.

## 2. Claim-by-Claim findings

### 2.1 Environmental health

**Claim:** `CLM-ENVIRONMENTAL_HEALTH_BASICS-A`

**Evidence Use:** `EU-ENVIRONMENTAL_HEALTH_BASICS-A` → `SRC-ENVIRONMENTAL_HEALTH_BASICS` (WHO, Environmental health).

**Exact support:** WHO's page, section “Overview”, states that good health depends on clean air, a stable climate, preserved natural environment, access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene, and protection from harmful radiation and unsafe chemical management. It also describes indirect effects through food production, migration, economic instability and social inequalities.

**Assessment:** Direct thematic support for the full list in the Claim. The source is a broad topic overview, not a detailed exposure-assessment or clinical standard.

**Proposed locator:** WHO, “Environmental health”, “Overview”, paragraph 1 (air, climate, water, sanitation, hygiene, radiation and chemical management).

**Disposition:** HOLD until this locator is recorded in EU and the generic description is replaced by a claim-specific description.

### 2.2 Food preservation — microbiological hazards

**Claim:** `CLM-FOOD_PRESERVATION_BASICS-A`

**Evidence Use:** `EU-FOOD_PRESERVATION_BASICS-A` → `SRC-FOOD_PRESERVATION_BASICS` (USDA FSIS, Food Safety Basics).

**Exact support checked:** The linked USDA FSIS page was not retrievable during this pass (HTTP 403). FDA's “Food Safety at Home”, section “4 basic steps for food safety”, describes clean/separate/cook/chill; it states that germs can spread between foods and that chilling slows the growth of harmful bacteria. This is an official corroborating source, but it is not the canonical USDA Source record.

**Assessment:** The proposition is well supported by the FDA page at the general level, but the canonical USDA page could not be directly checked. Do not silently substitute the FDA page for the USDA Source.

**Proposed locator:** USDA FSIS “Food Safety Basics” exact subsection/paragraph after retrieval. Interim corroboration: FDA, “Food Safety at Home”, “4 basic steps for food safety”, paragraphs 1–4.

**Disposition:** HOLD pending direct verification of the canonical USDA page or authorized addition of the FDA source as a separate Source/Evidence Use.

### 2.3 Food preservation — handling, storage, time and temperature

**Claim:** `CLM-FOOD_PRESERVATION_BASICS-B`

**Evidence Uses:**
- `EU-FOOD_PRESERVATION_BASICS-B` → `SRC-FOOD_PRESERVATION_BASICS` (USDA FSIS).
- `EU_FOOD_PRESERVATION_BASICS_FDA` → `SRC_FDA_FOOD_STORAGE_SAFETY` (FDA, Are You Storing Food Safely?).

**Exact support:** FDA's “Are You Storing Food Safely?”, section “Storage Basics”, states that perishables should be refrigerated/frozen promptly, gives the two-hour rule (one hour above 90°F), sets refrigerator/freezer temperatures, instructs users to check storage directions on labels, and explains that ready-to-eat foods and different food types have distinct storage considerations. FDA's “Food Safety at Home”, “Cook” and “Chill” sections, provides handling, cooking-temperature and refrigeration steps.

**Assessment:** Direct support for time/temperature and product-specific storage conditions. The FDA evidence item says it “independently corroborates” the proposition, but its own precise locator is absent from canonical EU. The USDA Source was not directly retrievable in this pass.

**Proposed locators:** FDA “Are You Storing Food Safely?”, “Storage Basics” (refrigeration timing, temperature, label instructions and ready-to-eat foods); FDA “Food Safety at Home”, “Cook” and “Chill”.

**Disposition:** HOLD until locators are recorded and the canonical USDA source is independently checked.

### 2.4 Food preservation — scope boundary

**Claim:** `CLM-FOOD_PRESERVATION_BASICS-C`

**Evidence Use:** `EU-FOOD_PRESERVATION_BASICS-C` → `SRC-FOOD_PRESERVATION_BASICS` (USDA FSIS).

**Exact support checked:** FDA “Are You Storing Food Safely?”, “Storage Basics”, explicitly instructs users to check storage directions on labels and distinguishes infant formula as an exception with a use-by date. This supports product-specific handling/storage requirements. The USDA FSIS source page itself was not retrievable.

**Assessment:** Partial support. The source supports product-specific storage directions, but does not by itself establish every method-specific preservation requirement or all applicable regulatory standards. Keep the Claim's boundary, but narrow the evidence description to the actual FDA label/storage passages and obtain direct USDA/source-specific support.

**Proposed locator:** FDA “Are You Storing Food Safely?”, “Storage Basics”, bullet “Check storage directions on labels”; separate specific source for method-specific preservation requirements if the Claim is intended to cover canning, drying, fermentation or other preservation methods.

**Disposition:** HOLD.

### 2.8 Wastewater — treatment and risk reduction

**Claim:** `CLM-WASTEWATER_BASICS-B`

**Evidence Uses:**
- `EU-WASTEWATER_BASICS-B` → `SRC-WASTEWATER_BASICS` (U.S. EPA, Wastewater 101: The Fundamentals).
- `EU-WHO-WASTEWATER_BASICS-B` → `SRC-WHO-WASTEWATER-SAFE-USE-2006` (WHO Guidelines for the safe use of wastewater, excreta and greywater).

**Exact support:**
- EPA “Wastewater 101” transcript: pp. 2–3, lines 43–50 and 101–112, describes removing undesirable constituents and pathogens from wastewater; pp. 4–5, lines 146–166, describes separation and biological degradation; pp. 8–10, lines 323–340, describes preliminary/primary treatment and solids separation.
- WHO's “Safe use of wastewater, greywater and excreta” page states that the Guidelines use risk assessment and risk management linked to health-based targets under local conditions and backed by strict monitoring. Volumes 2–4 address microbial hazards, vulnerable groups and health-based targets.

**Assessment:** Direct support for treatment as a means of removing undesirable constituents and reducing public/environmental-health risk. Scope note: EPA webinar is focused on domestic/decentralized wastewater and explicitly distinguishes industrial wastewater; WHO Guidelines focus on safe use in agriculture/aquaculture and associated health risk, not every municipal treatment design.

**Proposed locators:** EPA transcript, pp. 2–3, 4–5 and 8–10; WHO Guidelines landing page, “Safe use of wastewater, greywater and excreta”, paragraph 1 and relevant volume-specific overview.

**Disposition:** HOLD until the two EU records receive separate claim-specific locators and the Claim's scope is checked against domestic/industrial and use/disposal boundaries.

**Exact support:**

**Assessment:** Direct support for dependence on wastewater type, intended use and local risk-management conditions. The phrase “local regulatory conditions” should be linked to WHO Volume 1, policy and regulatory aspects, not left attached to the generic EPA description.

**Proposed locator:** EPA transcript, pp. 1–2 and pp. 8–10; WHO Guidelines, Volume 1, policy and regulatory aspects; relevant technical volume for the intended wastewater stream/use.

**Disposition:** HOLD.

### 2.11 Drinking-water distribution — contamination barrier and operation

**Claim:** `CLM-WATER_INFRASTRUCTURE_BASICS-B`

**Evidence Uses:**
- `EU-EPA-WATER-INFRASTRUCTURE-B` → `SRC-EPA-WATER-INFRASTRUCTURE-RESILIENCE` (EPA, Drinking Water Infrastructure Resilience and Sustainability Research).
- `EU-WATER_INFRASTRUCTURE_BASICS-B` → `SRC-WATER_INFRASTRUCTURE_BASICS` (EPA, Drinking Water Distribution System Tools and Resources).

**Exact support:**
- EPA distribution-systems page, opening section, calls distribution systems the final physical barrier against contamination and says they must be operated and maintained to reduce external and internal contamination risks.
- EPA infrastructure-resilience page, section “Drinking Water Distribution Systems”, describes contaminant accumulation in storage tanks and pipe walls, biofilm growth, corrosion, leaks and the resulting difficulty maintaining disinfectant residuals and water quality from treatment plant to tap.

**Assessment:** Direct support for the core Claim. The two sources are distinct EPA pages from the same agency; they provide complementary evidence, but should not automatically be treated as institutionally independent corroboration.

**Proposed locators:** EPA distribution-systems page, opening section; EPA infrastructure-resilience page, “Drinking Water Distribution Systems” and “Maintaining Water Quality and Building Distribution System Reliability”.

**Disposition:** HOLD until exact locators are recorded in both EUs.

### 2.12 Drinking-water distribution — operational variables and jurisdiction

**Claim:** `CLM-WATER_INFRASTRUCTURE_BASICS-C`

**Evidence Use:** `EU-WATER_INFRASTRUCTURE_BASICS-C` → `SRC-WATER_INFRASTRUCTURE_BASICS` (U.S. EPA).

**Exact support:** EPA distribution-systems page explicitly lists sections on water age, pressure management, storage facility management, corrosion/permeation/leaching, cross-connections and backflow. The page explains how water age can affect biofilm/pathogen growth and disinfectant residual; how pressure management supports water quality; and how corrosion can release metals into the water.

**Assessment:** Direct support for the listed technical factors. The final phrase about requirements depending on system and jurisdiction is a valid boundary but requires a separate, jurisdiction-specific regulatory source if intended as a factual description of legal requirements.

**Proposed locator:** EPA “Drinking Water Distribution System Tools and Resources”, sections “Water Age”, “Pressure Management”, “Storage Facility Management”, “Corrosion, Permeation and Leaching”, and “Cross-Connections and Backflow”.

**Disposition:** HOLD.
