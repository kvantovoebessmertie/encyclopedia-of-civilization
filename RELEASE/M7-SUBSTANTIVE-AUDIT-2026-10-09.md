# M7 Substantive Claim Audit — 2026-10-09

## Status

**PASS AFTER CONTROLLED CORRECTIONS — M7 remains OPEN.** This audit reviews the four scope-locked M7 slices claim by claim. It is not CLEAN acceptance and does not replace exact-head CI or independent verification.

## Method and boundary

Reviewed all 16 Claims, claim-linked Evidence Use descriptions, source identities/locators, Context and Scope records, and claim wording against the source-fit limitations recorded during authoring. The audit prefers narrow, source-bounded statements over unsupported generalizations. No new Record type, fixed content quota, or Relation was introduced.

## Claim-level findings

### woodworking-basics

| Claim | Disposition | Rationale / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| A — moisture and dimensional change | PASS | USDA Forest Products Laboratory Wood Handbook covers moisture, drying and dimensional change. The claim says moisture affects dimensions and does not prescribe a universal shrinkage value. |
| B — species, grain, defects and mechanical properties | PASS WITH BOUNDARY | The Wood Handbook is an appropriate technical source for variability in wood properties. The claim explicitly says visual inspection does not replace design of a loaded component; this is not structural engineering advice. |
| C — distinct craft operations and tool selection | PASS | Illinois 4-H introductory woodworking material supports a bounded overview of measurement, marking, cutting, shaping and joining. The statement does not prescribe machine settings or imply one tool suits every material. |
| D — wood-dust exposure | PASS AFTER CORRECTION | Claim was narrowed from a generic tool-risk statement to wood dust and linked to OSHA hazard guidance. Exposure controls remain subject to the actual process and applicable requirements. |

### papermaking-basics

| Claim | Disposition | Rationale / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| A — fibre mass, sheet formation, pressing and drying | PASS | MFA handmade-paper material and the Deutsches Technikmuseum overview support the broad hand-process sequence. The claim does not generalize to industrial process parameters. |
| B — preparation and fibre variation | PASS | Museum process descriptions support the relationship between fibre preparation and sheet formation. No universal pulp formula is asserted. |
| C — distinct functions of forming, pressing and drying | PASS | Both museum sources describe the stages as distinct parts of the process. No quantitative settings are inferred. |
| D — limits on suitability claims | PASS AS AN EPISTEMIC BOUNDARY | The source set does not establish archival permanence, food-contact suitability or compliance for a particular product. The claim warns against inferring those properties from sheet formation alone; it does not declare all handmade paper unsuitable. |

### ceramics-basics

| Claim | Disposition | Rationale / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| A — respirable crystalline silica in dry-clay dust | PASS | WCSU and Princeton EHS materials provide complementary ceramic-workplace hazard coverage. No universal exposure limit is inferred. |
| B — possible hazardous glaze constituents | PASS | University EHS sources support checking the actual glaze composition and safety documentation. The claim does not imply that every glaze has the same composition. |
| C — firing heat, emissions and ventilation | PASS | University EHS sources support treating kiln heat and emissions as process-specific hazards. No universal kiln design or ventilation plan is provided. |
| D — firing does not itself establish food-contact safety | PASS | Princeton EHS and FDA foodware material support the leaching/food-contact boundary. Suitability depends on the particular ware and applicable requirements; the claim is not a certification. |

### textile-fibre-processing-basics

| Claim | Disposition | Rationale / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| A — plant fibres need preparation before spinning | PASS WITH SCOPE LIMIT | FAO's coir material is a specific fibre example; The Met provides broader plant-fibre context. The claim explicitly says operations depend on the fibre and condition of raw material. |
| B — preparation may include alignment/combing | PASS AFTER CORRECTION | Wording was narrowed to say that plant fibres may undergo preparation, including alignment or combing, with operations varying by fibre. It no longer asserts an unverified uniformity mechanism. |
| C — yarn formation and later processing | PASS AFTER CORRECTION | Wording was narrowed to the process scope described by The Met: joining/twisting fibres to form yarn and the influence of spinning/later processing on yarn properties. The bibliographic-only FAO AGRIS record is not used as proof of detailed parameters. |
| D — fibre properties and product suitability | PASS AFTER CORRECTION | Wording was narrowed from a categorical non-interchangeability claim to the source-bounded point that plant fibres and their processing differ, and yarn suitability should be assessed against the intended product's requirements. |

## Cross-cutting result

- Four claims in the textile slice were tightened to match the accessible source scope; no quantitative process settings were added.
- Ceramic hazard claims remain warnings and boundaries, not kiln recipes, glaze formulations or ventilation designs.
- Paper-process claims do not assert archival permanence or food-contact safety.
- Woodworking claims distinguish craft knowledge from structural design and occupational exposure limits.
- The claims are introductory summaries, not substitutes for local standards, professional training, safety data sheets or product-specific testing.

## Remaining limitations

This is a desk-based content audit. It does not claim empirical testing with novice readers, certification of a product, or validation of every process variant worldwide. Those limits are reflected in the slices' Scope/Context and READMEs.
