## 2. Foundation engineering — 3 Claims

Canonical Source: FHWA, Geotechnical Engineering Circular No. 6, Shallow Foundations (FHWA-SA-02-054, September 2002).

- CLM-FOUNDATION_ENGINEERING_BASICS-A — supported by the manual's description of shallow foundations as structural support systems and its treatment of bearing capacity, settlement, eccentricity, overturning, sliding and global stability. Proposed locator: Chapters 1–2 and 5–6.
- CLM-FOUNDATION_ENGINEERING_BASICS-B — supported by Chapters 3–4, which cover foundation-type selection, subsurface investigation and laboratory testing, and Chapter 5, which provides soil-mechanics theory and design methods.
- CLM-FOUNDATION_ENGINEERING_BASICS-C — supported by Chapters 5–6 and the worked design examples in Appendices B–C, which show that design requires engineering calculations under specified load/design methods.

Disposition: DIRECT at the foundational shallow-foundation level. Important boundary: the 2002 circular is a technical reference, not a current building code or universal foundation design standard. It focuses on highway structures and shallow foundations; deep foundations and non-transportation applications need their own current standards and geotechnical review.

## 3. Hydraulic engineering — 3 Claims

Canonical Source: USACE Hydrology and Hydraulics page. The canonical page URI was not retrievable during this pass; the current USACE Hydrologic Engineering Center manuals provide more precise replacement/augmentation sources.

- CLM-HYDRAULIC_ENGINEERING-A — the distinction is supported by complementary USACE tools: HEC-HMS models watershed processes, precipitation, infiltration and runoff; HEC-RAS performs hydraulic calculations for steady/unsteady flow in natural and constructed channels. Proposed locators: HEC-HMS Technical Reference Manual §§3.1–3.2, 5.2, 7–9; HEC-RAS Hydraulic Reference Manual, Introduction and hydraulic capabilities.
- CLM-HYDRAULIC_ENGINEERING-B — directly supported by HEC-RAS documentation describing hydraulic calculations for channels, bridges, culverts, weirs, spillways and other structures. Proposed locator: HEC-RAS Hydraulic Reference Manual, Introduction, “Overview of Hydraulic Capabilities”, bridge/culvert/structure modelling chapters.
- CLM-HYDRAULIC_ENGINEERING-C — directly supported by USACE HEC-RAS documentation describing floodplain encroachment/floodway analysis, bridge scour, channel modification, levees and dam-break analysis. Proposed locator: HEC-RAS Hydraulic Reference Manual sections “Floodplain Encroachment Calculations”, “Estimating Scour at Bridges”, “Stable Channel Design Functions” and “Performing a Dam Break Study”.

Disposition: DIRECT with the HEC-HMS and HEC-RAS manuals added as distinct Sources. Preserve the boundary that model results depend on site-specific geometry, hydrologic inputs, calibration, boundary conditions, uncertainty and applicable engineering criteria. They are not a substitute for a qualified hydraulic engineer's analysis.

## 4. Industrial process safety — 2 Claims

Canonical Source: U.S. OSHA, Process Safety Management of Highly Hazardous Chemicals, 29 CFR 1910.119.

- CLM-INDUSTRIAL_SAFETY_BASICS-A — directly supported by the standard's process-safety management framework for covered processes involving highly hazardous chemicals.
- CLM-INDUSTRIAL_SAFETY_BASICS-B — directly supported by the standard's integrated requirements for process safety information, process hazard analysis, operating procedures, employee training, mechanical integrity, management of change, incident investigation, emergency planning and compliance audits.

Proposed locators: 29 CFR 1910.119(c)–(o), especially (d) Process Safety Information, (e) Process Hazard Analysis, (f) Operating Procedures, (g) Training, (j) Mechanical Integrity, (l) Management of Change, (m) Incident Investigation, (n) Emergency Planning and Response, and (o) Compliance Audits.

Disposition: DIRECT within the U.S. OSHA PSM scope. Do not present this standard as a complete or universal industrial-safety framework; it applies to covered processes and has defined thresholds and exclusions. Other jurisdictions require their own law and regulator guidance.

## 5. Information security operations — 3 Claims

Canonical Source: NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3, Incident Response Recommendations and Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management (April 2025).

- CLM-INFORMATION_SECURITY_OPERATIONS_BASICS-A — directly supported by the NIST incident-response lifecycle and its integration with cybersecurity risk management.
- CLM-INFORMATION_SECURITY_OPERATIONS_BASICS-B — directly supported by NIST's treatment of preparation, detection, response and recovery activities, including integrating incident response across the cybersecurity risk-management functions.
- CLM-INFORMATION_SECURITY_OPERATIONS_BASICS-C — directly supported by NIST's guidance on planning, coordination, incident-response policies and the roles/responsibilities of organizational stakeholders.

Proposed locators: SP 800-61 Rev. 3, Executive Summary; §§2.1–2.4 and the relevant incident-response policy, preparation, detection, response and recovery recommendations.

Disposition: DIRECT for cybersecurity incident-response operations. The claim wording should not imply that incident response exhausts all information-security operations. Preserve organization size, mission, technology, threat and regulatory context; the NIST publication is guidance, not a substitute for a tailored security program.

## 6. Energy storage — 3 Claims

Canonical Source: U.S. Department of Energy, Energy Storage Basics.

- CLM-ENERGY_STORAGE_BASICS-A — directly supported by DOE's explanation that energy storage captures energy, stores it in another form and releases it when needed.
- CLM-ENERGY_STORAGE_BASICS-B — directly supported by DOE's distinction between power capacity and energy capacity/duration and its description of storage performance.
- CLM-ENERGY_STORAGE_BASICS-C — partially supported. DOE describes technology-specific characteristics, efficiency, duration, use cases and constraints, but the current overview does not fully substantiate all claimed operational management requirements.

Proposed locators: DOE Energy Storage Basics, “What Is Energy Storage?”, technology overview and sections on power capacity, energy capacity/duration, efficiency and applications. For the management requirements in Claim C, add technology-specific NREL/DOE technical guidance and applicable electrical/fire/building codes.

Disposition: A/B DIRECT; C PARTIAL. Keep all three HOLD until the C source gap is addressed and exact locators are added. No installation, sizing, fire-safety or grid-interconnection advice should be inferred from the overview.

## 7. Indoor air quality — 3 Claims

Canonical Source: U.S. EPA, Indoor Air Quality.

- CLM-INDOOR_AIR_QUALITY_BASICS-A — directly supported by EPA's description of indoor pollutant sources and factors affecting indoor air quality.
- CLM-INDOOR_AIR_QUALITY_BASICS-B — directly supported by EPA's explanation that inadequate ventilation can increase indoor pollutant concentrations and that ventilation can reduce them.
- CLM-INDOOR_AIR_QUALITY_BASICS-C — directly supported at a general level by EPA's guidance that assessment and improvement depend on identifying sources, building conditions and ventilation.

Proposed locators: EPA Indoor Air Quality overview, “Sources of Indoor Air Pollution”, “Ventilation” and “Improving Indoor Air Quality”.

Disposition: DIRECT at general public-information level. Do not infer medical diagnosis, safe exposure thresholds, clearance after contamination, or a universal remediation procedure from this overview. Use contaminant-specific standards and qualified professionals where necessary.

## 8. Disaster risk reduction — 2 Claims

Canonical Source: UNDRR, Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction 2015–2030.

- CLM-DISASTER_RISK_REDUCTION_BASICS-B — directly supported by the Framework's emphasis on understanding disaster risk, including hazard, exposure, vulnerability and capacity.
- CLM-DISASTER_RISK_REDUCTION_BASICS-C — directly supported by its four priorities for action: understanding risk; strengthening disaster-risk governance; investing in resilience; and preparedness, response and recovery.

Proposed locators: Sendai Framework, Priorities for Action 1–4 and the sections on understanding disaster risk and disaster-risk governance.

Disposition: DIRECT as an international policy framework. Local risk reduction still requires hazard-specific assessment, territorial data, legal authority, community participation, resources and current local emergency plans.
