## 2. Passage findings

### Mining engineering — 3 Claims

Colorado School of Mines official programme page, FAQ “What is mining engineering?”, describes the discipline as applying engineering, geoscience and technology to discovery, extraction and processing of mineral resources, and says mining engineers design/manage the mine lifecycle from exploration and feasibility through planning, operations and closure. The page identifies worker safety, environmental and social impacts, rock mechanics, ventilation, mineral processing and sustainable practices.

- Claim A: source supports the disciplinary definition. The EU has no locator; the definition is broad and contains little mechanism.
- Claim B: source supports extraction, processing and multidisciplinary scope. This remains a broad taxonomy, not a complete account of the discipline.
- Claim C: source supports the need for site-specific engineering and safety/environmental expertise; the explicit “overview is not a project” boundary is appropriate. Do not derive mine design, ventilation, ground-control or operating parameters from this overview.

### Mechanical engineering — 2 Claims

The linked American University of Sharjah page was not retrievable in this check. An official AUS mechanical-engineering flyer (same institution) describes the field as combining engineering, physics, mathematics and materials science and lists mechanics, dynamics, thermodynamics, structural analysis, design, manufacture and maintenance of mechanical systems. This is useful institutional corroboration, but it is not the exact URL recorded in the Source record.

- Claims A and B are consistent with the official flyer at introductory level.
- The recorded Source URL remains unverified and the EU has no locator. Treat source fit as provisional; do not use these Claims as machine-design or safety instructions.

### Molecular biology — 3 Claims

The NLM/NCBI “Molecular Biology” resource defines the field around molecular structure/function, inheritance and protein synthesis, and identifies gene expression/regulation, DNA replication, transcription and translation. The open-access chapter “Basics of Molecular Biology” describes interactions among DNA, RNA and protein biosynthesis.

- Claim A is supported at a broad introductory level.
- Claim B's distinction among DNA, RNA and protein levels is directly relevant; NCBI identifies replication, transcription and translation.
- Claim C is a useful boundary statement about system, organization level and experimental context, but its full contextual proposition is not established by a broad field definition alone.
- The canonical Source points to a general NCBI guide landing page, not the precise NCBI resource or chapter subsection. No laboratory, clinical or genetic-engineering protocol may be inferred.

### Neuroscience — 3 Claims

NINDS “Brain Basics” states that the brain interprets senses, initiates body movement and controls behaviour; its linked primer describes motor and somatosensory functions. NINDS also states that better understanding of the brain can contribute to developing treatments for nervous-system diseases and disorders.

- Claim A's scope is broader than the linked brain-focused source: “nervous system” includes more than the brain. Partial support only.
- Claim B is directly supported for brain functions, but should not be generalized beyond the source's level.
- Claim C is broadly consistent with NINDS' research-to-treatment statement, but nervous-system scope and methods need a more specific source chain.
- The canonical Source is relevant but the EU has no pinpoint. All three remain HOLD pending precise passage and scope check.

### Exercise physiology — 3 Claims

The ACSM Physical Activity Guidelines page identifies aerobic activity and activities maintaining or increasing muscular strength and endurance; it states recommendations are based on current science and professional insights. The 2026 ACC scientific statement identifies frequency, intensity, time, type and progression as core components of exercise prescription, and requires individualization by health condition, functional capacity, symptoms, medications, comorbidities and preferences. ACSM's resistance-training resource describes strength, endurance and physical-function benefits.

- Claim A's frequency/intensity/duration/type framing is supported by exercise-guideline literature; the canonical ACSM link stores no pinpoint and should be tied to the exact source passage.
- Claim B is consistent with aerobic-activity guidance and the general role of activity in cardiorespiratory fitness; direct evidence should be pinned before E is raised.
- Claim C is supported generally by ACSM resistance-training guidance on strength and function. It must not be converted into an individualized prescription.
- ACSM material includes population-specific guidance and is not a substitute for individual clinical assessment.

### Payment systems — 1 Claim

BIS/CPSS-IOSCO, Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures (April 2012), §1.10, pp. 7–8 (PDF pp. 13–14), defines a payment system as instruments, procedures and rules for transfer of funds among participants, including participants and the operating entity. Annex D, pp. 147–148 (PDF pp. 153–154), repeats the definition and describes organisational arrangements.

- Claim A is directly supported by §1.10 and Annex D.
- The source is dated 2012 and focuses on financial market infrastructures; any extension into current national law, regulation, settlement finality or payment-system risk requires current jurisdiction-specific sources.
- The linked EU remains generic and lacks §1.10/Annex D locators.

### Materials science — 1 Claim

NIST Materials Science and Engineering Division identifies structure–property–processing relationships as core to the discipline. NIST's Thermodynamics and Kinetics Group describes phase transformations, microstructure evolution and material properties; NIST's Materials Design Toolkit describes processing–structure–property correlations and material selection.

- Claim C is supported directly at the general structure/process/property level.
- The caution that composition alone may be insufficient is consistent with NIST's emphasis on processing and microstructure.
- No material selection or performance prediction for a real component should be derived without application-specific data and testing. EU has no pinpoint.

### Metallurgy — 3 Claims

ASM International's Metallurgy for the Non-Metallurgist, 2nd ed. (2011), publisher description covers composition, structure, properties and processing, phase diagrams, stress–strain curves, transformation diagrams, microstructure, fracture surfaces, melting, casting, forging, forming, rolling, cold work, heat treatment, welding, testing and nondestructive inspection.

- Claim A's disciplinary scope is consistent with the publisher description.
- Claim B's structure, phase transformation, deformation and heat-treatment elements are reflected in the source description.
- Claim C's application to material selection, production and inspection is consistent with the description; the explicit requirement for specialist evaluation is an appropriate boundary.
- The publisher description is not a page-level passage from the book, and the canonical EU has no locator.
