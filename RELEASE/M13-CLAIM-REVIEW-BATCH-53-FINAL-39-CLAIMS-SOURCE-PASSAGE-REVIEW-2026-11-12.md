## 1. Method

Exact Claim statements and linked Evidence Use/Source records were inspected. Public primary, official, university, and scholarly source pages were checked for the relevant subject matter. D/E/B follow the locked M13 rubric: D explanatory depth; E claim-specific evidence fit; B boundary and epistemic discipline.

All 39 Evidence Use records still lack reproducible claim-specific page/section/figure/table locators. Therefore E=1 throughout, even where the external source directly supports the Claim. This reflects the state of the stored Evidence Use chain, not the general reputation of the external source.

- Claim A is broadly supported by UNESCO's framework.
- Claim B is directly supported in substance, but the Russian Claim repeats “неформальном” twice where the source distinguishes non-formal and informal learning. This is a textual defect requiring editorial correction under bounded authorization.
- Claim C is directly supported by UNESCO's listed contemporary topics.
- The stored EU records cite the broad UNESCO page without a specific paragraph/section locator.

### Metaphysics — 2 Claims

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry “Metaphysics” discusses what exists, substance, identity, causation, time and modality; it explains modality in terms of possibility and necessity and treats identity and causal relations as central metaphysical topics.

- Claims A and B are consistent with the entry's scope.
- These are broad disciplinary descriptions rather than claims that settle any one metaphysical position.
- The source is an overview entry; exact subsection locators are absent from EU.

### Migration — 1 Claim

IOM's World Migration Report materials describe migration drivers as multifaceted, including economic, social, environmental and conflict-related factors, and emphasize that drivers affect people differently across contexts. The IOM World Migration Education Toolkit also lists economic, social, environmental and conflict components.

- Claim B's central point—that migration has diverse causes—is supported.
- The Claim additionally names family and education circumstances. Those categories are plausible and commonly recognized, but the reviewed source record points only to the World Migration Report landing page; exact support for each listed driver is not preserved.
- Keep the Claim as a non-exhaustive list and add exact report chapter/page locators.

### Modeling and simulation — 3 Claims

MIT OpenCourseWare's Introduction to Modeling and Simulation course covers continuum methods, atomistic and molecular simulation, quantum mechanics and applications to engineering problems. Its course materials explicitly illustrate that systems at different time and length scales require different simulation techniques.

- Claim A is supported by the course's formal/computational modeling scope.
- Claim B is directly supported by the different length/time scales and corresponding simulation methods.
- Claim C's statement that simulation results depend on model assumptions and scope, and are not proof that the model is true, is a valid methodological boundary; the course overview alone does not establish a complete validation standard.
- The EU records contain no lecture/slide/page locator.

### Modeling — 2 Claims

NIBIB's “Computational Modeling” page describes computational models as computer-based simulations of complex systems using mathematical and scientific methods; variables define the system and simulations produce outputs under different conditions. It explicitly distinguishes mechanistic models based on underlying processes from data-driven models based on patterns in datasets.

- Claim A is supported by the model-as-simplified-representation idea and the use of assumptions/variables to study system behaviour.
- Claim B is directly supported by NIBIB's mechanistic versus data-driven distinction.
- The NIBIB source is focused on biomedical computational modeling; do not treat it as a universal technical validation standard.

### Money and banking — 1 Claim

The Federal Reserve's September 4, 2026 note, “New Forms of Money and the U.S. Monetary Aggregates,” states that U.S. monetary aggregates classify money and near-money assets according to liquidity and function, including store-of-value and medium-of-exchange functions.

- Claim B is directly supported.
- The Source and Claim should explicitly retain the U.S. jurisdictional context: monetary aggregates are institutionally defined and differ across central banks and time.
- The EU has no exact paragraph/table locator.

### Music theory — 3 Claims

Open University's OpenLearn course “An Introduction to Music Theory” introduces music staves, clefs, rhythmic and pitch values, metre and time signatures, and includes triads/chords and the use of musical elements to analyse compositions.

- Claim A is supported as an introductory description of music theory and notation.
- Claim B is directly supported by the course's listed notation elements.
- Claim C is broadly supported for intervals/chords/harmony and structural analysis, but the stored source page does not clearly pinpoint the claim's “modes” component. Keep this component on HOLD until a direct lesson locator is recorded.

### Negotiation — 2 Claims

Harvard Law School's Program on Negotiation describes negotiation as back-and-forth communication between interdependent parties with shared and opposed interests. It distinguishes positions (what a party says it wants) from underlying interests (why it wants it).

- Claim A is directly supported.
- Claim B is directly supported, including the distinction between positions and interests.
- The source is a practical overview; do not turn the claims into universal psychological rules.

### Open science — 2 Claims

UNESCO's Open Science page says open science aims to make scientific knowledge openly available, accessible and reusable; it lists publications, data, educational resources, software, hardware and open infrastructures.

- Claim B is directly supported by the listed open-science components.
- Claim C is a valid epistemic boundary: access and reuse do not themselves prove a claim true. The UNESCO page defines openness but does not directly state this logical distinction; add a direct evidence-quality source or retain the boundary as an explicitly reasoned principle.
- EU records lack exact Recommendation/section locators.

### Operations research — 3 Claims

INFORMS defines operations research as a scientific process for transforming data into insights for better decisions. It distinguishes descriptive analytics (past events), predictive analytics (future outcomes) and prescriptive analytics (decision support/actionable advice).

- Claim A is directly supported at the introductory level.
- Claim B is directly supported by the descriptive/predictive/prescriptive distinction.
- Claim C's caveat about problem formulation, data and assumptions is methodologically appropriate, but the INFORMS overview page does not directly provide a full model-validation passage. A specific modelling source is needed for that component.

### Optimization — 3 Claims

MIT OpenCourseWare's Optimization Methods course covers linear, network, discrete, nonlinear and dynamic optimization, and includes sensitivity analysis, robust optimization, network flows, dynamic programming and optimal control.

- Claim A's objective-function/constraints description is a standard formulation of an optimization problem, but the course landing page does not itself provide the exact definition. A lecture/reading locator is required.
- Claim B's list of linear, network, discrete, nonlinear and dynamic approaches is directly reflected in the course content.
- Claim C's warning about sensitivity, constraints and assumptions is consistent with the course's sensitivity and robust optimization content; “not universally best” is an appropriate boundary.
- EU records lack lecture/section locators.

### Papermaking — 2 Claims

Museum of Fine Arts Boston's “Handmade Paper” page describes plant/textile/recycled fibres, pulping, forming a sheet on a mould, drainage, couching onto absorbent material, pressing out water with a sponge and drying. The page explicitly notes that material type and pulp/water proportion change the resulting paper.

- Claim B's relationship between raw material/fibres and sheet properties is broadly supported, but “homogeneity” and the influence of technique need a direct process locator or additional source.
- Claim C is directly supported by the separate forming, drainage/couching, pressing and drying steps.
- The linked museum source is a hand-papermaking demonstration, not an industrial process standard; its EU correctly limits generalization.

### Philosophy of language — 2 Claims

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entries on philosophy of language, meaning and reference discuss meaning, reference, linguistic use, truth conditions, semantics, pragmatics and context-dependent interpretation.

- Claim A is consistent with the entry's disciplinary scope.
- Claim C is supported by the distinction between semantic content and what speakers communicate in context, and by context-sensitive reference.
- The EU records cite a broad SEP entry without subsection locators.

### Philosophy of mathematics — 3 Claims

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry “Philosophy of Mathematics” discusses mathematical objects, knowledge, proof, truth and competing philosophical positions. Its section on mathematical proof distinguishes formal and informal proof and discusses the epistemic status of computer-assisted proofs.

- Claim A is consistent with the subject's scope.
- Claim B's distinction between mathematical proof and empirical confirmation is broadly supported by the entry's treatment of proof and its epistemic role; direct wording should be tied to a precise section.
- Claim C is appropriately pluralist: the successful application of mathematics does not, by itself, settle among competing accounts of mathematical objects and knowledge.

### Philosophy of mind — 2 Claims

The recorded Stanford Encyclopedia source is “The Mind/Brain Identity Theory.” It explains one influential account relating mental states/processes to brain states/processes and discusses conscious experience and qualia.

- Claim A's general field description is only partially supported: a single theory entry does not comprehensively establish the scope of philosophy of mind.
- Claim B states that different theories describe mental/physical relations differently and that neural description alone does not finish philosophical analysis of experience. The source offers useful discussion of identity theory and qualia, but it is not a comparative survey of all major theories.
- Source-fit limitation: add a general philosophy-of-mind overview or more appropriate survey source before raising E.

### Philosophy of science — 2 Claims

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry “Scientific Method” discusses observation, experimentation, models, theories, testing and competing accounts of scientific method. The related entry “Theory and Observation in Science” explains that theoretical concepts and models shape the interpretation of empirical observations.

- Claim B is supported: scientific theories are not mere lists of observations; concepts, models and assumptions organize interpretation.
- Claim C is supported in its caution about scope and criteria: the SEP entry explicitly describes methodological pluralism and the absence of one universally fixed scientific method. The Claim's example about one observation not guaranteeing universal applicability is consistent with this scope discipline.

### Planetary science — 3 Claims

NASA's Planetary Science Division page states that planetary science brings together geology, chemistry, astronomy, physics and biology to study planets and small bodies. It says missions return observations, data and sometimes samples that help explain solar-system formation/evolution and possible signs of habitability.

- Claim A is directly supported.
- Claim B is directly supported by NASA's explicit mention of observations, mission data and returned samples.
- Claim C is directly supported by NASA's statement about solar-system formation, evolution and habitability.
- The source page was updated August 3, 2026; the linked EU has no pinpoint locator.
