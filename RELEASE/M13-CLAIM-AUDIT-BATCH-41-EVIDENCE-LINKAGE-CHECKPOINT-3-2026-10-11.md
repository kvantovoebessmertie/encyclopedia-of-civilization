- Identity and structural chain: filename/internal Claim ID match; `EU-ANATOMY_BASICS-A` references the intended Claim and Source and declares `supports`.

### Ancient Civilizations Basics A — `CLM-ANCIENT_CIVILIZATIONS_BASICS-A`
- Claim: early civilizations were characterized, among other things, by settled settlements, labor specialization, and social stratification.
- Structural chain: PASS; EU points to the intended Claim and Source, with `supports`, but has no section locator.
- OpenStax *World History, Volume 1*, section 3.1 directly describes settled populations, labor specialization, and social stratification as attributes of early civilizations.
- **Disposition:** substantive support PASS; improve traceability by pointing the EU to section 3.1 and the specific paragraphs on settlement, specialization, and hierarchy.

### Ancient Civilizations Basics B — `CLM-ANCIENT_CIVILIZATIONS_BASICS-B`
- Claim: in some regions, agriculture was associated with settlement and demographic changes, with direction and strength varying by time and place.
- Structural chain: PASS; EU is generic and has no locator.
- OpenStax section 3.1 supports links between farming, settled populations, population growth, food surplus and urban growth. Section 2.3 also stresses that the agricultural transition occurred independently at different times and places and that not all groups adopted farming.
- **Disposition:** core, qualified synthesis is supported. The variation qualifier is appropriate; pinpoint both sections and avoid implying a universal, single-direction causal sequence.

### Ancient Civilizations Basics C — `CLM-ANCIENT_CIVILIZATIONS_BASICS-C`
- Claim: sources for ancient societies include archaeological evidence and written materials where preserved.
- Structural chain: PASS; EU generic.
- OpenStax section 3.1 explicitly notes that prehistoric peoples left no writings and historians/archaeologists infer beliefs from artifacts; this supports the archaeological-evidence clause and the importance of distinguishing societies with and without surviving written records. The local Source is the same section 3.1.
- **Disposition:** core claim supported, but a second pinpoint on the role of written sources in literate ancient societies would strengthen the broader wording. Avoid suggesting all ancient societies had written evidence.
