## Claim A — risk management as a systematic process for organizational uncertainty

Claim: `CLM-RISK_MANAGEMENT_BASICS-A@1`  
Evidence Use: `EU-RISK_MANAGEMENT_BASICS-A@1`  
Source: `SRC-RISK_MANAGEMENT_BASICS@1` (ISO 31000)

**Passages located:** ISO's official ISO 31000:2018 overview describes the standard as providing principles and guidelines and outlines a comprehensive approach: identifying, analysing, evaluating, treating, monitoring and communicating risks across an organization. It also describes risk management as a foundation for integration throughout governance, strategy, planning, reporting, policies, values and culture.

**Finding:** The process/system framing is supported. However, “учитывать неопределённость при принятии решений” is a synthesis rather than a verbatim statement in the ISO overview. The current Evidence Use does not name any relevant part of the standard and has no pinpoint. The source landing page is a summary, not the complete normative text.

**Required follow-up:** Add a locator to ISO 31000:2018 clauses 4–6 only after verifying the accessible licensed/authorized text; otherwise cite the public ISO overview as a summary and state its limits. Avoid implying that the landing page reproduces the complete standard.

## Claim B — risk as effect of uncertainty on objectives; context dependence

Claim: `CLM-RISK_MANAGEMENT_BASICS-B@1`  
Evidence Use: `EU-RISK_MANAGEMENT_BASICS-B@1`  
Source: `SRC-RISK_MANAGEMENT_BASICS@1` (ISO 31000)

**Passages located:** OECD's current report section “Risk management standards” explicitly attributes to ISO 31000:2018 the definition of risk as the effect of uncertainty on objectives, noting that the effect can be positive or negative. It also explains that the framework/process applies across organizations and types of risk. ISO's official page describes use across different organizational sizes and sectors and provides tools for contextualizing risk management.

**Finding:** The definition is directly reproduced by OECD with attribution to ISO 31000. The claim's context-dependence is consistent with ISO's tailoring/contextualization approach. But the current Source record points only to the ISO overview; the current Evidence Use has no locator. OECD is a distinct secondary source and should not be silently treated as the ISO standard itself.

**Required follow-up:** Add a separate OECD Source identity if this secondary corroboration is retained, with an exact locator to the “Risk management standards” section. For the ISO primary source, cite the relevant ISO 31000 clause only where access and rights permit. Keep the positive/negative effect nuance visible in editorial explanation.

## Claim C — integration into governance and decision-making

Claim: `CLM-RISK_MANAGEMENT_BASICS-C@1`  
Evidence Uses: `EU-RISK_MANAGEMENT_BASICS-C@1` and `EU-RISK_MANAGEMENT_COSO@1`  
Sources: ISO 31000 overview and COSO ERM page

**Passages located:**
- ISO's official overview says risk management should be embedded in governance, strategy, planning, reporting processes, policies, values and culture, and calls integration throughout the organization a benefit.
- COSO's official ERM page identifies its framework as “Integrating with Strategy and Performance” and states that risk should be considered both in strategy-setting and in driving performance.
- The UK Government Orange Book, an official public-sector risk framework, states in §§2.1 and 3.1 that risk management is part of governance/leadership and integral to organizational activities and decision-making. This is useful corroboration but has public-sector scope.

**Finding:** The core Claim is well supported by ISO and COSO summaries. The stronger wording “а не ... отдельная проверка после события” is a contrastive interpretation, not an exact quotation from the current linked sources. It is reasonable as a teaching distinction but should not be attributed literally to ISO/COSO. The current ISO Evidence Use is generic; the COSO Evidence Use says “independently corroborates” but lacks a pinpoint.

**Required follow-up:** Add exact locators to the ISO overview's integration/governance statements and COSO's “Integrating with Strategy and Performance” description. Consider a note that COSO is an enterprise/governance framework and Orange Book is public-sector-specific, not universally interchangeable.

## Cross-claim findings

1. All three primary ISO Evidence Use records lack reproducible section/paragraph locators. The COSO corroborating Evidence Use also lacks a pinpoint.
2. ISO's public overview supports the broad process and integration ideas but is not the full text of ISO 31000:2018. Do not claim to have verified clauses 4–6 from that landing page.
3. The risk definition (“effect of uncertainty on objectives”) is directly reproduced in the OECD report with explicit attribution to ISO 31000. OECD should be represented as a separate secondary Source if used.
4. COSO supports the integration of risk with strategy and performance, but does not by itself prove every phrase in Claim C.
5. The UK Orange Book offers strong public-sector corroboration for governance and decision integration; its sector scope must remain explicit.
6. No D/E/B score is assigned here. Existing historical scores are not changed.
