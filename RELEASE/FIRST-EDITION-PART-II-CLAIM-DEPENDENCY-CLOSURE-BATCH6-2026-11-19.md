## 3. Substantive and evidence findings

- **Civil society:** all six EUs lack locators. The World Bank PDF is stored under a generic “public reference” identity; Britannica is an independent secondary overview. Both may support a general definition, but the organization categories and public-service/advocacy roles need direct sections and clearer source metadata. Rights and reuse review remains open.
- **Comparative civilizations:** all three EUs lack locators. The Source identity is generic (“Comparative civilizations — public reference”) and points to an UNESCO archive multimedia object without a descriptive pinpoint. Source identity and relevance must be established before evidence adequacy can be judged.
- **Comparative law:** all six EUs have locators. Cornell LII provides a general legal-systems overview; the independent U.S. Army Judge Advocate’s *Law of War Deskbook* is a narrower military/legal source, so its use must remain contextual rather than treated as a universal comparative-law authority. Rights, jurisdiction and scope review remain open.
- **Democratic institutions:** all three EUs lack locators. International IDEA’s Democracy portal is relevant but broad; add precise material on representation, elections, accountability and institutional performance.
- **Demography:** all three EUs lack locators. UN World Population Prospects 2024 is authoritative for population estimates/projections, but the exact tables/sections for fertility, mortality, migration, age structure and population momentum must be cited.
