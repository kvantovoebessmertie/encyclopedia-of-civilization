# M13 Claim Review — Batch 12: Nuclear-Physics Foundations
Date: 2026-10-09
Branch: `m13-system-wide-depth-audit-2026-10-09`
Scope: three foundational claims in `nuclear-physics-information-basics`, including their Evidence Use and Source records.
Review type: purposive conceptual-depth review. No content records changed.

## Scoring rubric
- **D — explanatory depth (0–3):** meaningful explanation for the claim's stated introductory scope.
- **E — claim-specific evidence fit (0–3):** whether the stored Evidence Use identifies support for the exact proposition.
- **B — boundaries / epistemic discipline (0–3):** whether scope and likely misinterpretation are adequately bounded.
Scores are reviewer judgments for triage, not automated measurements.

## Claim-level review

### 1. `CLM_NUCLEAR_PHYSICS_INFORMATION_BASICS_A` — D1 / E1 / B2
**Statement:** Nuclear physics studies properties of atomic nuclei, their composition, states and interactions.

**Linked record:** `EU-NUCLEAR_PHYSICS_INFORMATION_BASICS-A` → `SRC-NUCLEAR_PHYSICS_INFORMATION_BASICS`.

**External check:** IAEA's [Nuclear Science overview](https://www.iaea.org/topics/nuclear-science) is a relevant institutional starting point for nuclear science, but it is a broad topic page rather than a precise, claim-level technical reference.

**Assessment:** A sound introductory definition, but it is a label-level proposition without an explanatory distinction between nuclear structure, nuclear reactions and radioactive decay. The Evidence Use text (“the source is used within the stated material”) does not identify the passage or concept that supports the definition. The Source identity “Nuclear science — IAEA” is institutionally recognizable but not specific enough to make the exact evidence trail easy to audit.

**Disposition:** Low-severity depth and evidence-traceability finding. Candidate for a more specific reference and a short claim-specific Evidence Use description.

### 2. `CLM_NUCLEAR_PHYSICS_INFORMATION_BASICS_B` — D2 / E1 / B3
**Statement:** Changes in nuclear states may be accompanied by emission of particles or electromagnetic radiation; the process depends on the particular nucleus and transition.

**Linked record:** `EU-NUCLEAR_PHYSICS_INFORMATION_BASICS-B` → `SRC-NUCLEAR_PHYSICS_INFORMATION_BASICS`.

**External check:** IAEA material on [radiation basics and radiation protection](https://www-ns.iaea.org/downloads/iec/radiation-basics-leaflet_e.pdf) describes unstable nuclei and emission of particles or electromagnetic radiation, including alpha, beta and gamma radiation.

**Assessment:** The use of “may” and the explicit dependence on the nucleus and transition are appropriate boundaries. However, the claim could be more explanatory by distinguishing radioactive decay from other nuclear transitions and naming representative emitted particles/radiation without implying all processes emit the same forms. The linked Evidence Use is generic and does not identify what the IAEA material actually supports.

**Disposition:** Evidence-description correction candidate; consider a more specific source and an explanatory distinction if within the slice's authorized scope.

### 3. `CLM_NUCLEAR_PHYSICS_INFORMATION_BASICS_C` — D1 / E1 / B3
**Statement:** Describing nuclear processes requires distinguishing the physical mechanism, measured quantity and experimental conditions; everyday analogies do not replace quantitative description.

**Linked record:** `EU-NUCLEAR_PHYSICS_INFORMATION_BASICS-C` → `SRC-NUCLEAR_PHYSICS_INFORMATION_BASICS`.

**Assessment:** This is a sound methodological boundary, especially the caution against substituting analogy for quantitative description. It is abstract, however, and gives no example of the distinction between mechanism, observable and experimental conditions. The generic Evidence Use text provides no claim-specific support; the broad IAEA topic page is not a precise source for this epistemic recommendation.

**Disposition:** Low-severity depth and evidence-traceability finding. An example or a more precise methodological reference would improve usefulness, but no unsupported factual defect is established from this sample.

## Cross-slice relevance and limits
This slice is **foundational nuclear-science education**, not an emergency-response guide. The presence of nuclear-physics claims does not establish coverage of radiological emergencies, nuclear detonation, sheltering, fallout, contamination, decontamination, contaminated food/water or official emergency alerts. Conversely, this sample does not establish that those topics are absent from every other slice. The system-wide inventory and later scenario-focused gap analysis must answer that separately; do not infer readiness from this conceptual slice.

## Batch findings
1. All three Evidence Use records use generic wording and fail to identify claim-specific support. This is a consistent evidence-auditability weakness.
2. The stored Source locator is a broad IAEA topic page. It is appropriate as an entry point but weak as the sole traceable basis for precise scientific propositions.
3. Claim B has good epistemic boundaries but could make the relevant physical distinction clearer. Claim C has a valuable boundary principle but needs an example to become practically instructive.
4. These three claims are a purposive sample, not a full D/E/B census of the slice or corpus.
