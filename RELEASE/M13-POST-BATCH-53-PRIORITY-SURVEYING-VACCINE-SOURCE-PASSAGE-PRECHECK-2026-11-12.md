## 1. Surveying — 3 Claims

Canonical Source: NOAA / Defense Mapping Agency, Geodesy for the Layman, DMA TR 80-003 (last revised 1984).

Exact passages:
- Introduction, lines 69–72: defines surveying/geodesy in terms of determining positions and shapes/areas through observation and measurement; modern requirements include distance and direction.
- Chapter III, lines 138–141: defines survey as determining form, extent and position of land through linear and angular measurements and geometry/trigonometry; lists traditional techniques and satellite-based methods.
- Chapter III, lines 159–165: triangulation measures triangle angles and a baseline distance to compute positions; measurements are adjusted to reduce observational error.
- Chapter III, lines 191–196: traverse methods measure angles and distances between survey points to compute directions and positions.

## 2. Vaccine safety — 2 Claims

Canonical Source: WHO, “Vaccines and immunization: Vaccine safety,” updated Q&A dated 23 September 2025.

Exact passages:
- “How do we know that a vaccine is safe?”: WHO states that national health authorities continuously monitor safety after vaccines are introduced and investigate adverse events; independent experts assess whether events are causally related or due to other causes.
- “How is vaccine safety monitored?”: WHO states that safety monitoring continues after clinical trials and public introduction and operates at local, national, regional and global levels.
- “How are vaccines tested?”: WHO describes pre-introduction clinical trial phases and says additional studies may monitor efficacy and safety in larger populations over longer periods after introduction.
- “How are vaccines approved for public use?”: national/regional regulators oversee development, trial approval, evidence evaluation and licensing; WHO prequalification independently assesses quality, safety and efficacy.

Findings:
- CLM-VACCINATION_INFORMATION_BASICS-B is directly supported by WHO's statement that safety monitoring continues after vaccines are introduced and by the description of national and global surveillance.
- CLM-VACCINATION_INFORMATION_BASICS-C is supported by the separate clinical-trial phases, intended populations, post-introduction studies and ongoing evaluation. The Claim's insistence on vaccine-, population- and data-specific interpretation is appropriate.
- The WHO Q&A is a general international overview. It does not replace vaccine-specific product information, national regulator decisions, clinical assessment or current local schedules.
- Required follow-up: add exact section/heading locators in Evidence Use and preserve the distinction between an adverse event following immunization and an event causally attributable to the vaccine.
