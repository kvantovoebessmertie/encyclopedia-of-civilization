### Provenance gap

The public repository explicitly says the books' source files are private. No public source tree, complete edit history, adapter declaration, rights-holder identity for the adaptation, or separate rights grant from the adapter was independently located in the inspected public repositories.

**Disposition:** upstream OpenStax CC BY 4.0 at the exact cited source commit is confirmed at repository-file level; the Open Book Project's own CC BY 4.0 statement is public and explicit; however, the adaptation's complete provenance and the authority of the party making that licence assertion remain unverified independently. Do not label the adaptation's rights chain “fully cleared”. The safe use case remains citation and pinpoint locator only, with Encyclopedia text independently authored. Exclude adaptation prose, images, figures, maps, tables, bulk extraction and AI ingestion from this Package A pathway.

## 3. Independent research articles — rights and support reviewed

### 3.1 McMahon et al. (2023), Lagash — CC BY 4.0

Article: “Dense urbanism and economic multi-centrism at third-millennium BC Lagash”, *Antiquity* 97(393), 596–615. DOI: 10.15184/aqy.2023.36.

The Cambridge publisher page expressly identifies the article as CC BY 4.0 and says reuse is permitted provided the original work is properly cited. Its abstract and article describe dense urban occupation at Lagash, distinct walled quarters, and multiple foci of intensive industrial production. This provides case-specific independent support for urban density and economic/labour specialization relevant to Claim A. It is not evidence that every early civilization shared the same configuration.

Rights disposition: article text may be reused under CC BY 4.0 with compliant attribution, subject to checking any individual third-party material credit before reusing a figure or other component. No figure is proposed for Encyclopedia use.

Publisher: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/dense-urbanism-and-economic-multicentrism-at-thirdmillennium-bc-lagash/2D27165A470831B32B9B29C72EBEF8AD

### 3.2 Twiss et al. (2024), Çatalhöyük — CC BY

Article: “But some were more equal than others: Exploring inequality at Neolithic Çatalhöyük”, *PLOS ONE* 19(9), e0307067. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0307067.

The article's copyright statement expressly applies the Creative Commons Attribution License. PLOS's general policy explains that article content may be reused for any purpose, including commercial purposes, with proper attribution. PLOS separately requires proof of compatible rights or written permission for material owned by someone else.

Substantive boundary: the study finds differentiated assets and patterns of inequality at Çatalhöyük but expressly cautions that observed dissimilarities cannot automatically be equated with a hierarchical social order or treated as proof of institutionalized inequity. The abstract reports no evidence for institutionalized or lasting economic or social inequality. It is therefore useful as a limiting/counterexample source against simplistic universal claims about social stratification; it does not independently support a broad claim that early settlements necessarily had stratified hierarchies.

Figure-specific exclusion: the caption for Fig. 1 credits photographer Jason Quinlan and identifies copyright as belonging to the Çatalhöyük Research Project; the acknowledgments state that Jason Quinlan gave permission for the authors to use his photographs. That author-side permission does not establish a sublicense to the Encyclopedia. Do not reuse Fig. 1 or its photograph. Other figures/tables must likewise be checked individually if ever proposed for reuse; this project does not need them.

Rights disposition: article text and original article content are CC BY subject to attribution; third-party or separately credited components must not be presumed covered. Citation and independently written factual paraphrase are the preferred use here.

Article: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0307067  
PLOS rights policy: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/s/licenses-and-copyright

### 3.3 Khoury et al. (2016), crop origins — CC BY 4.0

Article: “Origins of food crops connect countries worldwide”, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 283(1832):20160792. DOI: 10.1098/rspb.2016.0792.

The PubMed Central full-text record reproduces the publisher's CC BY 4.0 notice. The article analyses the geographical regions of diversity of crops and the contribution of crops from other regions to present-day food supplies and agricultural production. Its analysis is based on modern (2009–2011) food-supply data and production systems, alongside historical discussion of crop movement.

Substantive boundary: this is not direct evidence for the chronology of prehistoric domestication, independent origins of agriculture, or causal effects of agriculture on ancient settlement and population growth. It can provide limited background on crop geographical diversity and later crop dispersal, but it should not be used as the principal evidence for Claim B's prehistoric causal/settlement proposition.

Rights disposition: article is expressly CC BY 4.0. The article's data are described as available through Dryad (DOI 10.5061/dryad.s08t2); the data repository's own licence and terms would need separate verification before any dataset reuse. No dataset or figure reuse is proposed.

Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4920324/  
Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2016.0792

### 3.4 Zeder (2011), Near Eastern agricultural origins — citation-only

Article: Melinda A. Zeder, “The Origins of Agriculture in the Near East”, *Current Anthropology* 52(S4), S221–S235. DOI: 10.1086/659307.

The University of Chicago Press page labels the article “Free”, but the article's information section states “© 2011 by The Wenner-Gren Foundation for Anthropological Research. All rights reserved.” The same page provides a “Request Permissions” route and does not identify a Creative Commons licence. The publisher site's current footer also reserves rights for text and data mining and AI training; this is an additional reason not to bulk-ingest or use this article for AI training without a separate rights determination.

Substantive value: the article's abstract and visible text describe a pluralistic model of Near Eastern domestication, multiple processes and substantial regional variation. It is highly relevant as a scholarly citation for Claim B's nuance. “Free” access is not itself a reuse licence.

Rights disposition: cite bibliographically and link to the publisher page; use only independently authored factual statements within ordinary scholarly citation practice, subject to applicable law and project policy. Do not reproduce the article's prose, abstract, figures, tables or substantial extracts; do not bulk download, text-mine or AI-ingest it absent permission. This is not a legal opinion on any jurisdiction's quotation exceptions.

Publisher: https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/659307  
Smithsonian bibliographic record: https://profiles.si.edu/display/sro_109973
