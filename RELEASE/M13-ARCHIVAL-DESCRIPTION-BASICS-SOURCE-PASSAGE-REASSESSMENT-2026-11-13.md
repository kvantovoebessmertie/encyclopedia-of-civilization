## 1. Current chain and source identity

All three Claims and their Evidence Uses point to `SRC-ARCHIVAL_DESCRIPTION_BASICS@1`. The EU descriptions are generic and contain no claim-specific pinpoint.

The Source identity is the formal title “Society of American Archivists — Describing Archives: A Content Standard”; the URI is the current SAA Technical Subcommittee GitHub Pages edition:
https://saa-ts-dacs.github.io/

The live site identifies the current text as **DACS Version 2022.0.4**. SAA describes DACS as its approved, output-neutral standard for describing archives, personal papers and manuscript collections; it is the official content standard of the U.S. archival community and was adopted in 2005, with a major revision in 2013 and continuing revisions.

Scope warning: DACS is a U.S. archival-community standard. It should not be presented as a universal records-management statute or as the only international archival-description framework.

## 2. Claim-by-Claim source passage review

### Claim A — `CLM-ARCHIVAL_DESCRIPTION_BASICS-A@1`

Claim: “Describing records requires identifying what the records are and the context needed to understand their use and management.”

Direct DACS passages:
- “Statement of Principles”, introduction, “What is archival description and what should it do?”: archival description helps users understand records, their meaning and the historical process by which they were created and maintained; good description communicates how records evidence organizational or individual functions.
- The same section explains that contextual description helps users evaluate records as evidence and information.
- The “Identifying aggregations of records” passage describes helping users understand what records are, who created them, what events they represent and what they mean.

Assessment:
- Direct support for identifying records and describing their context for understanding and use.
- “Management” should be kept tied to archival administration and description, not generalized to all records-management operations.
- Proposed locator: DACS 2022.0.4, Statement of Principles, Introduction, “What is archival description and what should it do?” and “Identifying aggregations of records”.
- Passage status: **DIRECT, with scope clarification**.

### Claim B — `CLM-ARCHIVAL_DESCRIPTION_BASICS-B@1`

Claim: “Records-management information distinguishes records from non-record materials and supports consistent handling of organizational records.”

DACS passages:
- Statement of Principles, Principle 3: applying common descriptive rules supports consistent descriptions across formats and maintains record context.
- DACS §5.3 “Appraisal, Destruction, and Scheduling Information”: explains that records schedules describe organizational records, establish how long they are required for business, and authorize disposition; it also says appraisal/destruction information can be relevant to interpretation and use.

Important gap:
- DACS is an archival-description standard. The reviewed passages do **not** directly establish the distinction between records and non-record materials.
- NARA's official “Records Basics” page directly defines Federal records and separately defines non-record materials, including extra convenience copies, stocks of publications, and library/museum holdings.
- NARA “Identification of Records, Nonrecord Materials, and Personal Papers” explains the Federal Records Act definition and warns that record status depends on business use and statutory criteria, not merely whether an item is an original or copy.

Assessment:
- DACS supports consistency of archival description and the scheduling/disposition context, but not the whole Claim.
- NARA supplies direct support for the records/non-record distinction **within U.S. Federal Government scope**.
- The Claim currently reads universal. It must be narrowed to U.S. Federal records management or supplemented by applicable jurisdiction-specific records law/standards for each jurisdiction where it is meant to apply.
- Proposed locators:
  - DACS 2022.0.4, Statement of Principles, Principle 3; §5.3 “Appraisal, Destruction, and Scheduling Information”.
  - NARA, “Records Basics”, “Definition of a Record” and “Non-record Materials”: https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/basics
  - NARA, “Identification of Records, Nonrecord Materials, and Personal Papers”: https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/scheduling/id
- Passage status: **PARTIAL on DACS alone; direct only after explicit U.S. Federal scope and the NARA source are added**.

### Claim C — `CLM-ARCHIVAL_DESCRIPTION_BASICS-C@1`

Claim: “Descriptive and management information becomes more useful when it is connected to the record's lifecycle, retention requirements and responsible organization.”

Direct DACS passages:
- Statement of Principles, “What is archival description and what should it do?”: describes the lifecycle and administration of records and calls for documenting appraisal, custodial history, administrative interventions, restrictions and reformatting when they affect interpretation.
- DACS §5.3 “Appraisal, Destruction, and Scheduling Information”: links retention/disposition schedules and appraisal rationale to the understanding and use of archival materials; it explains that a records schedule sets how long records are required for business and authorizes disposition.
- DACS Principle 4 describes records, agents, activities and their relationships as core concepts in archival description; the creator/agent passages explain why responsible actors and activities need to be described.

Assessment:
- Direct support for linking descriptive context to lifecycle, retention/disposition and responsible agents in archival/records administration.
- The statement should not imply that DACS alone determines statutory retention periods; schedules and governing law/policy supply those requirements.
- Proposed locator: DACS 2022.0.4, Statement of Principles, Introduction; Principle 4; §5.3.
- Passage status: **DIRECT, with retention-authority boundary**.

## 3. Rights, jurisdiction and standard-version controls

- The current DACS site identifies version 2022.0.4 and describes the text as an SAA-maintained standard. The reviewed pages do not expose a clear blanket open licence for reproducing the standard text. Do not copy or redistribute substantial DACS text into the encyclopedia without checking SAA's applicable rights terms or obtaining permission.
- A concise factual summary with attribution and exact section citation is distinct from reproducing the standard's text; nevertheless, the project must complete its rights review before storing substantial excerpts or adapted rule text.
- NARA guidance is explicitly about U.S. Federal records and the Federal Records Act. It does not define record/non-record status for every country, private organization, or non-governmental archive.
- For non-U.S. application, add the relevant national law, records authority, and/or international standard rather than generalizing NARA rules.
- Preserve DACS version 2022.0.4 and the access/review date in any future Source record; the standard is on a continuous revision cycle.
