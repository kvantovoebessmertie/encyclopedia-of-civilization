# M13 Claim Review Batch 19 — Generator/CO and Hypothermia
Date: 2026-10-10
Branch: `m13-system-wide-depth-audit-2026-10-09`
Status: **7 additional high-consequence Claims reviewed. This does not close either domain or the full corpus.**

## Method
Reviewed the seven Claim statements and their linked Evidence Use entries from the generated M13 worksheet. Checked the cited CDC, National Weather Service and CPSC public guidance. D (explanatory depth), E (claim-specific evidence fit) and B (boundary discipline) are scored independently from 0–3. These are desk-review scores, not clinical certification or live usability results.

## Claim-level results

| Claim ID | D | E | B | Rationale and disposition |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `CLM-GENERATOR-CO-ALARM-EMERGENCY` | 2 | 3 | 3 | Gives an immediate protective action and emergency escalation for suspected CO exposure, with a clear no-return boundary until safety is confirmed. Linked Evidence Use describes the CDC support specifically. CDC/CPSC guidance confirms CO is dangerous and generators must be operated outdoors; maintain explicit local emergency-service wording and do not encourage re-entry to investigate. |
| `CLM-GENERATOR-NO-INDOOR` | 2 | 3 | 3 | Clear prohibition with a plausible mechanism (CO accumulation in enclosed/semi-enclosed spaces). | `CLM-GENERATOR-OUTSIDE-20FT` | 2 | 3 | 2 | Actionable placement instruction, with distance and nearby openings named. | `CLM-COLD-HYPOTHERMIA-EMERGENCY` | 2 | 3 | 3 | Clearly labels hypothermia as an emergency and includes the 35 °C / 95 °F threshold. The Claim has two separately identified institutional Evidence Use records (NWS and CDC), both directly relevant. Keep the urgent medical-help instruction prominent; avoid implying the threshold is required before seeking help when symptoms are present. |
| `CLM-COLD-HYPOTHERMIA-SIGNS` | 2 | 3 | 2 | Lists recognizable adult warning signs. Linked CDC Evidence Use names the relevant signs and is claim-specific; CDC guidance matches the list. | `CLM-COLD-HYPOTHERMIA-WETNESS` | 2 | 2 | 3 | Correctly broadens risk beyond severe frost by mentioning prolonged cold/water exposure and wet clothing. The linked Evidence Use says the source supports this broader risk boundary but does not name a precise passage; NWS/CDC guidance confirms cold water, rain or sweat can chill a person and cause hypothermia. Tighten the Evidence Use to identify the exact support. |
| `CLM-COLD-LIMIT-OUTDOOR` | 1 | 2 | 1 | A sensible but broad directive without conditions, duration, or explanation. CDC winter-storm guidance supports limiting time outdoors, but the current claim is too generic to help a reader decide what to do under different conditions. Keep as a concise reminder only if linked to more specific cold-exposure guidance and warning signs; otherwise expand or narrow it in a separately authorized correction. |

## Findings
1. The generator placement and no-indoor rules have strong, specific source fit. Their U.S.-specific numerical and operational details must remain attributed to the relevant authority.
2. The hypothermia emergency Claim is stronger than a generic symptom list because it gives a clear escalation instruction and has two relevant institutional sources.
3. `CLM-COLD-LIMIT-OUTDOOR` is a depth/boundary weakness (D=1, B=1): it states a general rule but provides little decision support. It is a correction candidate, not an authorization to edit content in this review batch.
4. No content records were changed. All seven Claims remain in the fixed 1,494-Claim denominator.
