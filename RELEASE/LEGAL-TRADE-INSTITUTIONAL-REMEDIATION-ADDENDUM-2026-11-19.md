## Source and locator mapping

### political-economy-basics
- Source v2: Jeffry Frieden, “The Political Economy of Economic Policy,” IMF Finance & Development, 1 June 2020.
- EU-A: “What Is Political Economy and Why It Matters”; PDF p. 6.
- EU-B: “Institutions, Interests, and Distributive Politics” and “Where You Stand Depends on Where You Sit”; PDF p. 6.
- EU-C: “What Is Political Economy and Why It Matters” and “Political Dynamics Shaping Economic Policy”; PDF p. 6.
- Boundary: examples illustrate institutional mechanisms; no universal causal model or deterministic forecast is claimed.

### comparative-law-basics
- Source v2: Cornell Law School Legal Information Institute, Wex “Legal Systems” (last reviewed July 2023).
- EU-A: opening definition and paragraph on jurisdictional variation.
- EU-B: opening definition of a legal system as rules, procedures and institutions used to interpret/enforce law.
- EU-C: paragraphs on sources of law, judicial roles, evidentiary standards, decision-making authority, overlap and hybrid systems.
- Boundary: Wex is an American-perspective educational overview; categories are overlapping and non-exhaustive.

### administrative-law-basics
- Source v2: Cornell Law School Legal Information Institute, Wex “Administrative Law” (last reviewed July 2026).
- EU-A: opening definition of administrative law and agency creation/operation.
- EU-B: “Structure of Agencies” and “Statutory Regulation of Agencies”.
- EU-C: rulemaking, adjudication and non-binding guidance; federal APA and state analogues.
- Boundary: U.S. federal/state example only; no universal administrative procedure or legal advice is asserted.

### social-policy-basics
- Source v2: OECD “Social Policy” official policy issue page; related “Social Spending” indicator.
- EU-A: opening description and key message on social spending/support.
- EU-B: key message on social spending plus the Social Spending indicator definition (cash benefits, in-kind goods/services and tax breaks with a social purpose).
- EU-C: Context on public social spending differences and key message on financing, coverage, adequacy, delivery and take-up pressures.
- Boundary: OECD comparative data are country-, indicator- and period-specific; they do not create individual entitlements or establish programme causality.

### taxation-basics
- Source v2: OECD “Tax Policy” official policy issue page.
- EU-A: “Taxes seek to raise revenue”.
- EU-B: revenue-raising and “Tax policy shapes incentives to work, invest and innovate”.
- EU-C: differences in tax levels/structures, political choices and administrative capabilities; incentive effects depend on policy design and context.
- Boundary: no universal causal law, country-specific obligation or individual tax advice is asserted.

### migration-and-mobility-basics
- Source v2: United Nations Statistics Division, *Recommendations on Statistics of International Migration and Temporary Mobility* (2025, unedited version, revision 2).
- EU-A: Executive Summary recommendation 1; Chapter 2 §2(a), “Linking migration to changes in the country of residence”, pp. 6–10; supplementary IOM Module 1 definition.
- EU-B: Chapter 2 §§2 and 2(a), pp. 6–10; supplementary IOM Module 1 distinction between internal and international migration.
- EU-C: Executive Summary recommendations 1–3; Chapter 3 §§3 and 3(a), pp. 17–22.
- Boundary: distinguishes international migration from temporary mobility and stocks from flows; the IOM supplementary source covers internal/international movement. No individual immigration, asylum or residence status is determined.
