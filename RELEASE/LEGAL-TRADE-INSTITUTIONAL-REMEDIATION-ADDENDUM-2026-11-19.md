## Source and locator mapping

### political-economy-basics
- Primary Source v2: Jeffry Frieden, “The Political Economy of Economic Policy,” IMF Finance & Development, 1 June 2020.
- Independent Source v2: Verena Fritz, Brian Levy and Rachel Ort (eds.), *Problem-Driven Political Economy Analysis: The World Bank’s Experience* (2014), Chapter 1.
- EU-A: “What Is Political Economy and Why It Matters”; PDF p. 6.
- EU-B: “Institutions, Interests, and Distributive Politics” and “Where You Stand Depends on Where You Sit”; PDF p. 6.
- EU-C: “What Is Political Economy and Why It Matters” and “Political Dynamics Shaping Economic Policy”; PDF p. 6.
- Independent EU-A: Chapter 1, “A Problem-Driven Approach” and “Diagnosing the Political Drivers of Decision Making”, pp. 1–9.
- Independent EU-B: “Diagnosing the Political Drivers of Decision Making” and “Understanding the Mechanisms Used to Manage Political Support”, pp. 7–10.
- Independent EU-C: “Understanding Incentives”, pp. 8–9.
- Boundary: examples illustrate institutional mechanisms; no universal causal model or deterministic forecast is claimed.

### comparative-law-basics
- Primary Source v2: Cornell Law School Legal Information Institute, Wex “Legal Systems” (last reviewed July 2023).
- Independent Source v2: U.S. Army Judge Advocate General’s Legal Center and School, *Law of War Deskbook* (2010), Chapter 7 “Comparative Law”, hosted by the Library of Congress.
- EU-A: opening definition and paragraph on jurisdictional variation.
- EU-B: opening definition of a legal system as rules, procedures and institutions used to interpret/enforce law.
- EU-C: paragraphs on sources of law, judicial roles, evidentiary standards, decision-making authority, overlap and hybrid systems.
- Independent EU-A: Chapter 7 §E, p. 201 — comparative law as comparison of similarities/differences between legal approaches, traditions and systems.
- Independent EU-B: §G, p. 201 — legal system as institutions, procedures and rules.
- Independent EU-C: §§F–I, p. 201 — legal traditions/families, mixed systems and theoretical/applied comparison; it does not directly establish every listed judicial/evidence dimension.
- Boundary: Wex and the Deskbook are U.S.-oriented educational sources; categories are overlapping and non-exhaustive.

### administrative-law-basics
- Primary Source v2: Cornell Law School Legal Information Institute, Wex “Administrative Law” (last reviewed July 2026).
- Independent Source v2: U.S. Code, Title 5, 2024 Edition, Part I, Chapter 5, Subchapter II §§551–559 (Administrative Procedure Act).
- EU-A: opening definition of administrative law and agency creation/operation.
- EU-B: “Structure of Agencies” and “Statutory Regulation of Agencies”.
- EU-C: rulemaking, adjudication and non-binding guidance; federal APA and state analogues.
- Independent EU-A: 5 U.S.C. §551(1), definition of “agency”; §§551–559, administrative procedure.
- Independent EU-B: §551(1) and §§553–559, statutory agency procedures and powers; does not independently establish all constitutional limits.
- Independent EU-C: §553 rulemaking; §§554–557 adjudications/hearings/evidence/initial decisions; §558 sanctions and licences.
- Boundary: U.S. federal example only; no universal administrative procedure or legal advice is asserted.

### social-policy-basics
- Primary Source v2: OECD “Social Policy” official policy issue page; related “Social Spending” indicator.
- Independent Source v2: International Labour Organization, “Social protection” official topic page.
- EU-A: opening description and key message on social spending/support.
- EU-B: key message on social spending plus the Social Spending indicator definition (cash benefits, in-kind goods/services and tax breaks with a social purpose).
- EU-C: Context on public social spending differences and key message on financing, coverage, adequacy, delivery and take-up pressures.
- Independent EU-A: ILO opening definition of social protection as policies/programmes addressing life-cycle risks, poverty, vulnerability and social exclusion.
- Independent EU-B: ILO nine social-security branches and relevant Convention No. 102 contingencies; does not independently substantiate housing as a category.
- Independent EU-C: ILO discussion of coverage gaps, access barriers and nationally defined protection floors; not a cross-country financing/eligibility comparison.
- Boundary: OECD comparative data are country-, indicator- and period-specific; they do not create individual entitlements or establish programme causality.

### taxation-basics
- Primary Source v2: OECD “Tax Policy” official policy issue page.
- Independent Source v2: IMF Fiscal Affairs Department, “Tax Policy” official topic page.
- EU-A: “Taxes seek to raise revenue”.
- EU-B: revenue-raising and “Tax policy shapes incentives to work, invest and innovate”.
- EU-C: differences in tax levels/structures, political choices and administrative capabilities; incentive effects depend on policy design and context.
- Independent EU-A: IMF programme purpose/objectives, revenue raising for spending needs.
- Independent EU-B: IMF discussion of balancing efficiency/equity and incentives to invest, work and save.
- Independent EU-C: IMF discussion of institutional context and revenue-administration priorities; no optimal tax mix or invariant causal result is claimed.
- Boundary: no universal causal law, country-specific obligation or individual tax advice is asserted.

### migration-and-mobility-basics
- Primary Source v2: United Nations Statistics Division, *Recommendations on Statistics of International Migration and Temporary Mobility* (2025, unedited version, revision 2).
- Independent Source v2: World Bank Group, “Migration and Labor Mobility” official topic page.
- EU-A: Executive Summary recommendation 1; Chapter 2 §2(a), “Linking migration to changes in the country of residence”, pp. 6–10; supplementary IOM Module 1 definition.
- EU-B: Chapter 2 §§2 and 2(a), pp. 6–10; supplementary IOM Module 1 distinction between internal and international migration.
- EU-C: Executive Summary recommendations 1–3; Chapter 3 §§3 and 3(a), pp. 17–22.
- Independent EU-A: World Bank Context section on countries of origin/transit/destination and internal migration.
- Independent EU-B: Context and Main drivers of migration, including internal/international movement, forced displacement and labour mobility.
- Independent EU-C: Main drivers and Strategy sections provide context on migration flows, but do not define statistical stocks/flows; UNSD remains the direct statistical basis.
- Boundary: distinguishes international migration from temporary mobility and stocks from flows. No individual immigration, asylum or residence status is determined.
