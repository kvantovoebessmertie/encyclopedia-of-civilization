## 2. Electrical safety — 3 Claims

Canonical Source: `SRC-ELECTRICAL_SAFETY_BASICS`, CDC/NIOSH, “Electrical Safety in the Workplace”, https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/electrical-safety/about/

### Claim A — `CLM-ELECTRICAL_SAFETY_BASICS-A`

Claim: electrical hazards include shock, burns, arc phenomena and fires.

Exact support:
- NIOSH/CDC section “Electrical hazards” explicitly lists electric shock and burns from contact with energized parts, injury from arcing, and fire from faulty electrical equipment or installations.
- The page's “Electrical injuries” section lists electrocution, electric shock, burns/arc blast and falls.

Proposed locator: NIOSH/CDC, “Electrical Safety in the Workplace”, “Electrical hazards” and “Electrical injuries”.

Assessment: direct support for all listed hazard types.

### Claim B — `CLM-ELECTRICAL_SAFETY_BASICS-B`

Claim: work on energized electrical parts must be done by qualified persons using appropriate procedures and protective equipment.

Exact support:
- Key points: only work on energized equipment or distribution systems if qualified and using appropriate PPE; understand approach distances; use proper energy-control procedures and verify de-energization.
- “Qualified vs. unqualified persons”: only qualified persons should do electrical work; qualified persons require training in skills, techniques, PPE and safety procedures.
- “Unqualified persons”: should not work on energized parts.

Proposed locator: same page, “Key points” and “Qualified vs. unqualified persons”.

Assessment: direct support. Preserve the distinction between work on energized parts and de-energized work after verification.

### Claim C — `CLM-ELECTRICAL_SAFETY_BASICS-C`

Claim: unqualified people should treat electrical systems as hazardous, not as objects for independent repair.

Exact support:
- “Qualified vs. unqualified persons” states that only qualified persons should do electrical work and that unqualified persons should not work on energized parts.
- The key points require qualification, PPE and proper energy-control procedures.

Proposed locator: same page, “Qualified vs. unqualified persons” and “Unqualified persons”.

Assessment: direct support for the safety boundary. The phrase “independent repair” is a broader paraphrase; keep it tied to the rule that unqualified persons should not perform electrical work.

Source-identity note: the full-inventory audit found two Source records for this exact NIOSH URL (`SRC-ELECTRICAL_SAFETY_BASICS` and `SRC_ELECTRICAL_SAFETY_NIOSH`). They are one underlying NIOSH page, not independent corroboration.

## 3. Emergency management — 3 Claims

Canonical Source: `SRC-EMERGENCY_MANAGEMENT_BASICS`, FEMA IS-230.E, “Fundamentals of Emergency Management”, https://training.fema.gov/programs/independent-study/courseoverview.aspx?code=IS-230.e&lang=en

### Claim A — `CLM-EMERGENCY_MANAGEMENT_BASICS-A`

Claim: emergency management includes preparedness, response, resource coordination and recovery.

Exact support:
- FEMA IS-230.E course objectives include explaining partners' roles, core capabilities supporting preparedness, development of comprehensive emergency operations plans, and planning/managing/coordinating resources for an effective response.
- UNDRR Terminology, “Disaster management”, defines disaster management as the organization, planning and application of measures preparing for, responding to and recovering from disasters.

Proposed locators: FEMA IS-230.E, “Course Objectives”; UNDRR, “Definition: Disaster management”, definition and annotation.

Assessment: direct support for preparedness, response, recovery and resource coordination.

### Claim B — `CLM-EMERGENCY_MANAGEMENT_BASICS-B`

Claim: effective emergency management requires roles to be distributed among public, private and community participants.

Exact support:
- FEMA IS-230.E objectives ask learners to explain how different partners contribute to emergency management in a community and describe each partner's roles.
- UNDRR Terminology, “Response”, states that response includes public and private sectors, community and volunteer participation, and specialized emergency services.

Proposed locators: FEMA IS-230.E, “Course Objectives”; UNDRR, “Definition: Response”, annotation paragraph on institutional elements.

Assessment: direct support for multi-partner coordination. The Claim's categories are broad but consistent with both sources.

### Claim C — `CLM-EMERGENCY_MANAGEMENT_BASICS-C`

Claim: specific powers, plans and procedures depend on jurisdiction, event type and current management systems.

Exact support:
- FEMA IS-230.E identifies principles and authorities as foundational and covers development of comprehensive emergency operations plans and partner roles.
- UNDRR definitions distinguish disaster management, preparedness and response, but do not establish the legal powers or detailed procedures of any specific jurisdiction.

Proposed locator: FEMA IS-230.E, “Course Objectives” and “Course Overview”; add the applicable national/state/local legal framework, incident-command doctrine or emergency operations plan standard for any jurisdiction-specific wording.

Assessment: partial support. The general dependency on authority and planning is supported, but the complete legal/jurisdictional proposition needs direct statutory or doctrine sources. Do not present FEMA's U.S. training course as universal law.

Source-independence note: Claim A has a second UNDRR Evidence Use; that is a separate international terminology source from FEMA. Preserve its own exact locator rather than treating the two descriptions as one.

## 4. Shelter — 3 Claims

Canonical Source: `SRC-SHELTER_BASICS`, Ready.gov homepage, https://www.ready.gov/
Additional linked Source for Claim A: `SRC_RED_CROSS_SHELTER_BASICS`, American Red Cross, Disaster Shelters, https://www.redcross.org/get-help/disaster-relief-and-recovery-services/find-an-open-shelter.html

### Claim A — `CLM-SHELTER_BASICS-A`

Claim: shelter is a basic need when people are exposed to environmental conditions and emergencies.

Exact support:
- FEMA, “Shelter-in-Place Guidance”, opening statement says that when disaster strikes, it may be safer to stay in one's home, workplace or another location and directs readers to identify where to go for hazards in their area.
- American Red Cross, “What services are offered at Red Cross Shelters?”, lists a safe place to sleep, meals, water and health services during disasters.

Proposed locators: FEMA “Shelter-in-Place Guidance”, opening paragraph; Red Cross, “What services are offered at Red Cross Shelters?”.

Assessment: direct support for shelter as a basic protection/service in disaster conditions. The Ready.gov homepage alone is not a sufficiently precise Source locator; the FEMA/Red Cross page-level sources should be preserved explicitly.

### Claim B — `CLM-SHELTER_BASICS-B`

Claim: temporary shelter selection/setup should consider weather exposure, water availability, ventilation and site safety.

Exact support:
- FEMA “Shelter-in-Place Guidance”, Chemical Hazard section: directs people to stay inside, reduce airflow by turning off furnaces/air conditioners/fans/heaters, seal doors/windows/vents, and use stored water rather than tap water.
- FEMA guide's Hurricane/High Wind and Flooding sections specify different safe locations according to the hazard, including interior/windowless rooms for high winds and higher levels for flooding.
- CDC, “What to Do to Shelter in Place for a Chemical Emergency”, Step 1 “Choose a safe room”, specifies a large room with a source of water and on the highest floor possible; it also describes sealing off outside air.
- CDC, “Guidelines for Staying Safe at a Disaster Shelter”, notes good airflow and hydration/safe drinking water in a mass shelter.

Proposed locators: FEMA “Shelter-in-Place Guidance”, Chemical Hazard and Hurricane/Flooding sections; CDC Chemical Emergency, Step 1; CDC Disaster Shelter, “Protect your health and avoid germs”.

Assessment: direct support for considering hazard, water, ventilation/airflow and safe location. This is hazard-specific guidance; it should not be compressed into a universal shelter-design checklist.

### Claim C — `CLM-SHELTER_BASICS-C`

Claim: emergency shelter suitability depends on the specific hazard and environmental conditions; no single shelter is safe for all scenarios.

Exact support:
- FEMA “Shelter-in-Place Guidance” presents different actions for chemical hazards, floods, hurricanes, tornadoes, earthquakes, nuclear detonation and winter storms. For example, high-wind sheltering uses a small interior windowless room on the lowest level, while flooding may require moving to the highest level; manufactured/mobile homes are unsafe for certain hurricane/tornado scenarios.
- Ready.gov “Are You Ready?” guide, “Make an Evacuation and Shelter Plan”, states that the decision to evacuate or shelter in place depends on the hazard and whether the shelter will keep people safe.

Proposed locators: FEMA “Shelter-in-Place Guidance”, hazard-specific sections; Ready.gov “Are You Ready?”, “Make an Evacuation and Shelter Plan”.

Assessment: direct support for the non-universal, hazard-dependent boundary.

Source-fit finding: the current Source record is only the Ready.gov homepage. It does not preserve the exact FEMA PDF/guide locator or the CDC guidance. Add the specific official publications as Source records/Evidence Uses under bounded editorial authorization; do not assume a homepage supports every detailed shelter instruction.
