## 1. Diplomacy — 2 Claims

Canonical Source: United Nations Treaty Collection, Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations.

### CLM-DIPLOMACY_BASICS-A
Claim: “The Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations was adopted in 1961 and entered into force in 1964.”

Exact support:
- The UN Treaty Collection records the Convention as adopted on 14 April 1961.
- It records the Convention as signed at Vienna on 18 April 1961 and entering into force on 24 April 1964 under Article 51.

Assessment: DIRECT support. Proposed locator: UN Treaty Collection, Chapter III, Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, treaty status record and Article 51.

### CLM-DIPLOMACY_BASICS-B
Claim: “The Convention establishes the international legal framework for diplomatic relations between States.”

Exact support:
- The Convention's preamble recognizes that diplomatic relations, privileges and immunities contribute to the development of friendly relations among nations, irrespective of their differing constitutional and social systems.
- Article 2 states that the establishment of diplomatic relations between States and of permanent diplomatic missions takes place by mutual consent.
- Article 3 identifies the functions of a diplomatic mission.

Assessment: DIRECT support. Proposed locator: Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations, Preamble and Articles 2–3. Preserve the State-to-State scope of the Convention.

## 2. Finance — 1 Claim

Canonical Source: Investor.gov, Investing Basics.

### CLM-FINANCE_BASICS-A
Claim: “Financial decisions are related to cash flows over time.”

Exact support checked:
- The canonical Investor.gov landing page introduces investing but does not provide a precise passage on the timing and valuation of cash flows.
- OpenStax Principles of Finance 2e §7.1 explains that time affects the value of money and that lump-sum payments/receipts are cash flows occurring at a point in time.
- §9.1 explains timing of multiple cash flows and how present and future values depend on when payments or receipts occur.

Assessment: PARTIAL for the current canonical source chain; DIRECT if OpenStax Principles of Finance 2e is added as a separate Source/Evidence Use. Proposed locators: OpenStax Principles of Finance 2e §7.1, “Now versus Later Concepts”, and §9.1, “Timing of Cash Flows”. The statement is foundational and does not by itself constitute investment advice.

## 6. Labour relations — 1 Claim

Canonical Source: International Labour Organization, Collective Bargaining and Labour Relations.

### CLM-LABOR_RELATIONS_BASICS-A
Claim: “Collective bargaining is one mechanism through which employers and trade unions agree on working conditions.”

Exact support:
- ILO Collective Bargaining Convention, 1981 (No. 154), Article 2 defines collective bargaining as negotiations between employers/employers' organizations and workers' organizations for determining working conditions and terms of employment and/or regulating relations between employers and workers.
- Article 3 allows national law or practice to determine how negotiations with workers' representatives are treated under the Convention.

Assessment: DIRECT support. Proposed locator: ILO Convention No. 154, Article 2. Preserve the boundary that legal recognition, coverage and procedures vary under national law and practice.

Assessment: DIRECT support. Proposed locator: OpenStax University Physics Volume 1 §3.3, “Average and Instantaneous Acceleration”.

## 9. Political science — 3 Claims

Canonical Source: OpenStax, Introduction to Political Science.

### CLM-POLITICAL_SCIENCE_BASICS-A
Claim: “Political science systematically studies politics, including the actions and interactions of individuals, groups, institutions and States.”

Exact support:
- OpenStax §1.3 describes political science as the systematic study of political processes and government decisions.
- It explicitly discusses how individuals and groups seek to influence government action and how governmental decisions affect individuals and groups.

Assessment: DIRECT support for the general disciplinary definition. Proposed locator: OpenStax Introduction to Political Science §1.3, “Political Science: The Systematic Study of Politics”.

### CLM-POLITICAL_SCIENCE_BASICS-B
Claim: “The empirical approach in political science uses observable data and verifiable evidence to study political phenomena.”

Exact support:
- OpenStax §1.3 describes the scientific method as systematically collecting data to test hypotheses and making the data available for challenge or verification.
- §1.5 states that empirical political science is based on what is, uses factual evidence and seeks to describe, explain and predict political phenomena.

Assessment: DIRECT support. Proposed locator: OpenStax §§1.3 and 1.5, “Scientific Method” and “Empirical Political Science”.

### CLM-POLITICAL_SCIENCE_BASICS-C
Claim: “Political analysis distinguishes descriptive questions about what happens from normative questions about what ought to happen.”

Exact support:
- OpenStax §1.5 explicitly contrasts empirical political science, based on what is, with normative political science, concerned with what should be.
- The Chapter 1 Key Terms defines normative political science as the systematic study of ideal goals, principles and behaviours in politics.

Assessment: DIRECT support. Proposed locator: OpenStax §1.5 and Chapter 1 Key Terms, “normative political science”.

## 11. Public finance — 2 Claims

Canonical Source: International Monetary Fund, Fiscal Policies.

### CLM-PUBLIC_FINANCE_BASICS-A
Claim: “Fiscal policy concerns public revenues, expenditures and management of public resources.”

Exact support:
- IMF describes fiscal policy as the use of government spending and taxation to influence the economy.
- It explains that governments influence resource use through taxes, spending and borrowing and that fiscal-policy priorities vary with country circumstances.

Assessment: DIRECT support. Proposed locator: IMF, “Fiscal Policy: Taking and Giving Away”, Overview and “How fiscal policy works”.

### CLM-PUBLIC_FINANCE_BASICS-C
Claim: “The tax system and public expenditure are interconnected elements of public financial management.”

Exact support:
- IMF describes fiscal policy as involving both taxation and government spending.
- It explains how taxes, transfers, spending and borrowing affect economic activity and how revenue and expenditure choices interact with fiscal objectives.

Assessment: DIRECT support. Proposed locator: IMF, “Fiscal Policy: Taking and Giving Away”, “How fiscal policy works”. Preserve the scope: this is an introductory macro-fiscal description, not a complete public financial management framework.
