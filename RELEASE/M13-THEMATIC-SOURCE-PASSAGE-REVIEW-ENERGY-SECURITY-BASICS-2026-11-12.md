## Claim-by-claim findings

### A — reliable and affordable access plus resilience to supply disruption

Claim: “Энергетическая безопасность включает надежный и доступный доступ к энергии и способность систем противостоять нарушениям поставок.”

**Direct source evidence located:**

- IEA Energy Security topic page, heading “Reliable, affordable access to all fuels and energy sources” and opening description of avoiding, mitigating and managing energy disruptions and crises. The same page says the IEA's scope includes oil, natural gas, electricity and clean-energy supply chains.
- IEA Glossary, “Energy security”: “The uninterrupted availability of energy sources at an affordable price.”
- IEA (2026), ASEAN Energy Security Review, “Energy security context, framework and overview”: defines energy security as uninterrupted availability at affordable prices while maintaining flexibility and resilience to manage shocks and longer-term stresses.

**Assessment:** The substance is well supported. However, the existing linked Source is the IEA topic landing page; it does not itself preserve the exact glossary definition or the analytical definition in the ASEAN review. The resilience clause is also broader than the short topic-page header. Add distinct, accurately identified Source records or explicitly extend the bounded source set, then version the Evidence Use with exact locators. Do not silently repurpose the current Source identity.

**Recommended locator targets:** IEA Energy Security topic page, opening section; IEA Glossary entry “Energy security”; IEA (2026) ASEAN Energy Security Review, section “Assessment framework and time horizon,” especially the definition and system-resilience dimensions.

**Disposition:** HOLD until the exact source set and pinpoints are versioned.

### B — geopolitical, cyber, supply-chain and extreme-weather risks

Claim: “Риски энергетической безопасности могут возникать из геополитических факторов, киберугроз, нарушений цепочек поставок и экстремальных погодных явлений.”

**Direct source evidence located:**

- IEA Energy Security topic page explicitly enumerates geopolitical risks, cyberattacks on energy infrastructure, supply-chain disruptions and extreme-weather events in its opening description.
- The same page's electricity-security section discusses cybersecurity threats, climate impacts on infrastructure, supply chains, and resilience against cyberattacks and extreme weather.
- European Commission DG Energy, “In focus: EU energy security explained” (20 April 2026), sections “What are the threats to our energy security?” and “Securing our energy imports,” separately describes geopolitical disruptions, cyberattacks, physical threats, critical-raw-material supply and extreme weather.

**Assessment:** The four listed categories are directly supported by the IEA page. The Commission article provides a second institutional source with useful concrete examples, but it is explicitly about EU energy security and should not be represented as globally exhaustive. The claim uses “могут возникать,” which correctly avoids claiming that the list is exhaustive or that each risk occurs in every system.

**Recommended locator targets:** IEA Energy Security page, opening paragraph (risk list) and “Electricity security”; European Commission DG Energy article, “What are the threats to our energy security?” (EU-bounded corroboration).

**Disposition:** HOLD pending versioned pinpoint in the existing Evidence Use; the source-fit itself is strong.

### C — assessment depends on carrier, infrastructure, time horizon and regional context

Claim: “Оценка энергетической безопасности зависит от конкретного энергоносителя, инфраструктуры, горизонта времени и регионального контекста.”

**Direct source evidence located:**

- IEA (2026), ASEAN Energy Security Review, “Energy security context, framework and overview”:
  - Says energy security must be assessed across fuels, technologies and systems, and that not every dimension is equally relevant for every fuel or technology.
  - Identifies fuel availability, import dependency, infrastructure adequacy and emergency preparedness as central traditional risks.
  - Explains that regional indicators can mask differences between member states because resource endowments, infrastructure and fuel-trade patterns vary.
  - States that its assessment focuses primarily on a five-year horizon, with longer-term transition objectives also considered.
  - Distinguishes the tailored application of the framework across oil, gas, coal, electricity, critical minerals and energy-efficiency chapters.
- The European Commission article provides an EU example of interdependence between electricity and gas systems and of policy measures tied to EU institutional and geographic context.

**Assessment:** This is a defensible methodological claim. The IEA review directly supports the listed dimensions, but it is a regional ASEAN assessment and a five-year analytical example, not proof that every energy-security assessment everywhere must use exactly that horizon. Preserve the Claim's context-dependent wording; do not turn the five-year example into a universal prescription. The current linked IEA topic page is not enough to substantiate this methodological statement by itself.

**Recommended locator target:** IEA (2026) ASEAN Energy Security Review, section “Assessment framework and time horizon,” including the framework dimensions, five-year horizon, and country-level differences.

**Disposition:** HOLD pending adding the distinct IEA analytical Source and a versioned Evidence Use pinpoint. Consider a wording note that the five-year horizon is an example of an assessment horizon, not a universal fixed requirement.
