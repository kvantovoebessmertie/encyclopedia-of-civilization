## 2. Claim-level passage review

### Claim A — human dignity, human rights and prevention of harm

Claim: “UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence frames AI ethics around human dignity, human rights and the prevention of harm.”

**Passage support**
- Preamble and paragraph 5(c): protection, promotion and respect for human rights, fundamental freedoms and human dignity throughout the AI system life cycle.
- Paragraphs 13–16: dignity and human rights are foundational and must be respected, protected and promoted throughout the life cycle.
- Paragraph 25, “Proportionality and Do No Harm”: requires context-appropriate procedures for risk assessment and measures to prevent harm.

**Assessment: DIRECT.** The Recommendation expressly contains all three elements. The Claim is appropriately attributed to the Recommendation and does not imply that the document is itself a binding treaty.

**Proposed locator:** Recommendation, Preamble; §II, paragraph 5(c); §III.1, paragraphs 13–16; §III.2, paragraph 25.

### Claim B — transparency, fairness, sustainability and human oversight

Claim: “The Recommendation identifies transparency, fairness, sustainability and human oversight among principles relevant to responsible AI governance.”

**Passage support**
- Paragraph 29: AI actors should make reasonable efforts to minimize and avoid reinforcing discriminatory or biased applications and outcomes, to ensure fairness.
- Paragraph 31: identifies sustainability as a continuing assessment across human, social, cultural, economic and environmental dimensions.
- Paragraphs 35–36: human oversight, attribution of responsibility and the principle that AI systems cannot replace ultimate human responsibility and accountability.
- Paragraphs 37–41: transparency and explainability, including context-appropriate levels and links to accountability.

**Assessment: DIRECT.** All four elements are explicitly addressed. Editorial precision: the Recommendation presents a set of values and principles, and “fairness” appears in the text's treatment of bias and discrimination; do not imply these four are the only principles.

**Proposed locator:** Recommendation, §III.2, paragraphs 29, 31, 35–41.

### Claim C — contextual, risk and life-cycle assessment

Claim: “Ethical assessment of AI systems should consider context, risks and impacts across the AI system life cycle rather than treating technical performance as sufficient on its own.”

**Passage support**
- Paragraph 4: the Recommendation provides a basis for ethical impact assessment throughout the AI system life cycle.
- Paragraph 11: calls for contextual assessment where values and principles may be in tension.
- Paragraphs 25–26: require context-appropriate proportionality and risk assessment to prevent harm; method choice should be appropriate to context and based on rigorous scientific foundations.
- Paragraph 31: calls for continuous assessment of human, social, cultural, economic and environmental impacts.
- Paragraph 131: describes UNESCO's work on an Ethical Impact Assessment methodology grounded in scientific research and international human-rights law.

**Assessment: PARTIAL / QUALIFY THE INFERENCE.** Context, risk and life-cycle impact assessment are directly supported. The contrast with “technical performance alone” is a reasonable synthesis of the Recommendation's socio-technical and impact-based framework, but that exact contrast is not stated in the cited paragraphs. Keep it as an explicitly identified inference, or revise the Claim to state only that ethical assessment considers context, risks and impacts throughout the life cycle.

**Proposed locator:** Recommendation, paragraphs 4, 11, 25–26, 31 and 131. If retaining the “technical performance alone” contrast, record it as an inference rather than a direct quotation or direct source proposition.

## 3. Scope, authority and rights boundaries

- The Recommendation was adopted by UNESCO's General Conference on 23 November 2021. It is a normative international instrument and policy framework; do not describe it as a treaty or as directly enforceable domestic law.
- Its addressees and implementation mechanisms include Member States and AI actors; actual legal obligations depend on applicable international and domestic law and subsequent national/regional instruments.
- The Recommendation is broad and cross-sectoral. It does not replace sector-specific safety, privacy, equality, consumer-protection, procurement or professional requirements.
- The official UNESCO page is publicly accessible. Public accessibility does not by itself establish an open licence or permission for unrestricted reproduction, redistribution or machine-learning ingestion. This review paraphrases rather than reproduces the text; rights/attribution clearance remains a separate gate.
