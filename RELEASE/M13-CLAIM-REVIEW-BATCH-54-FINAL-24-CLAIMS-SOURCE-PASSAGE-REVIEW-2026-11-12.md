## 2. Source-passage findings

### SI units and measurement accuracy — 1 Claim

The BIPM SI Brochure (9th edition) defines the SI units and explains their definitions and realization. The International Vocabulary of Metrology (VIM) distinguishes measurement accuracy, measurement uncertainty and metrological traceability as separate concepts.

- CLM-SI-ACCURACY-BOUNDARY correctly distinguishes the unit definition from the accuracy/uncertainty of a particular measurement and from practical realization.
- The linked Source record points to the SI Brochure, which is direct for unit definitions and realization but not a complete source for the uncertainty/accuracy distinction. Add the exact VIM definitions (VIM 2.13 accuracy, 2.26 measurement uncertainty, and 2.41 metrological traceability) as an additional source under bounded authorization.
- The Claim is a useful boundary statement; it should not conflate accuracy with uncertainty.

### Social policy and social statistics — 3 Claims

OECD's Social Policy and Social Spending pages state that social spending includes cash benefits, in-kind goods/services and tax breaks with a social purpose; examples include support for poverty, unemployment, older people, healthcare, family assistance and housing. OECD SOCX describes cross-country social expenditure data and notes that programme structures and spending vary by country.

UN Statistics Division's principles for international statistical activities state that concepts, definitions, classifications, sources, methods and procedures should meet professional standards, be transparent and be documented; the Division's functions include data collection, processing/dissemination and standardization of methods, classifications and definitions.

- CLM-SOCIAL_POLICY_BASICS-B is directly supported by OECD's list of cash assistance and services.
- CLM-SOCIAL_POLICY_BASICS-C is supported by OECD's emphasis on country variation in programmes, accessibility, affordability and expenditure; retain country- and period-specific scope.
- CLM-SOCIAL_STATISTICS_BASICS-B is directly supported by UNSD's emphasis on standard definitions, classifications, methods and documentation for comparability.
- The canonical Source/EU records lack exact page/indicator/paragraph locators.

### Software testing — 3 Claims

NASA's Software Assurance and Software Safety page describes software assurance, verification/validation and testing levels/approaches, including white-box, black-box, model-based, functional, stress and regression testing. ISTQB Certified Tester Foundation Level Syllabus v4.0.1, §1.3, states that testing shows the presence, not absence, of defects and cannot prove test-object correctness even when no defects are found. It also states exhaustive testing is impossible and describes coverage limits.

- CLM-SOFTWARE_TESTING_BASICS-A is supported by NASA's description of testing to identify objective evidence and assess software quality.
- CLM-SOFTWARE_TESTING_BASICS-B is directly supported by NASA's list of testing levels, approaches and test types.
- CLM-SOFTWARE_TESTING_BASICS-C is directly supported by ISTQB §1.3 and its discussion of defect detection, exhaustive testing and coverage limitations.
- The canonical EU/source record points to a NASA overview and does not contain the exact ISTQB locator. Safety-critical software requires assurance, hazard analysis and independent verification beyond a generic test suite.

### Solar system — 1 Claim

NASA Solar System Facts lists the Sun, eight planets, five officially named dwarf planets, hundreds of moons and thousands of asteroids and comets.

- CLM-SOLAR_SYSTEM_BASICS-A is directly supported.
- NASA's current taxonomy is the appropriate source; keep the distinction between planets, dwarf planets, satellites and small bodies.

### State formation — 2 Claims

James C. Scott's Against the Grain: A Deep History of the Earliest States (2017) challenges a simple universal narrative in which agriculture automatically produces settled communities and states. It examines multiple historical conditions, including sedentism, cereal agriculture, livestock, population concentration, control of resources and coercive institutions. The Yale University Press description and scholarly review emphasize that archaeological evidence complicates the standard narrative.

- CLM-STATE_FORMATION-A is supported as a caution against a single universal sequence. One interpretive work is not a complete comparative survey of state formation across regions.
- CLM-STATE_FORMATION-B is supported as a list of factors compared in research, but the exact causal relations among agriculture, settlement, resource management, population and institutions remain contested and region-specific.
- Preserve the pluralist framing; do not turn Scott's interpretation into a universal causal model.

### Temperature — 2 Claims

NIST states that a temperature interval of one kelvin equals one degree Celsius exactly and that 0 K is absolute zero. NIST SP 330, §2, defines the kelvin as the SI unit of thermodynamic temperature and the Celsius temperature as T−273.15 K.

- CLM-TEMPERATURE_BASICS-B is directly supported.
- CLM-TEMPERATURE_BASICS-C is directly supported.
- The claims are basic metrology facts; avoid using the classical shorthand “all motion stops” as a literal description of quantum systems at absolute zero.

### Textile fibre processing — 2 Claims

The Metropolitan Museum of Art's “The Secret Life of Textiles: Plant Fibers” explains that fibre type and quality determine textile properties and that processing stages differ by fibre. It describes flax retting, scutching, repeated combing and spinning; hemp retting, scutching, combing and preparation for spinning; and ramie processing, splitting, splicing and twisting.

- CLM-TEXTILE_FIBRE_PROCESSING_BASICS-B is directly supported: preparation and combing operations depend on fibre type and properties.
- CLM-TEXTILE_FIBRE_PROCESSING_BASICS-C is directly supported: splicing/twisting and spinning are distinct operations and affect resulting thread/fabric quality.
- The Met source covers selected plant fibres and historical craft processes, not every industrial textile fibre or modern processing line.

### UTC(NIST) — 1 Claim

NIST's Time Realization page states that UTC(NIST) is NIST's representation of UTC and serves as the official time and frequency standard for the United States. It is formed from a group of atomic clocks whose outputs are combined using weighted averages and calibrated against primary standards.

- CLM-UTC-NIST is directly supported.
- The Claim correctly preserves U.S. scope. Exact realization details should be linked to the NIST page's “Time and Frequency Realization” section.

### Ancient trade networks — 2 Claims

The Metropolitan Museum's Old Assyrian Period essay describes long-distance trade organized by Assyrian merchants, communities established across central Anatolia and a corpus of roughly 24,000 cuneiform tablets recording trade. Museum objects include caravan accounts and letters documenting shipments, loans, business disputes, goods and communications between trading settlements.

- CLM-TRADE_NETWORKS-B is supported: evidence includes traded objects/materials, archaeological finds, written records and provenance/context.
- CLM-TRADE_NETWORKS-C is supported by evidence that merchants exchanged goods and information, used shared record-keeping practices and adopted aspects of local Anatolian life.
- The source is a specific ancient Assyrian/Anatolian case study. Do not generalize it to all ancient trade networks without regional evidence.

### Urban economics and urbanization — 4 Claims

OECD research on cities describes agglomeration benefits through sharing, matching and learning, while also identifying congestion, housing costs, pollution and inequality as costs. It notes that local governance arrangements and infrastructure influence the balance of benefits and costs.

UN DESA World Urbanization Prospects defines urbanization as an increasing share of people living in urban areas, through rural-to-urban migration and transformation of rural areas into urban areas. UN-Habitat's Urban Planning page describes integrated spatial strategies and planning that consider social, economic and environmental progress, local needs, services, regulatory frameworks and multilevel governance.

- CLM-URBAN_ECONOMICS_BASICS-B is directly supported by OECD's treatment of agglomeration benefits and costs.
- CLM-URBAN_ECONOMICS_BASICS-C is supported by OECD's emphasis on local geography, infrastructure, institutions and governance; preserve local/time-specific context.
- CLM-URBANIZATION-A is supported by UN DESA's definition. The Claim's wording should be clarified so that it does not conflate urban population growth with urbanization as a change in population share.
- CLM-URBANIZATION-C is directly supported by UN-Habitat's integrated planning approach.

### Writing systems — 3 Claims

The Metropolitan Museum's “The Origins of Writing” describes early record-keeping contexts and the development of pictographic/proto-cuneiform signs. The Oxford ETCSL introduction to cuneiform states that cuneiform was invented in Uruk in southern Mesopotamia around 3400–3300 BCE, initially to record goods and services in Sumerian, and was later adopted to write Akkadian and Hittite. UCL's Digital Egypt resource also describes cuneiform as a script used for several languages.

- CLM-WRITING_SYSTEMS-A is supported by evidence of distinct historical contexts and multiple early writing traditions; avoid presenting a single universal trajectory.
- CLM-WRITING_SYSTEMS-B is directly supported by the Uruk date and the early administrative/accounting use of cuneiform.
- CLM-WRITING_SYSTEMS-C is directly supported: cuneiform is a script, not a language, and it was used to write multiple languages.
- Preserve the distinction between a writing system/script and the languages represented by it.
