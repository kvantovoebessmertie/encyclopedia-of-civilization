## 5. education-science-basics — 9 records

Claims:
- A: education research studies learning processes and student outcomes.
- B: educational research uses quantitative and qualitative methods to investigate questions about learning.
- C: transferring a research result to another educational context requires checking the comparability of conditions.

Current Source: Institute of Education Sciences (IES)  
https://ies.ed.gov/

Findings:
- Claims A/B/C are substantive and appropriately bounded at an introductory level.
- The current IES homepage is an institutional landing page, not a direct source for learning-process outcomes, mixed methods or transferability/generalizability.
- All three EUs use generic descriptions and lack a specific IES report, research-method section or passage.
- Claim C needs careful qualification: comparability may involve population, intervention, implementation, setting, measures and outcome definitions; avoid implying that a single universal checklist guarantees transferability.
- Context and Scope target Claim A only.

Decision: **HOLD — good candidate, insufficient source precision.** Link direct IES evidence or methods guidance for each Claim and add precise locators; retain the external-validity/transferability boundary.

## 7. demographic-methods-basics — 9 records

Claims:
- A: demography studies population size, structure and dynamics.
- B: fertility, mortality and migration are principal components of population change.
- C: demographic indicators require specification of period, population and calculation method.

Current Source: UN DESA Population Division  
https://www.un.org/development/desa/pd/

Findings:
- The UN Population Division directly describes work on population size and structure, population dynamics, fertility, mortality and international migration. This provides strong conceptual support for Claims A/B.
- Claim C is methodologically sound, but the current landing page does not provide the precise indicator metadata/denominator/period/method definitions required to substantiate it.
- All three EUs are generic and lack exact report, glossary or data-method locators.
- Context and Scope target Claim A only.
- Demographic estimates/projections are time-sensitive; the edition must preserve reference period, population universe and source vintage where numerical examples are later added.

Decision: **HOLD — strong source-family fit, incomplete methodological pinpoints.** Add precise UN DESA definitions and methods references for each Claim; preserve the period/population/method boundary.

## 8. history-methods-basics — 9 records

Claims:
- A: historical research uses sources to reconstruct and interpret the past.
- B: a source has provenance and context that affect its use as evidence.
- C: temporal sequence alone does not prove a causal relationship.

Current Source: American Historical Association, Teaching & Learning  
https://www.historians.org/teaching-learning/

Additional historical-source guidance checked:
- Historical Association, “Using Historical Sources”: https://www.history.org.uk/student/categories/93/resource/3211/using-historical-sources
- Harvard, “A Historian’s Use of Evidence”: https://history.fas.harvard.edu/resource/historypdfpdf

Findings:
- Claim A is directly supportable by historical-method guidance that historical writing depends on sources and uses primary/secondary evidence to answer questions.
- Claim B is directly supported by source-analysis guidance that asks who created a source, when/where it was created, its audience, purpose, bias and context.
- Claim C is a sound methodological caution, but the current AHA landing page does not directly support the distinction between temporal succession and causation. It needs a direct historical-method or general causal-inference locator.
- The current Source is a broad AHA landing page rather than a pinpoint source for any of the three Claims.
- All EUs are generic and lack specific source-analysis sections.
- Context and Scope target Claim A only.

Decision: **HOLD — substantive Claims, but evidence chain is too broad.** Replace or supplement the landing-page source with exact methodological resources, including a direct locator for the causation boundary. Do not treat chronology as proof of causality.
