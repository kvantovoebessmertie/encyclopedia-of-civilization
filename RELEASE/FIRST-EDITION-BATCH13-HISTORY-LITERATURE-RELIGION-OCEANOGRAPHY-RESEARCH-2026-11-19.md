Sources:
- Harvard Pluralism Project, “Rivers of Faith”: https://pluralism.org/rivers-of-faith
- Religious Studies Project, “What is the Study of Religion/s? Self-Presentations of the Discipline on University Web Pages”: https://www.religiousstudiesproject.com/response/what-is-the-study-of-religionsself-presentations-of-the-discipline-on-university-web-pages/

The Harvard essay directly states that traditions interact historically, contain internal complexity and change over time. It is a U.S.-focused pluralism project, so do not turn its U.S. examples into a universal description of every tradition or region.

The Religious Studies Project article analyzes 101 university programme webpages across 70 universities and 15 countries. It identifies non-confessional, outsider and non-normative approaches, while also noting that academic definitions of the field are not perfectly uniform. Claim A/C are supported as a description of one widely used academic approach, not as a rule that all scholarship is identical.

Recommended source graph:
- Version the current Harvard Source identity/URI to “Rivers of Faith” for Claim B.
- Add a separate Religious Studies Project Source/EU for Claims A/C. Keep the source's scope and methodology explicit; do not present a content analysis of university webpages as a universal legal or philosophical rule.

Sources:
- NOAA Ocean Service, “What does an oceanographer do?”: https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/oceanographer.html
- NOAA Ocean Exploration, “What causes ocean currents?”: https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/ocean-fact/currents/
- NOAA Ocean Exploration, “CTD”: https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/technology/ctd/

The current NOAA homepage is too broad to support the three placeholder Claims. Correct the existing Source identity/URI to the NOAA Ocean Service oceanography overview for Claim A, and add separate Source/Evidence Use chains for currents (B) and CTD instrumentation (C). New canonical IDs must be allocated through the normal ID process; none are invented here.

**Technical boundaries**
- Claim B should not imply that all currents have the same cause. NOAA identifies multiple mechanisms: wind, density differences, gravity, tides, topography and episodic events.
- Claim C should distinguish conductivity from salinity: conductivity is measured; salinity is derived using conductivity, temperature and pressure. Depth is derived from pressure.
- Do not infer the biological or chemical composition of the whole water column from one CTD cast.

NOAA factual pages are appropriate for the proposed Claims. Do not reuse NOAA images, videos or third-party media without item-level rights checks.
