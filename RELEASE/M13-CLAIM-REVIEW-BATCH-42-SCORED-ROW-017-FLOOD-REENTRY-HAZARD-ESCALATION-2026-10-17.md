## Current record linkage

The Claim and Evidence Use point to the U.S. EPA page **Flooded Home**:

https://www.epa.gov/emergencies-iaq/flooded-home

The canonical Evidence Use description says EPA describes electrical, gas, and structural hazards as reasons not to return until safety is confirmed, but it contains no passage-level locator. The linked Flooded Home page is an interactive tour; its accessible HTML states that it teaches safe re-entry and cleanup, but the detailed interactive content is not exposed in the page text captured during this review.

## Passage-level evidence checked

A related official EPA page, **Emergencies and Indoor Air Quality**, has a flooding-specific section and says that a household should wait to re-enter until professionals confirm the home is safe from structural, electrical, or other hazards (https://www.epa.gov/emergencies-iaq/emergencies-and-indoor-air-quality-iaq, section “Hurricanes and Flooding”). It also advises evacuating and calling authorities from a safe location when a gas leak is suspected, and professional evaluation when structural damage is seen (section “Earthquakes”). These passages corroborate the Claim's overall hazard-escalation principle, though the gas/structural examples on that page's latter section are discussed in an earthquake context, not specifically a flood.

The EPA page **Coming Home** separately advises that a visibly unstable building should not be entered until a qualified professional verifies it is safe, and that entry should not occur until electricity is confirmed off:
https://www.epa.gov/flooded-homes/coming-home

These are relevant authoritative corroborating passages. However, they are not pinpointed in the current Evidence Use, and the full detail behind the canonical Flooded Home interactive source was not independently captured. This distinction matters because the reviewed Claim is safety-relevant.
