# M11 Evidence and Source-Boundary Audit — 2026-10-09

## Method

Review each existing Claim against its linked Evidence Use and recorded canonical Source. Independence is assessed per Claim, not by counting Source records. This is a desk-based source-fit audit, not a formal systematic review.

## Accessibility

- Source: UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.
- Claim A is supported at introductory level by the Convention's purpose of promoting equal enjoyment of rights and full participation.
- Claim B is supported by Article 9's identification and elimination of accessibility barriers and its coverage of physical environments, transport, information/communications and public facilities/services.
- Claim C needs the explicit international-framework/local-requirements distinction recorded in the substantive audit. The Convention calls for States Parties to develop and monitor minimum standards; it does not itself certify a particular local design.
- The three current Evidence Use descriptions are generic and must identify these distinct contributions and the source's limits.
- No second Source is required for these introductory claims by count alone.

Reference within the same Convention: https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities/article-9-%20accessibility.html

## Ergonomics

- Source: CDC/NIOSH, “About Ergonomics and Work-Related Musculoskeletal Disorders,” https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/ergonomics/about/index.html
- The locator directly defines ergonomics as fitting work tasks/demands to worker capabilities, describes risk factors including force, repetition, awkward postures and vibration, and defines an ergonomics program around identifying, analyzing and controlling risk factors.
- Claim C must be narrowed to that directly described process; do not imply that this one overview supplies a complete evaluation protocol or a site-specific risk assessment.
- The current three Evidence Use descriptions are generic and should be rewritten to identify their distinct support and boundaries.
- No second Source is required by count alone.

## Human factors

- Source record: NASA — Human Factors, https://www.nasa.gov/reference/human-factors/
- The three Claims remain at a general educational level and should not be converted into universal engineering prescriptions. The current Evidence Use descriptions do not distinguish the support for system interaction, human capabilities/limitations, and whole-system design.
- The exact recorded locator could not be independently retrieved by the current external fetch. Related official NASA material describes human-system integration and human capabilities/limitations, but it is not substituted for the recorded Source and does not authorize changing the Source identity/URL in this scope. Treat direct-locator verification as a stated limitation, not as a confirmed broken link.
- No additional Source is authorized in this pass. If the substantive re-audit establishes that a Claim cannot be supported by the recorded source even after accurate narrowing, stop and request a scope amendment before adding/replacing a Source.

## Conclusion

The confirmed evidence-use debt is explanatory and boundary-specific, not a lack of raw Source count. Rewrite the existing Evidence Use descriptions so each says what its linked source supports and what it does not establish. Preserve the existing source identities/URLs, record IDs, provenance, claim/source refs and `supports` roles.
