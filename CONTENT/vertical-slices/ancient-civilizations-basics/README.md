## Additional scholarly evidence — CC BY 4.0 candidate route

The following six article-level publications were selected for bounded factual use. Their publisher/repository landing pages are recorded in the corresponding Source records. Article-level CC BY 4.0 does not automatically clear third-party figures, maps, tables, photographs or datasets; none of those materials is reproduced here. The local corpus stores bibliographic identity, external locator and independently written evidence-use descriptions, not copied article text.

### Claim A — early urbanism and social differentiation

- McMahon et al. (2023), *Dense urbanism and economic multi-centrism at third-millennium BC Lagash*, Antiquity 97(393):596–615. Pinpoints: Abstract; Introduction; Results and Discussion; Conclusion. Bounded Lagash case; not a universal template.
- Lamb (2022), *Rural social differentiation in Early Classic Chunhuayum, Yucatan, Mexico*, Ancient Mesoamerica 33(1):162–185. Pinpoints: Abstract; household social differentiation; identifying difference; Discussion; Conclusions. One rural Maya community and period; supports the social-differentiation component only, not a universal or permanent class structure.

### Claim B — agriculture, settlement and demographic variation

- Lesure et al. (2021), *Large-Scale Patterns in the Agricultural Demographic Transition of Mesoamerica and Southwestern North America*, American Antiquity 86(3):593–612. Pinpoints: Abstract; Spatial Structure; Temporal Structure; macroregional sections; Conclusions. Regional synthesis, not a global sequence.
- Crema et al. (2024), *Regional variations in the demographic response to the arrival of rice farming in prehistoric Japan*, Antiquity 98(401):1290–1305. Pinpoints: Abstract; Introduction; Results; Discussion; Conclusions. Radiocarbon-date frequency and settlement evidence are proxies, not census counts.
- Neil et al. (2016), *Isotopic evidence for residential mobility of farming communities during the transition to agriculture in Britain*, Royal Society Open Science 3(1):150522. Pinpoints: Abstract; Introduction; Results and Discussion. One Early Neolithic burial population; does not establish a universal mobility pattern.

### Claim C — archaeological and written evidence

- Clark, Altaweel & Gordin (2022), *Urbanscape, Land Use Change and Centralization in the Region of Uruk, Southern Mesopotamia from the 2nd to 1st Millennium BCE*, Land 11(11):1955. Pinpoints: Abstract; Textual Description & Sources; Materials and Methods; Results; Discussion; Conclusions. Regional method case combining published cuneiform-derived place-name data and archaeological survey; does not imply that all ancient societies had writing.
